\section{The prospective suite, arm by arm}
\label{app:prospective_arms}

\textbf{(i) FLTrust as $d_1$ (7 pairs): out of scope, 2/7.} The five predicted-HIGH misses are fltrust$\to$\{rep, nc, krum, mkrum, tm\}. Their C2/C3 failures are real (FLTrust does destroy Reputation's consensus-distance signal), and are costless because $d_2$ was never load-bearing: the C0 mechanism of §\ref{sec:prospective}.

\textbf{(ii) FLTrust as $d_2$ (6 pairs): in scope, 6/6, and the near-identity of §\ref{sec:prospective} is visible per seed rather than only in the aggregate.} Krum$\to$FLTrust and MKrum$\to$FLTrust are \textbf{bit-identical} to FLTrust alone on all six runs ($0.0519/0.0459/0.0453$ scaling, $0.0492/0.0466/0.0426$ pixel), as expected for a pass-through $d_1$. NC$\to$FLTrust is \emph{bit-identical on the pixel attack}, where no client exceeds $\tau{=}5$ so $c_i \equiv 1$, and differs only within seed noise on model-scaling, where clipping actually fires. Rep$\to$FLTrust and RFA$\to$FLTrust likewise differ only within noise. The single genuine deviation is FG$\to$FLTrust ($0.064$, per-seed up to $0.084$): the one $d_1$ that produces exact \emph{zeros} rather than positive rescalings, changing FLTrust's effective client set instead of only its magnitudes, which is the Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} interaction. Every $d_1$ in the menu is a non-negative per-client rescaling whose positive part FLTrust cancels exactly, so only the \emph{zero pattern} is free to matter, which is where the one deviation appears.

\textbf{(iii) Neither defense is FLTrust (7 pairs): 5/7.} Per-pair values are in Table~\ref{tab:prospective}; both misses are diagnosed in §\ref{sec:prospective} and neither is a mechanism error.

\section{Metric-swap ablation: verification and per-cell detail}
\label{app:metric_swap_detail}

\textbf{The mechanism is verified in code before it is claimed.} An outcome alone would leave open whether the mirrors are invariant at all, so we check the statistic itself rather than only the ASR (\texttt{experiments/verify\_cos\_invariance.py}, \texttt{results/cos\_invariance\_check.json}), comparing each $d_2$'s internal state on $\{u_i\}$ against $\{c_iu_i\}$ for the $c_i$ that NormClip and RFA actually produce on live rounds. Over 18 rounds (3 seeds $\times$ 2 upstream defenses $\times$ 3 rounds), \texttt{cos\_reputation}'s weight vector agrees to $\le 2.5\times10^{-7}$ and its weight \emph{ordering} never changes ($0/18$), and \texttt{cos\_krum} selects the same client in $18/18$. The Euclidean originals do not: Reputation's weights move by up to $0.222$ and its ordering changes in $12/18$ rounds; Krum's selection changes in $6/18$. The size of the disturbance tracks the realized weight ratio $\rho$, as Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} predicts: under NormClip ($\rho \in [1.00,4.90]$) Reputation's ordering changes in $3/9$ rounds and Krum's selection in $0/9$; under RFA ($\rho \in [2.11,17.38]$) they change in $9/9$ and $6/9$. On 1000 synthetic draws with larger rescalings the separation is cleaner: ordering changes in $0/1000$ trials for \texttt{cos\_reputation} against $992/1000$ for Reputation, and $0/1000$ against $618/1000$ for the two Krums. The mirrors are additionally checked against the shipped implementations they stand in for (selected index identical, aggregate relative error ${\approx}10^{-8}$, below the ${\approx}7\times10^{-5}$ MPS-versus-CPU nondeterminism of the same computation), so the invariance is a property of the statistic and not of a divergent reimplementation.

One prediction in the suite was genuinely two-sided, and it is the round's only prospective positive result. \texttt{cos\_krum}'s C1 verdict on model-scaling turns entirely on whether the accuracy gate is applied: $0.078$ passes the numeric threshold, $0.146$ accuracy fails the gate. Rather than choose a reading after the fact we froze \emph{both}, with a resolution rule fixed in advance. Primary (accuracy-gated) $\Rightarrow$ C1 fails $\Rightarrow$ HIGH; secondary (numeric C1 only) $\Rightarrow$ the pair is certified $\Rightarrow$ LOW; and if the outcome had been LOW at accuracy $\ge 0.35$, the gate would have been wrong and our two primary labels for those cells would have been recorded as misses. Both pairs came out \textbf{HIGH} (NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.508$, RFA$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.566$), refuting the secondary reading and vindicating the accuracy gate against a pre-registered alternative that would have overturned it. This is a small result but a real one: the gate is usually defended by argument, and here it beat a competing specification of itself on data neither of us had seen. We flag the weaker half honestly: NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum}'s $0.508$ sits barely above threshold on per-seed values $0.084 / 0.676 / 0.766$, so that cell is threshold-noise even though its label is correct, and the RFA arm at $0.566$ (per-seed $1.000 / 0.121 / 0.576$) is what carries the conclusion.

The pattern holds across the suite once cells without power are excluded. $\Delta$ is only observable where the standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack, since a baseline already above $0.5$ has no suppression left to lose and will show $\Delta \approx 0$ whatever the transform did: a ceiling effect, not preservation. Restricting to standalone ASR $< 0.5$ at accuracy $\ge 0.35$ leaves six cells, and measured disturbance splits them perfectly at both seed counts. At the frozen $n{=}3$: undisturbed ($0/9$) $\Delta \in [-0.027, +0.001]$, disturbed ($>0/9$) $\Delta \in [+0.630, +0.865]$, a separation of $+0.629$. At $n{=}5$ after the post-hoc top-up: $[+0.001, +0.190]$ versus $[+0.718, +0.878]$, a separation of $+0.527$. Neither has any overlap, but the two extra seeds move both halves toward each other. Two of the three undisturbed cells are \texttt{cos\_krum} on the pixel backdoor, where the mirror suppresses standalone ($0.300$ at $0.598$ accuracy) and carries that suppression through under \emph{both} upstreams: at $n{=}3$ intact ($0.300$, $\Delta = +0.000$ under NormClip; $0.273$, $\Delta = -0.027$ under RFA), at $n{=}5$ degraded but still under threshold ($0.490$, $\Delta = +0.190$; $0.447$, $\Delta = +0.147$; both at $0.61$ accuracy, on new per-seed values $0.864 / 0.686$ and $0.849 / 0.565$). So the confirming half of the split is contributed by the invariant construction behaving as C2 predicts (which is the one thing the metric-swap arm does deliver, even though C1's failure on model-scaling denied it a frozen LOW label) but only weakly at $n{=}5$: exact invariance of the \emph{selection} leaves the magnitude channel (Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation}) free to move $\Delta$, and two seeds are enough to see it move. That is a limitation of this post-hoc contrast and a direct motivation for arm 3 of the ladder.

\textbf{Excluded cells.} All four \texttt{cos\_*} model-scaling cells are excluded as uninterpretable (hollow standalone baseline), and the six Reputation / \texttt{cos\_reputation} / Krum pixel cells for lack of power (standalone $\ge 0.5$).

\section{Dose--response design: the full pre-registered protocol}
\label{app:dose_protocol}
\label{app:details}

Four properties make this a dial on the theory rather than an arbitrary perturbation. (i) It is exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}'s form $T(u_i)=c_iu_i$ with $c_i>0$, so every rung lies inside the theory's scope. (ii) \textbf{The dose is $\rho$}: $\max_j c_j/\min_j c_j = e^{2\kappa}$ exactly, the same upstream weight ratio Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} and both mechanism-preserving regimes are stated in, verified by reading $\rho$ back off the \emph{transformed update norms} on live rounds, where realized $\rho$ matches nominal to three decimals at every rung and $\mathrm{mean}(c)$ stays in $[0.99999, 1.00002]$. (iii) $\kappa{=}0$ gives $c \equiv 1$: the implementation short-circuits, so the identity rung is bit-identical to $d_2$ standalone by construction rather than by rounding. (iv) Normalizing the \emph{mean} to 1 holds the aggregate's scale fixed, separating the quantity under test (disturbance of $d_2$'s \emph{statistic}, i.e.\ C2) from the magnitude-contraction channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} that costs accuracy; without it the ladder would confound C2 with attenuation. The permutation is keyed on $(\text{seed},\text{round})$ and \textbf{not} on client identity: assigning by id would place the adversaries at a fixed end of the ladder and systematically attenuate them, manufacturing a success, whereas this keying gives every arm at a given seed and round the \emph{identical} coefficient vector, so the arms differ in $d_2$ and nothing else. \textbf{$\texttt{dose\_kappa}\langle\kappa\rangle$ is an instrument, not a defense}, and is never evaluated or recommended as one.

\textbf{Frozen grid and arms.} $\kappa \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, i.e.\ $\rho \in \{1.00, 2.72, 7.39, 54.60\}$: four rungs spanning a ${\approx}55\times$ ratio. Arms are selected by the paper's own power rule (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}), fixed before the freeze: the ladder is informative only where standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack (ASR $<0.5$ at clean accuracy $\ge 0.35$), since a baseline already above threshold has no suppression left to lose and would give a flat curve by ceiling effect. Applying that rule to the measured baselines leaves exactly four $(d_2, \text{attack})$ cells, and they happen to cover three invariance classes of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}). The $\kappa{=}0$ rung is run \emph{fresh} at all five seeds for every arm rather than reused from the published baselines, so every rung of every curve comes from one code path at one seed set; the published baselines then serve as an independent per-seed cross-check of that rung (tolerance $10^{-6}$) instead of as data in it.

\textbf{The abscissa is measured, not nominal.} What matters to C2 is not $\rho$ but what the rescaling does to the specific statistic $d_2$ reads, so we measure that separately and \emph{before} freezing any prediction, pushing live FL updates through the shipped transform and recording each arm's decision at every $\kappa$ on the \emph{same} raw updates (\texttt{measure\_dose\_disturbance.py}, \texttt{results/dose\_disturbance.json}; 5 seeds $\times$ 3 rounds per attack). No ASR is computed there, which is why it can legitimately precede the freeze. It establishes three things before any outcome exists (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}): \emph{there is a dose}: disturbance is exactly $0$ at the identity rung and rises monotonically with $\kappa$ in every non-invariant arm; \emph{the class-(a) invariance claim holds in the shipped code}: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is unchanged in $\mathbf{0}$ of $120$ cells at $\rho$ up to $54.6$, its cosine \emph{scores} moving by at most $1.1\times10^{-4}$ relative, the float32 floor for a ${\approx}1.1$M-dimensional vector, which we record rather than assert on; and \emph{class (b) is graded rather than binary}, at roughly half the class-(c) disturbance ($0.487$ vs.\ $0.867$/$0.933$ at $\kappa{=}2.0$), which is what arm 4's prediction turns on.

\textbf{Frozen predictions.} The \emph{primary} test is pooled: across all 16 $(\text{arm},\kappa)$ cells, the rise in ASR over that arm's own $\kappa{=}0$ rung is predicted to be positively rank-correlated with the measured disturbance rate (Spearman, one-sided, $\alpha = 0.05$). This is the dose--response claim the design exists for, and its predictor is a \emph{measured property of the statistic}, not the identity of the defense and not its standalone effectiveness, both held fixed within every arm. The \emph{secondary} predictions are the per-arm shapes of Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}, tested by a one-sided Jonckheere--Terpstra trend test across the four ordered rungs at $n{=}5$. For arm 3, ``flat'' is scored as an \emph{equivalence} claim rather than a failed rejection: confirmed if $|\overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}2.0) - \overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}0)| < 0.15$ \emph{and} every rung stays below $0.5$. That margin is fixed and justified in advance: it is under a quarter of the $+0.629$ within-defense gap for Krum on record at the freeze (the post-hoc top-up later put that gap at $+0.716$ at $n{=}5$, which only makes the margin more conservative; it is not revised), and $0.300 + 0.15 = 0.450 < 0.5$, so ``within the margin'' and ``still suppressing'' coincide by design for this arm. Arm 4's class-(b) signature is the \emph{shallower slope}, so its criterion includes its $\kappa{=}2.0$ rise being smaller than both class-(c) arms'.

\section{Dose--response caveats recorded before the freeze}
\label{app:dose_caveats}

\textbf{Caveats recorded in advance.} (i) Arms 1--2 are on model-scaling and arms 3--4 on the pixel backdoor. This is not a free choice: on model-scaling \emph{no} invariant $d_2$ suppresses at usable accuracy, that is the finding of §\ref{sec:metric_swap} (\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.078$ at $0.146$ accuracy), which is also why \texttt{cos\_krum}/scaling is not an arm. ``Same attack'' is required \emph{within} each ladder, which is where the causal claim lives; across arms we compare curve \emph{shapes and slopes}, never levels. (ii) Arm 4 has thin headroom: \texttt{coord\_median}/pixel sits only $0.057$ below threshold, so the binary above/below-$0.5$ reading is weak evidence either way and we report the continuous curve with intervals. (iii) The dose is uncorrelated with adversary status by construction, but it is not adversary-\emph{adversarial}: a dose assignment that deliberately up-weights adversaries would be a strictly harder test and is not run here. (iv) A synthetic upstream transform is not a real defense, so we test ecological validity by overlaying the two \emph{real} upstream transforms already measured on these same cells (NormClip and RFA, §\ref{sec:metric_swap}) as anchor points at their own measured disturbance; if they fall far off the synthetic curve at matched disturbance, that is a finding about the instrument and is reported as one. (v) Disturbance is measured on FedAvg trajectories on one shared set of raw updates per round, which is what makes it a clean abscissa, but means the rates in Table~\ref{tab:dose_design} are not re-measured along each arm's own 50-round trajectory.

\section{Decomposing the emitted displacement: re-selection against rescaling}
\label{app:decomposition}

\noindent The $\Delta$~agg.\ column of Table~\ref{tab:channels_full} mixes two things the causal claim of §\ref{app:targeted_full} needs apart. For a selector, $\mathrm{agg}(U) = u_{s_0}$ and $\mathrm{agg}(T(U)) = c_{s_1} u_{s_1}$, with $s_0$ the client selected on the raw stack and $s_1$ the client selected on the transformed one, so exactly
\[ c_{s_1}u_{s_1} - u_{s_0} \;=\; \underbrace{(u_{s_1} - u_{s_0})}_{\text{re-selection}} \;+\; \underbrace{(c_{s_1}-1)\,u_{s_1}}_{\text{rescaling}}, \]
both components reported relative to $\|u_{s_0}\|$. The re-selection component is what a magnitude-controlled dose would still move (it takes both updates \emph{untransformed}) while the rescaling component is what such a control closes. They are \textbf{not orthogonal}: the measured angle runs from $\cos{=}-0.99$ to $-0.43$, mean ${\approx}-0.65$, so their norms need not sum to the total, and the table prints the angle rather than leaving that discrepancy unexplained. What is asserted instead is the exact vector identity above: worst residual $2.66\mathrm{e}{-}07$ over all $240$ (arm, family, rung, seed, round) cells, float32 noise. \textbf{This decomposes the published number, not a second similar one}: every recomputed total agrees with the corresponding $\Delta$~agg.\ value in \texttt{results/admission\_measurement.json} to $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$, on the same $5$ seeds $\times$ $3$ live rounds, the same rungs and the same configuration as that measurement. \textbf{Selectors only}: a weighted averager and a coordinate-wise order statistic emit no single selected client, so ``the selected update'' and hence this split are undefined for them, and \texttt{coord\_median} and \texttt{reputation} do not appear. \texttt{experiments/measure\_displacement\_decomposition.py} trains no model per rung, computes no ASR, writes a new file (\texttt{results/displacement\_decomposition.json}) and leaves every frozen artifact byte-identical.

\section{Adversary-correlated dispersion $\Delta_c$, measured}

\label{sec:rho_vs_deltac}

\section{Anticipated objections}
\label{app:objections}

Five objections we expect, answered against text that is already in the paper rather than by new
argument. Nothing here is a new claim; each answer is a pointer plus the scope it turns on.

\paragraph{1. This is small-scale federated learning: five clients, one architecture, mostly one
dataset.} Agreed, and every empirical magnitude is scoped accordingly, per arm, never pooled across
arms, and enumerated in Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels} with the scale and architecture evidence in
Appendix~\ref{app:scale_external}. What does \emph{not} depend on scale is the
identification argument: any criterion whose eligibility gate is defined on the same outcome it predicts
conditions on a descendant of both mechanism and effectiveness
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}), and that holds at any client count, model size or sample size.
The boundary results forbid an inference; they do not measure a system.

\paragraph{2. The interventions are oracles: no deployed system can pin an adversary's coefficient or
read adversary identity.} Correct, and that is why Mode~S is an \textbf{instrument, not a screen}. Mode~S
measures level~(P4) only by reading adversary identity, which no deployable defense can, so these
experiments \textbf{identify the causal channel and are not a (P4) screen}. Pinning $c{=}1.0$ has one
purpose, to cut the confounded path of Figure~\ref{fig:modeS}(a) by construction, exactly as an
oracle-labelled control isolates a channel in an ablation. The one control that \emph{removes} an oracle
component (score-only, which scores the transformed stack but aggregates the untransformed selected
update) does not change the verdict (§\ref{app:targeted_full}, Appendix~\ref{app:decomposition}).

\paragraph{3. $n{=}5$ seeds is small.} It is: $n{=}5$ per arm, $n{=}8$ for \texttt{cos\_krum}, with
Student-$t$ $95\%$ intervals throughout and no claim that a small effect is zero. Where suppression does
not move we report \textbf{no evidence of a practically meaningful change under the pre-specified
$\pm0.15$ equivalence margin}, a margin frozen before the runs (Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}), never a
claim that suppression is unchanged. Two design choices do more for precision than seeds would: the
identity rungs are imported \textbf{bit-identical per seed} (largest deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$),
so the contrast is within-seed rather than across noise, and the central claim is a \textbf{sign
reversal} ($-0.272$ against $+0.098$ on the identical cell, Table~\ref{tab:comparability}), which is not
a null result. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} says the cell that would validate the
criterion is \emph{empty by design}: more seeds cannot repair it (Table~\ref{tab:can_cannot}).

\paragraph{4. If ASR does not move, why does a change of decision matter?} Because the change of
decision is what a preservation criterion \emph{measures}, and the gap between the two is the finding.
An invariance argument certifies the top three levels of Definition~\ref{def:levels_full} while the security
content sits at level~(P4), the adversarial contribution admitted. A criterion reading the Krum arm would
call that composition broken (the decision flips in up to $80\%$ of rounds) for a composition whose
suppression shows no measurable change: a false alarm in the direction that costs coverage
(Proposition~\ref{prop:emergent_full}, recall bound $1/5$). The converse fails too: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s
selection is bit-identical at every rung yet it never suppresses ($0.584$ at the identity) and its ASR
\emph{falls} $0.425$ across the ladder, so exact invariance of the statistic neither implies suppression
nor holds it fixed (§\ref{app:targeted_full}, Appendix~\ref{app:dose_detail}).

\paragraph{5. The theory covers only positive per-client rescalings.} Yes, and we say so wherever it is
used (Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}, Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight},
§\ref{sec:restricted_theorem}). The class is not arbitrary (it is what norm clipping, contribution
weighting and reputation weighting do to a client's update) but it excludes coordinate-wise filters and
any transform that changes an update's direction, and the theorem's own margin closes under heterogeneity
($m{=}3.24 \to 1.01$ at $\alpha{=}0.1$), so \textbf{it certifies nothing where heterogeneity is high}.
The negative does not rest on the theory: it is a \emph{measured} counterexample, and a narrower theory
makes the counterexample sharper rather than weaker, since \textbf{the theorem certifies mechanism
preservation, not security}; it bounds no ASR.
