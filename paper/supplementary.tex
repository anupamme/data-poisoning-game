\section{The prospective suite, arm by arm}
\label{app:prospective_arms}

\textbf{(i) FLTrust as $d_1$ (7 pairs): out of scope, 2/7.} The five predicted-HIGH misses are fltrust$\to$\{rep, nc, krum, mkrum, tm\}. Their C2/C3 failures are real (FLTrust does destroy Reputation's consensus-distance signal), and are costless because $d_2$ was never load-bearing: the C0 mechanism of §\ref{sec:prospective}.

\textbf{(ii) FLTrust as $d_2$ (6 pairs): in scope, 6/6, and the near-identity of §\ref{sec:prospective} is visible per seed rather than only in the aggregate.} Krum$\to$FLTrust and MKrum$\to$FLTrust are \textbf{bit-identical} to FLTrust alone on all six runs ($0.0519/0.0459/0.0453$ scaling, $0.0492/0.0466/0.0426$ pixel), as expected for a pass-through $d_1$. NC$\to$FLTrust is \emph{bit-identical on the pixel attack}, where no client exceeds $\tau{=}5$ so $c_i \equiv 1$, and differs only within seed noise on model-scaling, where clipping actually fires. Rep$\to$FLTrust and RFA$\to$FLTrust likewise differ only within noise. The single genuine deviation is FG$\to$FLTrust ($0.064$, per-seed up to $0.084$): the one $d_1$ that produces exact \emph{zeros} rather than positive rescalings, changing FLTrust's effective client set instead of only its magnitudes, which is the Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} interaction. Every $d_1$ in the menu is a non-negative per-client rescaling whose positive part FLTrust cancels exactly, so only the \emph{zero pattern} is free to matter, which is where the one deviation appears.

\textbf{(iii) Neither defense is FLTrust (7 pairs): 5/7.} Per-pair values are in Table~\ref{tab:prospective}; both misses are diagnosed in §\ref{sec:prospective} and neither is a mechanism error. This arm is the one §\ref{sec:prospective} calls the only one that could have surprised us, and §\ref{app:mkrum} reports that $5$ of its $7$ pairs reduce to a single defense, leaving $2$ that compose two real mechanisms.

\section{MultiKrum at $K{=}5$, and the matched contrasts we withdrew}
\label{app:mkrum}

\textbf{The mechanism.} \texttt{multi\_krum} scores each participant by its summed distance to nearest neighbours, keeps the $k$ lowest and averages them, with $k$ defaulting to $5$. Every experiment in this project runs \texttt{clients\_per\_round}${}={}5$ and none passes $k$, so the kept set is \emph{all five} participants and the call reduces to plain averaging. Measured rather than argued (\texttt{experiments/verify\_wave3\_invariance.py}, test D; \texttt{experiments/analyze\_mkrum\_degeneracy.py}): at $K{=}5$ the largest absolute difference between \texttt{multi\_krum} and \texttt{fedavg} on identical inputs is $1.9\times10^{-6}$, which is float summation order alone; at $K{=}10$ it is $25.2$, a genuine selector again. MultiKrum is therefore a distinct rule in this codebase only when more clients participate than it selects, which no run reported here does.

\textbf{Two degeneracies compose, and we had recognized only one.} \emph{(A) Pass-through $d_1$.} A rank aggregator emits one vector rather than per-client updates, so \texttt{krum}, \texttt{multi\_krum}, \texttt{trimmed\_mean} and \texttt{coord\_median} supply no upstream transform and $d_1{\to}d_2$ measures $d_2$ alone. This was known and is what the table's DEGEN label records; it makes Krum$\to$FLTrust and MKrum$\to$FLTrust, and Krum$\to$RFA and MKrum$\to$RFA, the same measurement twice (per-seed $|\Delta\mathrm{ASR}| = 0.000$ on both attacks). \emph{(B) MultiKrum as $d_2$.} Because MultiKrum is FedAvg here, $d_1{\to}$MKrum applies $d_1$'s per-client transform and then averages, which for every weighting or clipping $d_1$ in this menu \emph{is} that defense standalone. This is the new fact, and the affected rows carry frozen labels of C1-fail or C2-fail rather than DEGEN because it was not known when the labels were committed. Counting both, $8$ of the suite's $20$ rows reduce to a single defense, not the $5$ marked DEGEN: the five already marked, plus RFA$\to$MKrum, NC$\to$MKrum and FLTrust$\to$MKrum.

\textbf{The matched contrasts do not survive, and substituting Krum is what shows it.} §\ref{sec:prospective} previously drew three contrasts holding $d_1$ fixed while $d_2$ moved from FLTrust to a Euclidean rule, reading the gap as evidence for Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}. Two used MultiKrum and so had no downstream defense on one side. Replacing it with the genuine selector Krum gives a repaired-looking $12.6$--$19.1\times$ gap, and the repair is illusory: for the gap to be about invariance, C1 would have to hold across it, and it does not. Every Krum arm takes its max-committed ASR from the pixel backdoor, on which Krum's own standalone ASR is $0.583$, above threshold.

The NormClip row is the sharpest form of the point: NC$\to$Krum is bit-identical to Krum alone on all three seeds ($0.779 / 0.039 / 0.932$, accuracy $0.396 / 0.592 / 0.467$), because under the pixel backdoor the maximum client update norm over $45$ client-rounds is $3.04 < \tau{=}5$, so NormClip is the exact identity in $9/9$ measured rounds (test F; under model-scaling norms reach $24.5$ and it binds in $11/45$). Nothing about the invariance class of $d_2$ is being tested there. In $0$ of the $3$ contrasts does the non-invariant $d_2$ suppress its own max-committed attack standalone, which is the condition that would make the contrast informative. The Krum arms for NC and RFA come from the metric-swap suite (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}) rather than the prospective suite, at the identical protocol and the same seeds $42$--$44$; we note the change of source rather than smooth it over.

\textbf{What arm (iii) is left with.} Of its $7$ pairs, Krum$\to$CM is CoordMedian alone, Krum$\to$RFA and MKrum$\to$RFA and RFA$\to$MKrum are RFA alone, and NC$\to$MKrum is NormClip alone. Two compose two real mechanisms: FG$\to$Krum, which is a miss at $0.078$ and $9.9\%$ clean accuracy (the collapsed-model artifact), and Rep$\to$Krum, which is a hit at $0.880$. So the arm's $5/7$ is $1/2$ on the pairs that put two mechanisms in series, and the surviving hit is a predicted HIGH, so it still bears on specificity only.

\textbf{A noise floor this degeneracy hands us, and a second use for the accuracy gate.} $d_1{\to}$MKrum and $d_1{\to}$FedAvg are the same function computed through two code paths that differ by $1.9\times10^{-6}$ per aggregation, as are MultiKrum and FedAvg standalone. Over $50$ rounds that difference is amplified chaotically through the training trajectory, so whatever such a cell pair disagrees by is a measured floor on how small an ASR difference can be called real at this protocol. At matched seeds, across the five such cells with clean accuracy above the $0.35$ gate, the per-seed $|\Delta\mathrm{ASR}|$ reaches $0.046$; that is what licenses the paper's repeated ``differ only within seed noise''. In the one cell below the gate, MultiKrum against FedAvg under model-scaling at $0.10$ accuracy, the same function disagrees with itself by $0.270$ (per-seed ASR $0.000/0.995/0.730$ against $0.000/1.000/1.000$). So the accuracy gate is a precondition for reading small ASR differences at all, not only for reading suppression claims: on a collapsed model ASR is not a stable quantity. Neither figure is to be confused with the exactly $0.000$ of degeneracy (A), where the two rows traverse an identical code path.

\textbf{What we did not change.} The suite's frozen categories, predictions and $13/20$ score stand exactly as committed at \texttt{262cf35}; relabelling them after seeing the data is what the pre-registration forbids. What is retracted is the body's interpretation of three rows and of the three contrasts, and Table~\ref{tab:prospective} carries a marker and a footnote rather than an edited label.

\section{Metric-swap ablation: verification and per-cell detail}
\label{app:metric_swap_detail}

\textbf{The mechanism is verified in code before it is claimed.} An outcome alone would leave open whether the mirrors are invariant at all, so we check the statistic itself rather than only the ASR (\texttt{experiments/verify\_cos\_invariance.py}, \texttt{results/cos\_invariance\_check.json}), comparing each $d_2$'s internal state on $\{u_i\}$ against $\{c_iu_i\}$ for the $c_i$ that NormClip and RFA actually produce on live rounds. Over 18 rounds (3 seeds $\times$ 2 upstream defenses $\times$ 3 rounds), \texttt{cos\_reputation}'s weight vector agrees to $\le 2.5\times10^{-7}$ and its weight \emph{ordering} never changes ($0/18$), and \texttt{cos\_krum} selects the same client in $18/18$. The Euclidean originals do not: Reputation's weights move by up to $0.222$ and its ordering changes in $12/18$ rounds; Krum's selection changes in $6/18$. The size of the disturbance tracks the realized weight ratio $\rho$, as Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} predicts: under NormClip ($\rho \in [1.00,4.90]$) Reputation's ordering changes in $3/9$ rounds and Krum's selection in $0/9$; under RFA ($\rho \in [2.11,17.38]$) they change in $9/9$ and $6/9$. On 1000 synthetic draws with larger rescalings the separation is cleaner: ordering changes in $0/1000$ trials for \texttt{cos\_reputation} against $992/1000$ for Reputation, and $0/1000$ against $618/1000$ for the two Krums. The mirrors are additionally checked against the shipped implementations they stand in for (selected index identical, aggregate relative error ${\approx}10^{-8}$, below the ${\approx}7\times10^{-5}$ MPS-versus-CPU nondeterminism of the same computation), so the invariance is a property of the statistic and not of a divergent reimplementation.

One prediction in the suite was genuinely two-sided, and it is the only prospective positive result either screening suite produced. \texttt{cos\_krum}'s C1 verdict on model-scaling turns entirely on whether the accuracy gate is applied: $0.078$ passes the numeric threshold, $0.146$ accuracy fails the gate. Rather than choose a reading after the fact we froze \emph{both}, with a resolution rule fixed in advance. Primary (accuracy-gated) $\Rightarrow$ C1 fails $\Rightarrow$ HIGH; secondary (numeric C1 only) $\Rightarrow$ the pair is certified $\Rightarrow$ LOW; and if the outcome had been LOW at accuracy $\ge 0.35$, the gate would have been wrong and our two primary labels for those cells would have been recorded as misses. Both pairs came out \textbf{HIGH} (NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.508$, RFA$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.566$), refuting the secondary reading and vindicating the accuracy gate against a pre-registered alternative that would have overturned it. This is a small result but a real one: the gate is usually defended by argument, and here it beat a competing specification of itself on data neither of us had seen. We flag the weaker half honestly: NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum}'s $0.508$ sits barely above threshold on per-seed values $0.084 / 0.676 / 0.766$, so that cell is threshold-noise even though its label is correct, and the RFA arm at $0.566$ (per-seed $1.000 / 0.121 / 0.576$) is what carries the conclusion.

The pattern holds across the suite once cells without power are excluded. $\Delta$ is only observable where the standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack, since a baseline already above $0.5$ has no suppression left to lose and will show $\Delta \approx 0$ whatever the transform did: a ceiling effect, not preservation. Restricting to standalone ASR $< 0.5$ at accuracy $\ge 0.35$ leaves six cells, and measured disturbance splits them perfectly at both seed counts. At the frozen $n{=}3$: undisturbed ($0/9$) $\Delta \in [-0.027, +0.001]$, disturbed ($>0/9$) $\Delta \in [+0.630, +0.865]$, a separation of $+0.629$. At $n{=}5$ after the post-hoc top-up, with each standalone recomputed at the same five seeds from the dose ladder's $\kappa{=}0$ rung (\texttt{results/dose\_response/summary.json}, that $d_2$ alone under the identical configuration, bit-identical to the published standalone baselines on every seed they share, largest deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$): $[-0.044, +0.001]$ versus $[+0.717, +0.878]$, a separation of $+0.716$. Neither has any overlap, and the extra seeds widen the split rather than narrow it. At both seed counts the two extremes are the two Krum cells, which share a standalone, so the width of the split is algebraically the Krum contrast's own gap and not independent evidence beyond it; the other four cells extend the halves outward, not the distance between them. Two of the three undisturbed cells are \texttt{cos\_krum} on the pixel backdoor, where the mirror suppresses standalone ($0.300$ at $0.598$ accuracy on the frozen three seeds, $0.490$ at $0.607$ on all five) and carries that suppression through under \emph{both} upstreams: at $n{=}3$ intact ($0.300$, $\Delta = +0.000$ under NormClip; $0.273$, $\Delta = -0.027$ under RFA), at $n{=}5$ still intact ($0.490$, $\Delta = +0.000$; $0.447$, $\Delta = -0.044$; both at $0.61$ accuracy, on new per-seed values $0.864 / 0.686$ and $0.849 / 0.565$ against a baseline that moves to $0.490$ on those same seeds). The NormClip arm is intact \emph{exactly}: NormClip never binds on this attack ($0/9$ rounds; max client norm $3.04$ against $\tau = 5$), so its composition is the same five runs as the \texttt{cos\_krum} baseline, bit-identical per seed, and $\Delta = +0.000$ is an identity rather than a measurement. Had the frozen three-seed baseline been kept under the five-seed compositions, the same two rows would have read $\Delta = +0.190$ and $+0.147$, and the natural reading of that, the magnitude channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} moving $\Delta$ where the selection is exactly invariant, would have credited an effect to a transform that provably did nothing: the movement is entirely the baseline's seed set, which is why the subtrahend has to move with the composition. So the confirming half of the split is contributed by the invariant construction behaving as C2 predicts (which is the one thing the metric-swap arm does deliver, even though C1's failure on model-scaling denied it a frozen LOW label), and this contrast has no power on the magnitude channel in either direction: its only exactly-invariant downstream sits under an upstream that never rescales. That is a limitation of this post-hoc contrast and a direct motivation for arm 3 of the ladder, which moves that dial on purpose.

\textbf{Excluded cells.} All four \texttt{cos\_*} model-scaling cells are excluded as uninterpretable (hollow standalone baseline), and the six Reputation / \texttt{cos\_reputation} / Krum pixel cells for lack of power (standalone $\ge 0.5$).

\textbf{Selection-bias note.} All 6 LOW triples in Table~\ref{tab:three_way} use FoolsGold or reputation as $d_1$: the same successful weighting defenses from the 2-way analysis. \section{Dose--response design: the full pre-registered protocol}
\label{app:dose_protocol}
\label{app:details}

Four properties make this a dial on the theory rather than an arbitrary perturbation. (i) It is exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}'s form $T(u_i)=c_iu_i$ with $c_i>0$, so every rung lies inside the theory's scope. (ii) \textbf{The dose is $\rho$}: $\max_j c_j/\min_j c_j = e^{2\kappa}$ exactly, the same upstream weight ratio Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} and both mechanism-preserving regimes are stated in, verified by reading $\rho$ back off the \emph{transformed update norms} on live rounds, where realized $\rho$ matches nominal to three decimals at every rung and $\mathrm{mean}(c)$ stays in $[0.99999, 1.00002]$. (iii) $\kappa{=}0$ gives $c \equiv 1$: the implementation short-circuits, so the identity rung is bit-identical to $d_2$ standalone by construction rather than by rounding. (iv) Normalizing the \emph{mean} to 1 holds the aggregate's scale fixed, separating the quantity under test (disturbance of $d_2$'s \emph{statistic}, i.e.\ C2) from the magnitude-contraction channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} that costs accuracy; without it the ladder would confound C2 with attenuation. The permutation is keyed on $(\text{seed},\text{round})$ and \textbf{not} on client identity: assigning by id would place the adversaries at a fixed end of the ladder and systematically attenuate them, manufacturing a success, whereas this keying gives every arm at a given seed and round the \emph{identical} coefficient vector, so the arms differ in $d_2$ and nothing else. \textbf{$\texttt{dose\_kappa}\langle\kappa\rangle$ is an instrument, not a defense}, and is never evaluated or recommended as one.

\textbf{Frozen grid and arms.} $\kappa \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, i.e.\ $\rho \in \{1.00, 2.72, 7.39, 54.60\}$: four rungs spanning a ${\approx}55\times$ ratio. Arms are selected by the paper's own power rule (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}), fixed before the freeze: the ladder is informative only where standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack (ASR $<0.5$ at clean accuracy $\ge 0.35$), since a baseline already above threshold has no suppression left to lose and would give a flat curve by ceiling effect. Applying that rule to the measured baselines leaves exactly four $(d_2, \text{attack})$ cells, and they happen to cover three invariance classes of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}). The $\kappa{=}0$ rung is run \emph{fresh} at all five seeds for every arm rather than reused from the published baselines, so every rung of every curve comes from one code path at one seed set; the published baselines then serve as an independent per-seed cross-check of that rung (tolerance $10^{-6}$) instead of as data in it.

\textbf{The abscissa is measured, not nominal.} What matters to C2 is not $\rho$ but what the rescaling does to the specific statistic $d_2$ reads, so we measure that separately and \emph{before} freezing any prediction, pushing live FL updates through the shipped transform and recording each arm's decision at every $\kappa$ on the \emph{same} raw updates (\texttt{measure\_dose\_disturbance.py}, \texttt{results/dose\_disturbance.json}; 5 seeds $\times$ 3 rounds per attack). No ASR is computed there, which is why it can legitimately precede the freeze. It establishes three things before any outcome exists (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}): \emph{there is a dose}: disturbance is exactly $0$ at the identity rung and rises monotonically with $\kappa$ in every non-invariant arm; \emph{the class-(a) invariance claim holds in the shipped code}: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is unchanged in $\mathbf{0}$ of $120$ cells at $\rho$ up to $54.6$, its cosine \emph{scores} moving by at most $1.1\times10^{-4}$ relative, the float32 floor for a ${\approx}1.1$M-dimensional vector, which we record rather than assert on; and \emph{class (b) is graded rather than binary}, at roughly half the class-(c) disturbance ($0.487$ vs.\ $0.867$/$0.933$ at $\kappa{=}2.0$), which is what arm 4's prediction turns on.

\textbf{Frozen predictions.} The \emph{primary} test is pooled: across all 16 $(\text{arm},\kappa)$ cells, the rise in ASR over that arm's own $\kappa{=}0$ rung is predicted to be positively rank-correlated with the measured disturbance rate (Spearman, one-sided, $\alpha = 0.05$). This is the dose--response claim the design exists for, and its predictor is a \emph{measured property of the statistic}, not the identity of the defense and not its standalone effectiveness, both held fixed within every arm. The \emph{secondary} predictions are the per-arm shapes of Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}, tested by a one-sided Jonckheere--Terpstra trend test across the four ordered rungs at $n{=}5$. For arm 3, ``flat'' is scored as an \emph{equivalence} claim rather than a failed rejection: confirmed if $|\overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}2.0) - \overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}0)| < 0.15$ \emph{and} every rung stays below $0.5$. That margin is fixed and justified in advance: it is under a quarter of the $+0.629$ within-defense gap for Krum on record at the freeze (the post-hoc top-up later put that gap at $+0.716$ at $n{=}5$, which only makes the margin more conservative; it is not revised), and $0.300 + 0.15 = 0.450 < 0.5$, so ``within the margin'' and ``still suppressing'' coincide by design for this arm. Arm 4's class-(b) signature is the \emph{shallower slope}, so its criterion includes its $\kappa{=}2.0$ rise being smaller than both class-(c) arms'.

\section{Dose--response caveats recorded before the freeze}
\label{app:dose_caveats}

\textbf{Caveats recorded in advance.} (i) Arms 1--2 are on model-scaling and arms 3--4 on the pixel backdoor. This is not a free choice: on model-scaling \emph{no} invariant $d_2$ suppresses at usable accuracy, that is the finding of §\ref{sec:metric_swap} (\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.078$ at $0.146$ accuracy), which is also why \texttt{cos\_krum}/scaling is not an arm. ``Same attack'' is required \emph{within} each ladder, which is where the causal claim lives; across arms we compare curve \emph{shapes and slopes}, never levels. (ii) Arm 4 has thin headroom: \texttt{coord\_median}/pixel sits only $0.057$ below threshold, so the binary above/below-$0.5$ reading is weak evidence either way and we report the continuous curve with intervals. (iii) The dose is uncorrelated with adversary status by construction, but it is not adversary-\emph{adversarial}: a dose assignment that deliberately up-weights adversaries would be a strictly harder test and is not run here. (iv) A synthetic upstream transform is not a real defense, so we test ecological validity by overlaying the two \emph{real} upstream transforms already measured on these same cells (NormClip and RFA, §\ref{sec:metric_swap}) as anchor points at their own measured disturbance; if they fall far off the synthetic curve at matched disturbance, that is a finding about the instrument and is reported as one. (v) Disturbance is measured on FedAvg trajectories on one shared set of raw updates per round, which is what makes it a clean abscissa, but means the rates in Table~\ref{tab:dose_design} are not re-measured along each arm's own 50-round trajectory.

\section{Anticipated objections}
\label{app:objections}

Five objections we expect, answered against text that is already in the paper rather than by new
argument. Nothing here is a new claim; each answer is a pointer plus the scope it turns on.

\paragraph{1. This is small-scale federated learning: five clients, one architecture, mostly one
dataset.} Agreed, and every empirical magnitude is scoped accordingly, per arm, never pooled across
arms, and enumerated in Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels} with the scale and architecture evidence in
§\ref{app:scale_external}. What does \emph{not} depend on scale is the
identification argument: any criterion whose eligibility gate is defined on the same outcome it predicts
conditions on a descendant of both mechanism and effectiveness
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}), and that holds at any client count, model size or sample size.
The boundary results forbid an inference; they do not measure a system.

\paragraph{2. The interventions are oracles: no deployed system can pin an adversary's coefficient or
read adversary identity.} Correct, and that is why Mode~S is an \textbf{instrument, not a screen}. Mode~S
measures level~(P4) only by reading adversary identity, which no deployable defense can, so these
experiments \textbf{identify the intervention's effect at a fixed adversarial coefficient share and are
not a (P4) screen}. Pinning $c{=}1.0$ has one purpose, to cut the confounded path of
Figure~\ref{fig:modeS}(a) by construction, exactly as an oracle-labelled control closes one path in an
ablation: one path, not every path, the magnitude of the emitted aggregate still moving with the dose. The one control that \emph{removes} an oracle
component (score-only, which scores the transformed stack but aggregates the untransformed selected
update) does not change the verdict (§\ref{app:targeted_full}, Appendix~\ref{app:decomposition}).

\paragraph{3. $n{=}5$ seeds is small.} It is: $n{=}5$ per arm, $n{=}8$ for \texttt{cos\_krum}, with
Student-$t$ $95\%$ intervals throughout and no claim that a small effect is zero. Where suppression does
not move we report \textbf{no evidence of a practically meaningful change under the pre-specified
$\pm0.15$ equivalence margin}, a margin frozen before the runs (Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}), never a
claim that suppression is unchanged. On the arms where we report it, that margin is one-sided in a way the
phrase does not say on its own: identity-rung mean ASR is below $0.15$ there, so $\Delta > -0.15$ holds by
arithmetic before any seed runs and the only leg that tests anything is the upper one, which bounds a
\emph{rise}. Section~\ref{par:reach} scores every arm on both legs and prints the one-sided bound
beside each two-sided interval. Two design choices do more for precision than seeds would: the
identity rungs are imported \textbf{bit-identical per seed} (largest deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$),
so the contrast is within-seed rather than across noise, and the central claim is a \textbf{sign
reversal} ($-0.273$ against $+0.125$ at $n{=}20$ on the identical cell, Table~\ref{tab:comparability}), which is not
a null result. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} says the cell that would validate the
criterion is \emph{empty by design}: more seeds cannot repair it (Table~\ref{tab:can_cannot}).

\paragraph{4. If ASR does not move, why does a change of decision matter?} Because the change of
decision is what a preservation criterion \emph{measures}, and the gap between the two is the finding.
An invariance argument certifies the top three levels of Definition~\ref{def:levels_full} while the security
content sits at level~(P4), the adversarial contribution admitted. A criterion reading the Krum arm would
call that composition broken (the decision flips in up to $80\%$ of rounds) for a composition whose
suppression shows no measurable change: a false alarm in the direction that costs coverage
(Proposition~\ref{prop:emergent_full}, recall bound $4/5$). The converse fails too: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s
selection is bit-identical at every rung yet it never suppresses ($0.584$ at the identity, Mode~S at
$n{=}8$) and its ASR \emph{falls} $0.425$ across that ladder, so exact invariance of the statistic
neither implies suppression nor holds it fixed. As in the body, the $\kappa{=}2$ endpoint carrying that
$0.425$ sits below our own clean-accuracy floor, so \textbf{the witness we rest on is the first
gate-passing rung} ($0.173$); the full-ladder figure is quoted here only for its sign
(§\ref{app:targeted_full}).

\paragraph{5. The theory covers only positive per-client rescalings.} Yes, and we say so wherever it is
used (Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}, Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight},
§\ref{sec:restricted_theorem}). The class is not arbitrary (it is what norm clipping, contribution
weighting and reputation weighting do to a client's update) but it excludes coordinate-wise filters and
any transform that changes an update's direction, and the theorem's own margin closes under heterogeneity
($m{=}3.24 \to 1.01$ at $\alpha{=}0.1$), so \textbf{it certifies nothing where heterogeneity is high}.
The negative does not rest on the theory: it is a \emph{measured} counterexample, and a narrower theory
makes the counterexample sharper rather than weaker, since \textbf{the theorem certifies mechanism
preservation, not security}; it bounds no ASR.

\section{The oracle-free boundary instrument: the freeze we superseded, and the corrected instrument's machinery}
\label{app:blend_provenance}

\noindent This section holds the provenance of the boundary-blend instrument of main
§\ref{app:channel_separation}: the measurements behind our first freeze of it failing its own share gate,
the correction that freeze's own sentence needed, and the machinery of the corrected instrument. The gate
in question is \texttt{SHARE\_TOL} $=10^{-6}$, imported from the admission measurement and the tolerance
Mode~S itself meets; the \emph{flip} gate is that the shipped selections differ across the arm pair. The
verdicts of main §\ref{app:channel_separation} rest on the corrected instrument alone, and nothing here
revises any of them.

\textbf{What the first freeze measured, and the sentence in it that needs correcting.} It bisected the
crossing of the float64 Gram statistic; the shipped \texttt{krum\_selection} runs in float32 and crosses at
a slightly different $t$, so the converged bracket is tight around the wrong predicate. Measured on live
stacks before any arm was run, that instrument's supremum share gap exceeds \texttt{SHARE\_TOL} in $13$ of
$13$ flip rounds, up to $1.059{\times}10^{-4}$, and its realized gap exceeds it in $11$ of the $12$ rounds
that carry an adversary; it also fails its own flip gate, the shipped selections differing across the pair
in only $10$ of $13$. One sentence in that document needs correcting rather than defending: the
$5.109{\times}10^{-7}$ it offered as an existence proof of a within-tolerance pair is the \emph{minimum}
over those same $12$ rounds and the only one inside tolerance, and the supremum on that same round is
$8.368{\times}10^{-5}$. The repair its own linearity argument implies, shrink the bracket and bisect the
same float64 predicate to convergence, is measured too: it is share-neutral to $3.3{\times}10^{-16}$ and
the shipped selections differ in $0$ of $13$, so it is perfectly neutral and informative on nothing.

\textbf{The cost bound, and the cost.} The step cap of $40$ is a \emph{cost} bound, so exhausting it is a
gate failure and never a relaxed tolerance. One shipped evaluation costs $66.0$ ms at
$D = 1{,}117{,}354$, and the corrected bisection needs $2$ to $26$ shipped Krum evaluations per round. The
realized cost, re-derived from the finished artifact rather than carried over as the arithmetic we froze,
is $40$ runs in $8.7$ h, of which the search is $29{,}925$ shipped Krum evaluations, or $0.55$ h and $6\%$
of the total; we had projected $0.95$ h of search on an $11.5$ h ladder from main
§\ref{app:oracle_free}'s measured per-run cost, so this arm came in under its own estimate rather than
over it.

\textbf{How the supersession is enforced, and the four harness checks that ran before the ladder.} The
first freeze, \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak pre\_registration\_\allowbreak oracle\_free\_\allowbreak
decomposition.md} at commit \texttt{bed6562}, is left byte-untouched and superseded by
\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak pre\_registration\_\allowbreak oracle\_free\_\allowbreak
decomposition\_refined.md} at \texttt{947c000}, which cites it, records the failure with the numbers above,
pins the premise artifact by md5, and corrects that sentence; the runner refuses to start unless the
superseded document is present, last committed at its recorded hash, and clean, so the append-only claim is
enforced mechanically rather than promised. Four harness checks ran before the ladder and their verdict
dict is persisted into the artifact rather than left in a terminal. The one keyword this arm adds to shared
code is bit-neutral on a frozen Mode~S cell at $\Delta=(0,0)$; the blend at $t{=}0$ reproduces main
§\ref{app:oracle_free}'s identity arm at a shared seed, also at $\Delta=(0,0)$, \emph{and} reports that the
hook was invoked $50$ times over $50$ rounds, which is the assertion that matters, because the standing
failure mode in this codebase is a manipulation hook that is never called while the run passes silently;

\section{The pre-registered composition audit: rubric, frame and the 59 coded papers}
\label{app:audit}

\noindent §\ref{sec:related} reports one rate out of this audit and withdraws one claim because of it. This subsection is the artifact behind both. The rubric is \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak pre\_registration\_literature\_audit.md}, the coder's records are one JSON file per paper under \texttt{results/\allowbreak literature\_audit/\allowbreak coding/}, and every count in this subsection is emitted by \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak analyze\_literature\_audit.py} into \texttt{results/\allowbreak literature\_audit/\allowbreak summary.json}. \textbf{The body of Tables~\ref{tab:audit_rows_a} and~\ref{tab:audit_rows_b} is a file that script writes}, \texttt{results/\allowbreak literature\_audit/\allowbreak table\_rows.tex}, so no code and no count in them is transcribed by hand.

\textbf{Not all of the rubric predates the coding, and that is the first thing to report rather than the last.} Table~\ref{tab:audit_timeline} is the chronology. Pre-registered without qualification, and unchanged from the freeze to now: the primary quantity, the five criteria's text, the four ambiguity rules, the frame and its screening procedure, the ten-paper floor, and the three admissible outcomes. Amendment 1 re-pointed the retrieval endpoint before any paper had been retrieved, and changed no query string, no criterion and no threshold. Amendments 2 through 5 are adjudication rules for questions the frozen criteria underdetermine, and they were written with rows already in hand. \textbf{Exactly one of them moves a reported number.} Under Amendment 4a's mechanism-distinctness rule the primary is $6/59$; without it, $5/59$. The single row that turns on it is \texttt{2601.06466}, whose three-way threshold decision over a Mahalanobis score and a trajectory score is three components under 4a and one under the coarser reading, and both rates are reported everywhere either is.

\textbf{The frame is a procedure, not a reading list.} Three routes in: every \texttt{references.bib} entry that proposes or evaluates an FL poisoning or backdoor defense; five frozen query strings issued against the arXiv search interface, paginated to exhaustion, over 2019--2026; and the complete title listings of USENIX Security, NDSS, IEEE S\&P and PMLR screened by the frozen regex \texttt{federated} AND (\texttt{backdoor} OR \texttt{poison} OR \texttt{byzantine} OR \texttt{robust}). A one-hop snowball through the defense-comparison tables of \citet{nguyen2022flame}, \citet{rieger2022deepsight} and \citet{fenaux2025hammer} closes it, and no second hop is taken. \textbf{Adding a query after seeing results is forbidden by the rubric and none was added.} The bias this realizes is stated in the same breath as the rate: coding requires quotable full text, so the frame is \emph{arXiv-reachable} FL poisoning-defense work, ACM CCS is a coverage gap rather than a sample because its proceedings pages return HTTP 403, \textbf{and CCS is not the only gap}: OpenReview is unscreened, so ICLR and NeurIPS proceedings enter only by the \texttt{references.bib} and snowball routes, as do IEEE venues other than S\&P on IEEE Xplore. A paper whose title carries none of the regex's tokens enters only by those same two routes. \textbf{We do not close these gaps by hand}: the rubric forbids adding a retrieval route after seeing results, and a hand-added paper would void the pre-registration it is supposed to be evidence under, so the gaps are named and left open.

\textbf{The funnel.} $622$ records retrieved, $593$ after deduplication, all $593$ screened on title and abstract, $59$ full texts sought and $59$ obtained, $59$ included, $534$ excluded. Of the exclusions, $362$ are single-mechanism or non-pipeline on their own title and abstract, $71$ are a retrieval artifact in which a bibliography line was captured as a record, $38$ were adjudicated individually on other stated grounds, $24$ name two mechanism families with no joint application, $18$ are retrieval-stage losses with neither an abstract nor a resolvable identifier, $15$ are surveys, reviews or position papers, and $6$ are outside federated learning. Of the $593$ screening decisions, $495$ are mechanical and every one of those is an exclusion; the remaining $98$ were adjudicated by hand, the $59$ inclusions and $39$ further exclusions, because the mechanical rule can only exclude and \emph{no} paper enters the audit without a hand decision. Every exclusion carries its reason string in \texttt{results/\allowbreak literature\_audit/\allowbreak screening.json}, and the analyzer refuses to print any rate if one does not.

\textbf{The criteria, and why the primary is a conjunction with a negated term.} C-a: two or more mechanisms applied jointly to the same round. C-b: the pairing is selected or motivated by the constituents' standalone effectiveness or their complementary threat coverage, and by nothing else. C-c: the composed system's own adversarial outcome, its ASR, backdoor accuracy or robust accuracy, is what validates it. C-d: an identifying contrast, meaning an experiment or derivation that varies the \emph{upstream} stage with the downstream mechanism and the attack held fixed. C-e: a mechanism-attribution claim is actually made. \textbf{The primary is C-a$\wedge$C-b$\wedge$C-c$\wedge$C-e$\wedge\neg$C-d}, and the C-e term is what stops it from measuring ``nobody runs the contrast we invented'', which would be near total and worth nothing. Any UNCLEAR criterion sends the paper's verdict to UNCLEAR, and UNCLEAR papers leave both the numerator and the denominator, so the measured prevalence can only be smaller than a guessing coder's. No row resolved to UNCLEAR.

\textbf{The result, with the secondaries it must be read against.} Primary $6/59 = 10.2\%$, or $5/59$ without Amendment 4a. The outcome-gated \emph{design}, C-a$\wedge$C-b$\wedge$C-c, holds for $33/59 = 55.9\%$. An identifying contrast is \emph{present} in $46/59 = 78.0\%$, and among the $33$ design papers, $27$ run one. \textbf{So the six are papers that had the design and did not run the contrast, not papers for which it was unavailable}, and any sentence implying the contrast is rare in this literature is false against our own audit. The pre-registered secondary that would count against the sharp reading, a design paper making no attributional claim at all, is $0/33$; the one C-e $=$ NO row in the frame, \texttt{1909.05125}, has no design either, and a paper with neither is evidence in neither direction. The six are \texttt{2307.00356}, \texttt{2310.13403}, \texttt{2502.07011}, \texttt{2507.16134}, \texttt{2601.01053} and \texttt{2601.06466}.

\textbf{What the $21$ C-a $=$ NO rows are made of, in three kinds.} First, Amendment 3's purpose test excises constituents whose own stated purpose is privacy, communication efficiency or server verifiability rather than resisting corrupted updates, which is what leaves \texttt{2607.06612} and \texttt{2609.03420} with exactly one defense-purposed mechanism per round. Second, several papers decompose a \emph{single} mechanism into stages that exist to serve it: \texttt{2505.17226}'s median filter repairs an estimate the Krum-derived rule needs, and \texttt{2311.15894}'s four steps are four steps of one rule. Third, \texttt{2302.07173} evaluates its schemes as alternatives to one another, which C-a's own exclusion list names as a menu rather than a composition. The per-row basis is in each record's C-a locator, and the reader who disagrees with a call can see the sentence it rests on.

\textbf{C-b fails on five of the $38$ C-a $=$ YES rows, for four distinct reasons, and the reasons matter more than the count.} \texttt{2508.12978} composes for \emph{guarantee preservation}, proving that adding its compression does not destroy what the aggregator already provides, which is a reason about the composition rather than about the constituents' effectiveness. \texttt{2601.04930} and \texttt{2602.22269} give a real constituent-property reason of the residual-coverage kind, but the surface being closed is one an Amendment-3-excluded constituent creates, so the pairing reason does not connect two counted mechanisms. \texttt{2503.18284} names the deficiencies of \emph{prior} schemes as motivation for its framework and states the integration of its own two stages as a fact and an optimization convenience. \texttt{1909.05125}'s only stated reason is computational and communication efficiency. \textbf{We record these rather than fold them in}: three of the four are pairing reasons of kinds the rubric could plausibly have admitted, and it does not, so C-b is stricter than it might have been in a direction that shrinks the primary.

\textbf{The C-d route field stratifies the $46$ contrasts and decides nothing.} Of them, $21$ are hyperparameter sweeps of an upstream defense parameter with an adversarial outcome read at each setting, $21$ are component contrasts in which an upstream stage is removed, substituted or cumulatively built up, and $4$ are analytic. Precedence when a row satisfies more than one is sweep, then contrast, then analytic, fixed in advance of the backward pass rather than deferred to which finding a paper calls central. \textbf{Amendment 5's backward pass was run across all $46$ rows and relabelled four}, \texttt{2409.01435}, \texttt{2502.00587}, \texttt{2505.10297} and \texttt{2604.03862}, moving the stratification from $25/17/4$ to $21/21/4$; no rate moved, because the field enters none. \textbf{It is a floor and not a census}, since it records only routes a record actually quotes. LASA is the proof: its appendix sweep of the upstream sparsification level, with accuracy under three attacks at every rung and the downstream filter fixed, was in the paper and merely pointed at in the record until the backward pass quoted it.

\textbf{One contrast in the frame is confounded by its own design, and it is coded YES anyway.} \texttt{2609.03420} varies its upstream privacy budget as a graded dose with the downstream median and the attacks fixed, and states the reason: ``This selection isolates privacy-fairness interactions from aggregation-induced variance.'' But the client learning rate is a declared function of that budget, so two things move together along the ladder, and the paper demonstrates that this matters by reporting collapse rather than a graded decline under a static rate. The rubric's fourth ambiguity rule gives C-d the benefit of the doubt, and the code follows the rule rather than our preference: a rule that resolves toward finding the contrast present is a rule that shrinks our own primary.

\textbf{Quotation integrity, and the substitutions this table cannot avoid.} Every quotation in every record is asserted verbatim against the paper's own extracted full text by \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak verify\_quotes\_literature\_audit.py}, which exits non-zero on any failure: $59$ records, $295$ quotations, $334$ spans, all verbatim, with four spans under $40$ characters and two pairs out of source order reported rather than failed, the locator saying in each case that the spans are not one passage. \textbf{The first run of that check over the whole set failed $28$ quotations in $15$ records, and the repair log is committed} at \texttt{results/\allowbreak literature\_audit/\allowbreak quote\_repair\_log.md} with the pre-repair records beside it. \textbf{No code changed in any repair}, and two repairs produced \emph{stronger} evidence for the code already recorded, which is the uncomfortable part: the defective quotations were faithful paraphrases, so a reader checking the argument would not have caught them and only the machine check did. One consequence lands in this subsection. Verbatim spans contain a true minus, an en dash inside a citation range, typographic apostrophes, \texttt{ff} and \texttt{fi} ligatures, Greek and math-italic letters and a set-membership sign, none of which this paper's source may carry, so quoted spans are rendered with a LaTeX equivalent, for instance \texttt{CSFT's} for the typographic apostrophe and $\delta$ for math-italic delta. \textbf{The JSON records, not this section, are the authority for what each paper says.}

\textbf{Extraction hazards, named because they are what a re-adjudication will hit first.} \texttt{pdftotext} interleaves the columns of a two-column layout, which spliced a word from the adjacent column into a sentence in \texttt{2503.18284} and produced adjacent-but-unrelated spans in \texttt{2602.16480} and \texttt{2607.06612}; three papers, \texttt{2606.31066}, \texttt{2608.08574} and \texttt{2608.21172}, emit table bodies in column-major order, so a number's row label is not the label above it in the stream; and displayed fractions and page numbers appear mid-sentence. Every affected locator says so. Separately, \texttt{2609.03064} carries a journal line dated 2024 against a 2609 arXiv identifier; we code it under the arXiv year and record the inconsistency rather than choose silently.

\textbf{What this audit does not establish, recorded before the numbers existed and unchanged by them.} It is one coder with no inter-rater statistic, and the mitigation is the quotation rather than a claim of reliability. Four rows were known to us before the freeze and are marked in the tables: coding them is not blind. The rate is about this frame, which is arXiv-reachable, preprint-heavy and 2026-heavy, and it is not an estimate of ``the FL literature''. And the rate carries no claim that the six papers' conclusions are wrong: the audit measures whether an evaluation design can identify the mechanism it attributes to, which is a question about what the evidence licenses and not about whether the defense works.

\label{app:randomization}

\textbf{What this is, and what it is not.} This is a new experiment, pre-registered in
\texttt{experiments/}\allowbreak\texttt{pre\_registration\_randomized\_composition.md} and committed at
\texttt{e3fb038} before the first run. It is \emph{not} a reinstatement of the randomized-menu comparison
§\ref{sec:related} withdraws: that artifact stays withdrawn, because it is unpaired and
compute-confounded, and nothing below is scored against it. The question here is narrower and answerable:
holding defense computation fixed, does randomizing \emph{which} composition is applied each round cost
suppression relative to committing to the best member of the same set? The direction was fixed in advance
as $H_1: \bar{\Delta} > 0$, randomization \emph{hurts}, because that is the claim the withdrawn
comparison made and the claim this test is built to substantiate.

objects.} Materiality required \emph{both} $\bar{\Delta} \geq 0.10$ and at least $4/5$ positive signs.
\emph{(i) Material, in the pre-registered direction, on model scaling}: $\bar{\Delta}=+0.1012$ with $5/5$
signs, so we state, scoped to this menu and this attack, that randomizing over non-degenerate
compositions costs suppression at matched defense computation. \emph{(ii) Not material on the pixel
backdoor}: $\bar{\Delta}=+0.0663$ clears the sign leg but not the magnitude leg, and the honest phrasing
at $n=5$ is that no material difference was detected and the smallest effect this design resolves is
$\approx 0.10$. \emph{(iii) The accuracy floor is breached by one arm}: \texttt{temporal\_mix} under model
scaling means $0.307$ clean accuracy against a frozen floor of $0.35$, so that cell is reported as
uninterpretable for ASR rather than compared, and every contrast involving it inherits the exclusion.
This is why arm 3 is a reference point in the table and carries no verdict.

\textbf{Three caveats, and what survives them.} First, $\bar{\Delta}$ clears the $0.10$ threshold by
$0.0012$ and is carried by two of five seeds ($+0.2899$ and $+0.1564$); on the three others the gap is
$\leq 0.036$. A threshold met by $1.2\%$ of itself is met, and it is also the weakest way a threshold can
be met, so we do not describe the scaling result as large. Second, the two tests disagree in opposite
directions across the two attacks: Wilcoxon reaches $p=0.0312$ on both while the $t$-test reaches
$p=0.0299$ on pixel, where materiality \emph{fails}, and only $p=0.0677$ on scaling, where it holds. The
stable part of the result is the sign structure, $10/10$ across both attacks, and not either magnitude.
Third, control 1 fails as literally pre-registered, as recorded above. What survives all three is the
weakest claim consistent with them and the one the design was built to license: at matched defense
computation and with the degeneracy confound removed, randomizing over a set of compositions did not
\emph{help} on either attack and was consistently worse on both, materially so on one. Nothing here
licenses a claim about randomized menus in general, about randomized \emph{internals} such as noise or
subsampling (L3), or about any menu but this one.

\textbf{And the attack is committed in both arms, which is the scope condition that bites hardest on what
this test can be read as answering.} Randomization's theoretical case is against an adversary that
\emph{adapts} to the composition: a menu the adversary cannot predict is a menu it cannot optimize against.
Both attacks here are fixed in advance, identical in every arm, and blind to the draw. So this arm bounds
the \emph{cost} of randomizing under a committed threat and measures none of the benefit randomization is
proposed for, and a favourable outcome would not have shown that benefit either. No adaptive-adversary test
of a randomized menu is run here or anywhere else in this paper: the criterion-aware adversary of
§\ref{sec:fg_rfa_flagship} adapts to a composition that is \emph{fixed} and known to it, which is the
opposite of the setting randomization is meant to deny an adversary, and building an adversary that adapts
to a distribution over menus is a separate experiment we do not run.

\section{Equivalence testing at the existing seed counts: where the frozen margin passes and where it only appears to}
\label{app:tost}

\textbf{Why this is here and what it is not.} The pre-registered rule scores a \emph{point} estimate
against a margin: $|\mathrm{mean}\,\mathrm{ASR}(\kappa{=}2) - \mathrm{mean}\,\mathrm{ASR}(\kappa{=}0)| <
0.15$ and every rung $< 0.5$. A point estimate inside a margin is consistent with a negligible effect
\emph{and} with an effect the sample cannot locate, and those are different claims. Table~\ref{tab:tost}
scores the same frozen margin as a two-one-sided-$t$ (TOST) equivalence test on the \emph{paired}
per-seed differences, declaring equivalence only when the $90\%$ interval lies inside $(-0.15,+0.15)$.
It is \textbf{post-hoc}, reads only frozen artifacts, \textbf{adds no runs and revises no published
verdict}; the emitter is \texttt{experiments/analyze\_tost\_existing.py}. Pairing is not a modelling
choice: every rung of an arm runs at the same seeds, so each difference holds one data partition fixed
and the seed-to-seed spread that dominates this suite drops out of the contrast.

\paragraph{Which leg is a test: on most of these arms ASR's own floor satisfies the lower one before
any run.}
\label{par:reach}
Equivalence at a symmetric margin is two claims at once, that the effect does not rise past $+0.15$ and
that it does not fall past $-0.15$, and on most of these arms the second one cannot fail. ASR is bounded
below by $0$, so for \emph{any} dosed outcome whatsoever, a perfect defense included, the paired mean
obeys $\mathrm{mean}_i(\Delta_i) \ge -\,\mathrm{mean}_i \mathrm{ASR}^{\mathrm{id}}_i$: the largest fall
arithmetically available on a cell is its identity rung's own mean ASR. \textbf{Where that mean is below
the margin, the lower leg is satisfied before a single run happens.} It is not a test, and the honest
form of the claim is a pre-registered \emph{non-increase} reported with its $95\%$ upper bound. Where the
identity mean exceeds the margin both legs are tests, the two-sided reading is earned, and those arms
keep it. Table~\ref{tab:reach} scores every arm of Table~\ref{tab:tost} on this, and §\ref{app:targeted_full}
already states the same arithmetic for one arm's ladder branch, in the same words (\emph{arithmetically
unreachable}, at an identity rung of $0.062$); what is added here is that it holds on nearly every arm
this paper reads an equivalence off, which we had not said. \textbf{The one Mode~S arm on which the
lower leg \emph{is} a test reports a fall that leaves the margin} (§\ref{app:pixel_headroom}, identity
mean $0.3232$, $\Delta = -0.286$): the two-sided reading fails there in the direction of more
suppression while the one-sided bound on a rise holds at $-0.087$, which is why the distinction drawn
here is substantive rather than a change of wording.

\noindent \textbf{Which leg sets the published $p$, and the answer is the wrong one.} TOST reports
$\max(p_{\mathrm{lower}},p_{\mathrm{upper}})$, so exactly one leg sets the number each row prints, and on
$5$ of the $6$ arms this table marks equivalent that maximum is attained by the leg ASR's floor has
already settled. The flagship's printed $p{=}0.0014$ is its \emph{lower} leg's; the $p$ for the claim
actually being made, that suppression does not meaningfully \emph{rise}, is $0.0004$. The same holds for
the score-only control ($0.0071$ printed, $0.0024$ one-sided), the EMNIST-byclass row ($0.0004$,
$0.0003$) and ResNet18 ($0.0404$, $0.0143$). \textbf{So the relabelling reports a stronger number than
the one already published on each of those arms, not a weaker one}, and no published $p$ is revised
anywhere: $p_{\mathrm{tost}}$ stays exactly as printed and the upper-leg $p$ is reported beside it as the
separate quantity it is. The one equivalent arm whose $p$ is set by the leg that \emph{is} a test is the
CIFAR-100 row, at an identity mean of $0.703$.

\noindent \textbf{Beyond this table, and the count that may be quoted.} Three further arms carry an
equivalence reading and are scored by the same emitter. The body's $n{=}20$ top-up of row~1 has identity
mean $0.044$ and upper bound $+0.012$; Mode~M, the coordinate-masking class of §\ref{app:factorial}, has
$0.053$ and $+0.019$; the $200$-round ResNet18 replication has $0.045$ and $+0.138$. All three have an
arithmetic lower leg. Counted over \emph{distinct arms} rather than rows, because the flagship and its
EMNIST replication are each scored in more than one place at more than one seed block, the paper makes
equivalence readings about $5$ arms and \textbf{$4$ of the $5$ have an arithmetically satisfied lower
leg}. The fifth, \texttt{coord\_median} / scaling under the outcome-gated design (identity mean $0.557$,
$95\%$ CI $[+0.023,+0.102]$, $n{=}20$), keeps a genuinely two-sided reading and is reported as one;
after its seed top-up that interval is inside the margin \emph{and} excludes zero, which separates the
margin reading from a reading of no effect on this arm. So
\emph{every} equivalence claim is not the right quantifier and is not used. \textbf{The frozen
point-estimate rule has the same arithmetic on its lower side}: $\Delta > -0.15$ is free whenever the
identity mean is below $0.15$, so a sentence reading $|\Delta| < 0.15$ off one of these arms inherits the
same one-sidedness. \textbf{Nothing here re-margins, re-tests or withdraws a published interval}; the
emitter is \texttt{experiments/\allowbreak measure\_\allowbreak margin\_\allowbreak reachability.py},
which reads frozen artifacts only, recomputes every interval with the estimator its own table used, and
asserts each one equal to the published value before it reports anything about that arm's legs
(\texttt{results/\allowbreak margin\_\allowbreak reachability.json}).
\textbf{The fifth arm's $n{=}20$ figures above are the one exception to that provenance, and not to the
claim}: that artifact predates this arm's top-up and still carries its identity mean at $0.519$ on
$n{=}5$, so the $0.557$ and the interval quoted beside it are read from
\texttt{results/\allowbreak coordmedian\_\allowbreak scaling\_\allowbreak topup/\allowbreak summary.json}
instead, and no frozen value is rewritten to agree with them.
