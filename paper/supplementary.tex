\section{The prospective suite, arm by arm}
\label{app:prospective_arms}

\textbf{(i) FLTrust as $d_1$ (7 pairs): out of scope, 2/7.} The five predicted-HIGH misses are fltrust$\to$\{rep, nc, krum, mkrum, tm\}. Their C2/C3 failures are real (FLTrust does destroy Reputation's consensus-distance signal), and are costless because $d_2$ was never load-bearing: the C0 mechanism of §\ref{sec:prospective}.

\textbf{(ii) FLTrust as $d_2$ (6 pairs): in scope, 6/6, and the near-identity of §\ref{sec:prospective} is visible per seed rather than only in the aggregate.} Krum$\to$FLTrust and MKrum$\to$FLTrust are \textbf{bit-identical} to FLTrust alone on all six runs ($0.0519/0.0459/0.0453$ scaling, $0.0492/0.0466/0.0426$ pixel), as expected for a pass-through $d_1$. NC$\to$FLTrust is \emph{bit-identical on the pixel attack}, where no client exceeds $\tau{=}5$ so $c_i \equiv 1$, and differs only within seed noise on model-scaling, where clipping actually fires. Rep$\to$FLTrust and RFA$\to$FLTrust likewise differ only within noise. The single genuine deviation is FG$\to$FLTrust ($0.064$, per-seed up to $0.084$): the one $d_1$ that produces exact \emph{zeros} rather than positive rescalings, changing FLTrust's effective client set instead of only its magnitudes, which is the Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} interaction. Every $d_1$ in the menu is a non-negative per-client rescaling whose positive part FLTrust cancels exactly, so only the \emph{zero pattern} is free to matter, which is where the one deviation appears.

\textbf{(iii) Neither defense is FLTrust (7 pairs): 5/7.} Per-pair values are in Table~\ref{tab:prospective}; both misses are diagnosed in §\ref{sec:prospective} and neither is a mechanism error. This arm is the one §\ref{sec:prospective} calls the only one that could have surprised us, and §\ref{app:mkrum} reports that $5$ of its $7$ pairs reduce to a single defense, leaving $2$ that compose two real mechanisms.

\section{MultiKrum at $K{=}5$, and the matched contrasts we withdrew}
\label{app:mkrum}

\textbf{The mechanism.} \texttt{multi\_krum} scores each participant by its summed distance to nearest neighbours, keeps the $k$ lowest and averages them, with $k$ defaulting to $5$. Every experiment in this project runs \texttt{clients\_per\_round}${}={}5$ and none passes $k$, so the kept set is \emph{all five} participants and the call reduces to plain averaging. Measured rather than argued (\texttt{experiments/verify\_wave3\_invariance.py}, test D; \texttt{experiments/analyze\_mkrum\_degeneracy.py}): at $K{=}5$ the largest absolute difference between \texttt{multi\_krum} and \texttt{fedavg} on identical inputs is $1.9\times10^{-6}$, which is float summation order alone; at $K{=}10$ it is $25.2$, a genuine selector again. MultiKrum is therefore a distinct rule in this codebase only when more clients participate than it selects, which no run reported here does.

\textbf{Two degeneracies compose, and we had recognized only one.} \emph{(A) Pass-through $d_1$.} A rank aggregator emits one vector rather than per-client updates, so \texttt{krum}, \texttt{multi\_krum}, \texttt{trimmed\_mean} and \texttt{coord\_median} supply no upstream transform and $d_1{\to}d_2$ measures $d_2$ alone. This was known and is what the table's DEGEN label records; it makes Krum$\to$FLTrust and MKrum$\to$FLTrust, and Krum$\to$RFA and MKrum$\to$RFA, the same measurement twice (per-seed $|\Delta\mathrm{ASR}| = 0.000$ on both attacks). \emph{(B) MultiKrum as $d_2$.} Because MultiKrum is FedAvg here, $d_1{\to}$MKrum applies $d_1$'s per-client transform and then averages, which for every weighting or clipping $d_1$ in this menu \emph{is} that defense standalone. This is the new fact, and the affected rows carry frozen labels of C1-fail or C2-fail rather than DEGEN because it was not known when the labels were committed. Counting both, $8$ of the suite's $20$ rows reduce to a single defense, not the $5$ marked DEGEN: the five already marked, plus RFA$\to$MKrum, NC$\to$MKrum and FLTrust$\to$MKrum.

\textbf{The matched contrasts do not survive, and substituting Krum is what shows it.} §\ref{sec:prospective} previously drew three contrasts holding $d_1$ fixed while $d_2$ moved from FLTrust to a Euclidean rule, reading the gap as evidence for Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}. Two used MultiKrum and so had no downstream defense on one side. Replacing it with the genuine selector Krum gives a repaired-looking $12.6$--$19.1\times$ gap, and the repair is illusory: for the gap to be about invariance, C1 would have to hold across it, and it does not. Every Krum arm takes its max-committed ASR from the pixel backdoor, on which Krum's own standalone ASR is $0.583$, above threshold.

The NormClip row is the sharpest form of the point: NC$\to$Krum is bit-identical to Krum alone on all three seeds ($0.779 / 0.039 / 0.932$, accuracy $0.396 / 0.592 / 0.467$), because under the pixel backdoor the maximum client update norm over $45$ client-rounds is $3.04 < \tau{=}5$, so NormClip is the exact identity in $9/9$ measured rounds (test F; under model-scaling norms reach $24.5$ and it binds in $11/45$). Nothing about the invariance class of $d_2$ is being tested there. In $0$ of the $3$ contrasts does the non-invariant $d_2$ suppress its own max-committed attack standalone, which is the condition that would make the contrast informative. The Krum arms for NC and RFA come from the metric-swap suite (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}) rather than the prospective suite, at the identical protocol and the same seeds $42$--$44$; we note the change of source rather than smooth it over.

\textbf{What arm (iii) is left with.} Of its $7$ pairs, Krum$\to$CM is CoordMedian alone, Krum$\to$RFA and MKrum$\to$RFA and RFA$\to$MKrum are RFA alone, and NC$\to$MKrum is NormClip alone. Two compose two real mechanisms: FG$\to$Krum, which is a miss at $0.078$ and $9.9\%$ clean accuracy (the collapsed-model artifact), and Rep$\to$Krum, which is a hit at $0.880$. So the arm's $5/7$ is $1/2$ on the pairs that put two mechanisms in series, and the surviving hit is a predicted HIGH, so it still bears on specificity only.

\textbf{A noise floor this degeneracy hands us, and a second use for the accuracy gate.} $d_1{\to}$MKrum and $d_1{\to}$FedAvg are the same function computed through two code paths that differ by $1.9\times10^{-6}$ per aggregation, as are MultiKrum and FedAvg standalone. Over $50$ rounds that difference is amplified chaotically through the training trajectory, so whatever such a cell pair disagrees by is a measured floor on how small an ASR difference can be called real at this protocol. At matched seeds, across the five such cells with clean accuracy above the $0.35$ gate, the per-seed $|\Delta\mathrm{ASR}|$ reaches $0.046$; that is what licenses the paper's repeated ``differ only within seed noise''. In the one cell below the gate, MultiKrum against FedAvg under model-scaling at $0.10$ accuracy, the same function disagrees with itself by $0.270$ (per-seed ASR $0.000/0.995/0.730$ against $0.000/1.000/1.000$). So the accuracy gate is a precondition for reading small ASR differences at all, not only for reading suppression claims: on a collapsed model ASR is not a stable quantity. Neither figure is to be confused with the exactly $0.000$ of degeneracy (A), where the two rows traverse an identical code path.

\textbf{What we did not change.} The suite's frozen categories, predictions and $13/20$ score stand exactly as committed at \texttt{262cf35}; relabelling them after seeing the data is what the pre-registration forbids. What is retracted is the body's interpretation of three rows and of the three contrasts, and Table~\ref{tab:prospective} carries a marker and a footnote rather than an edited label.

\section{Metric-swap ablation: verification and per-cell detail}
\label{app:metric_swap_detail}

\textbf{The mechanism is verified in code before it is claimed.} An outcome alone would leave open whether the mirrors are invariant at all, so we check the statistic itself rather than only the ASR (\texttt{experiments/verify\_cos\_invariance.py}, \texttt{results/cos\_invariance\_check.json}), comparing each $d_2$'s internal state on $\{u_i\}$ against $\{c_iu_i\}$ for the $c_i$ that NormClip and RFA actually produce on live rounds. Over 18 rounds (3 seeds $\times$ 2 upstream defenses $\times$ 3 rounds), \texttt{cos\_reputation}'s weight vector agrees to $\le 2.5\times10^{-7}$ and its weight \emph{ordering} never changes ($0/18$), and \texttt{cos\_krum} selects the same client in $18/18$. The Euclidean originals do not: Reputation's weights move by up to $0.222$ and its ordering changes in $12/18$ rounds; Krum's selection changes in $6/18$. The size of the disturbance tracks the realized weight ratio $\rho$, as Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} predicts: under NormClip ($\rho \in [1.00,4.90]$) Reputation's ordering changes in $3/9$ rounds and Krum's selection in $0/9$; under RFA ($\rho \in [2.11,17.38]$) they change in $9/9$ and $6/9$. On 1000 synthetic draws with larger rescalings the separation is cleaner: ordering changes in $0/1000$ trials for \texttt{cos\_reputation} against $992/1000$ for Reputation, and $0/1000$ against $618/1000$ for the two Krums. The mirrors are additionally checked against the shipped implementations they stand in for (selected index identical, aggregate relative error ${\approx}10^{-8}$, below the ${\approx}7\times10^{-5}$ MPS-versus-CPU nondeterminism of the same computation), so the invariance is a property of the statistic and not of a divergent reimplementation.

One prediction in the suite was genuinely two-sided, and it is the round's only prospective positive result. \texttt{cos\_krum}'s C1 verdict on model-scaling turns entirely on whether the accuracy gate is applied: $0.078$ passes the numeric threshold, $0.146$ accuracy fails the gate. Rather than choose a reading after the fact we froze \emph{both}, with a resolution rule fixed in advance. Primary (accuracy-gated) $\Rightarrow$ C1 fails $\Rightarrow$ HIGH; secondary (numeric C1 only) $\Rightarrow$ the pair is certified $\Rightarrow$ LOW; and if the outcome had been LOW at accuracy $\ge 0.35$, the gate would have been wrong and our two primary labels for those cells would have been recorded as misses. Both pairs came out \textbf{HIGH} (NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.508$, RFA$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.566$), refuting the secondary reading and vindicating the accuracy gate against a pre-registered alternative that would have overturned it. This is a small result but a real one: the gate is usually defended by argument, and here it beat a competing specification of itself on data neither of us had seen. We flag the weaker half honestly: NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum}'s $0.508$ sits barely above threshold on per-seed values $0.084 / 0.676 / 0.766$, so that cell is threshold-noise even though its label is correct, and the RFA arm at $0.566$ (per-seed $1.000 / 0.121 / 0.576$) is what carries the conclusion.

The pattern holds across the suite once cells without power are excluded. $\Delta$ is only observable where the standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack, since a baseline already above $0.5$ has no suppression left to lose and will show $\Delta \approx 0$ whatever the transform did: a ceiling effect, not preservation. Restricting to standalone ASR $< 0.5$ at accuracy $\ge 0.35$ leaves six cells, and measured disturbance splits them perfectly at both seed counts. At the frozen $n{=}3$: undisturbed ($0/9$) $\Delta \in [-0.027, +0.001]$, disturbed ($>0/9$) $\Delta \in [+0.630, +0.865]$, a separation of $+0.629$. At $n{=}5$ after the post-hoc top-up, with each standalone recomputed at the same five seeds from the dose ladder's $\kappa{=}0$ rung (\texttt{results/dose\_response/summary.json}, that $d_2$ alone under the identical configuration, bit-identical to the published standalone baselines on every seed they share, largest deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$): $[-0.044, +0.001]$ versus $[+0.717, +0.878]$, a separation of $+0.716$. Neither has any overlap, and the extra seeds widen the split rather than narrow it. At both seed counts the two extremes are the two Krum cells, which share a standalone, so the width of the split is algebraically the Krum contrast's own gap and not independent evidence beyond it; the other four cells extend the halves outward, not the distance between them. Two of the three undisturbed cells are \texttt{cos\_krum} on the pixel backdoor, where the mirror suppresses standalone ($0.300$ at $0.598$ accuracy on the frozen three seeds, $0.490$ at $0.607$ on all five) and carries that suppression through under \emph{both} upstreams: at $n{=}3$ intact ($0.300$, $\Delta = +0.000$ under NormClip; $0.273$, $\Delta = -0.027$ under RFA), at $n{=}5$ still intact ($0.490$, $\Delta = +0.000$; $0.447$, $\Delta = -0.044$; both at $0.61$ accuracy, on new per-seed values $0.864 / 0.686$ and $0.849 / 0.565$ against a baseline that moves to $0.490$ on those same seeds). The NormClip arm is intact \emph{exactly}: NormClip never binds on this attack ($0/9$ rounds; max client norm $3.04$ against $\tau = 5$), so its composition is the same five runs as the \texttt{cos\_krum} baseline, bit-identical per seed, and $\Delta = +0.000$ is an identity rather than a measurement. Had the frozen three-seed baseline been kept under the five-seed compositions, the same two rows would have read $\Delta = +0.190$ and $+0.147$, and the natural reading of that, the magnitude channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} moving $\Delta$ where the selection is exactly invariant, would have credited an effect to a transform that provably did nothing: the movement is entirely the baseline's seed set, which is why the subtrahend has to move with the composition. So the confirming half of the split is contributed by the invariant construction behaving as C2 predicts (which is the one thing the metric-swap arm does deliver, even though C1's failure on model-scaling denied it a frozen LOW label), and this contrast has no power on the magnitude channel in either direction: its only exactly-invariant downstream sits under an upstream that never rescales. That is a limitation of this post-hoc contrast and a direct motivation for arm 3 of the ladder, which moves that dial on purpose.

\textbf{Excluded cells.} All four \texttt{cos\_*} model-scaling cells are excluded as uninterpretable (hollow standalone baseline), and the six Reputation / \texttt{cos\_reputation} / Krum pixel cells for lack of power (standalone $\ge 0.5$).

\section{Dose--response design: the full pre-registered protocol}
\label{app:dose_protocol}
\label{app:details}

Four properties make this a dial on the theory rather than an arbitrary perturbation. (i) It is exactly Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}'s form $T(u_i)=c_iu_i$ with $c_i>0$, so every rung lies inside the theory's scope. (ii) \textbf{The dose is $\rho$}: $\max_j c_j/\min_j c_j = e^{2\kappa}$ exactly, the same upstream weight ratio Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} and both mechanism-preserving regimes are stated in, verified by reading $\rho$ back off the \emph{transformed update norms} on live rounds, where realized $\rho$ matches nominal to three decimals at every rung and $\mathrm{mean}(c)$ stays in $[0.99999, 1.00002]$. (iii) $\kappa{=}0$ gives $c \equiv 1$: the implementation short-circuits, so the identity rung is bit-identical to $d_2$ standalone by construction rather than by rounding. (iv) Normalizing the \emph{mean} to 1 holds the aggregate's scale fixed, separating the quantity under test (disturbance of $d_2$'s \emph{statistic}, i.e.\ C2) from the magnitude-contraction channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} that costs accuracy; without it the ladder would confound C2 with attenuation. The permutation is keyed on $(\text{seed},\text{round})$ and \textbf{not} on client identity: assigning by id would place the adversaries at a fixed end of the ladder and systematically attenuate them, manufacturing a success, whereas this keying gives every arm at a given seed and round the \emph{identical} coefficient vector, so the arms differ in $d_2$ and nothing else. \textbf{$\texttt{dose\_kappa}\langle\kappa\rangle$ is an instrument, not a defense}, and is never evaluated or recommended as one.

\textbf{Frozen grid and arms.} $\kappa \in \{0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, i.e.\ $\rho \in \{1.00, 2.72, 7.39, 54.60\}$: four rungs spanning a ${\approx}55\times$ ratio. Arms are selected by the paper's own power rule (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}), fixed before the freeze: the ladder is informative only where standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses the attack (ASR $<0.5$ at clean accuracy $\ge 0.35$), since a baseline already above threshold has no suppression left to lose and would give a flat curve by ceiling effect. Applying that rule to the measured baselines leaves exactly four $(d_2, \text{attack})$ cells, and they happen to cover three invariance classes of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}). The $\kappa{=}0$ rung is run \emph{fresh} at all five seeds for every arm rather than reused from the published baselines, so every rung of every curve comes from one code path at one seed set; the published baselines then serve as an independent per-seed cross-check of that rung (tolerance $10^{-6}$) instead of as data in it.

\textbf{The abscissa is measured, not nominal.} What matters to C2 is not $\rho$ but what the rescaling does to the specific statistic $d_2$ reads, so we measure that separately and \emph{before} freezing any prediction, pushing live FL updates through the shipped transform and recording each arm's decision at every $\kappa$ on the \emph{same} raw updates (\texttt{measure\_dose\_disturbance.py}, \texttt{results/dose\_disturbance.json}; 5 seeds $\times$ 3 rounds per attack). No ASR is computed there, which is why it can legitimately precede the freeze. It establishes three things before any outcome exists (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}): \emph{there is a dose}: disturbance is exactly $0$ at the identity rung and rises monotonically with $\kappa$ in every non-invariant arm; \emph{the class-(a) invariance claim holds in the shipped code}: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is unchanged in $\mathbf{0}$ of $120$ cells at $\rho$ up to $54.6$, its cosine \emph{scores} moving by at most $1.1\times10^{-4}$ relative, the float32 floor for a ${\approx}1.1$M-dimensional vector, which we record rather than assert on; and \emph{class (b) is graded rather than binary}, at roughly half the class-(c) disturbance ($0.487$ vs.\ $0.867$/$0.933$ at $\kappa{=}2.0$), which is what arm 4's prediction turns on.

\textbf{Frozen predictions.} The \emph{primary} test is pooled: across all 16 $(\text{arm},\kappa)$ cells, the rise in ASR over that arm's own $\kappa{=}0$ rung is predicted to be positively rank-correlated with the measured disturbance rate (Spearman, one-sided, $\alpha = 0.05$). This is the dose--response claim the design exists for, and its predictor is a \emph{measured property of the statistic}, not the identity of the defense and not its standalone effectiveness, both held fixed within every arm. The \emph{secondary} predictions are the per-arm shapes of Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}, tested by a one-sided Jonckheere--Terpstra trend test across the four ordered rungs at $n{=}5$. For arm 3, ``flat'' is scored as an \emph{equivalence} claim rather than a failed rejection: confirmed if $|\overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}2.0) - \overline{\mathrm{ASR}}(\kappa{=}0)| < 0.15$ \emph{and} every rung stays below $0.5$. That margin is fixed and justified in advance: it is under a quarter of the $+0.629$ within-defense gap for Krum on record at the freeze (the post-hoc top-up later put that gap at $+0.716$ at $n{=}5$, which only makes the margin more conservative; it is not revised), and $0.300 + 0.15 = 0.450 < 0.5$, so ``within the margin'' and ``still suppressing'' coincide by design for this arm. Arm 4's class-(b) signature is the \emph{shallower slope}, so its criterion includes its $\kappa{=}2.0$ rise being smaller than both class-(c) arms'.

\section{Dose--response caveats recorded before the freeze}
\label{app:dose_caveats}

\textbf{Caveats recorded in advance.} (i) Arms 1--2 are on model-scaling and arms 3--4 on the pixel backdoor. This is not a free choice: on model-scaling \emph{no} invariant $d_2$ suppresses at usable accuracy, that is the finding of §\ref{sec:metric_swap} (\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.078$ at $0.146$ accuracy), which is also why \texttt{cos\_krum}/scaling is not an arm. ``Same attack'' is required \emph{within} each ladder, which is where the causal claim lives; across arms we compare curve \emph{shapes and slopes}, never levels. (ii) Arm 4 has thin headroom: \texttt{coord\_median}/pixel sits only $0.057$ below threshold, so the binary above/below-$0.5$ reading is weak evidence either way and we report the continuous curve with intervals. (iii) The dose is uncorrelated with adversary status by construction, but it is not adversary-\emph{adversarial}: a dose assignment that deliberately up-weights adversaries would be a strictly harder test and is not run here. (iv) A synthetic upstream transform is not a real defense, so we test ecological validity by overlaying the two \emph{real} upstream transforms already measured on these same cells (NormClip and RFA, §\ref{sec:metric_swap}) as anchor points at their own measured disturbance; if they fall far off the synthetic curve at matched disturbance, that is a finding about the instrument and is reported as one. (v) Disturbance is measured on FedAvg trajectories on one shared set of raw updates per round, which is what makes it a clean abscissa, but means the rates in Table~\ref{tab:dose_design} are not re-measured along each arm's own 50-round trajectory.

For a selector, $\mathrm{agg}(U) = u_{s_0}$ and $\mathrm{agg}(T(U)) = c_{s_1} u_{s_1}$, with $s_0$ the client selected on the raw stack and $s_1$ the client selected on the transformed one, so exactly
\[ c_{s_1}u_{s_1} - u_{s_0} \;=\; \underbrace{(u_{s_1} - u_{s_0})}_{\text{re-selection}} \;+\; \underbrace{(c_{s_1}-1)\,u_{s_1}}_{\text{rescaling}}, \]

The re-selection component is what a magnitude-controlled dose would still move (it takes both updates \emph{untransformed}) while the rescaling component is what such a control closes. \section{Anticipated objections}
\label{app:objections}

Five objections we expect, answered against text that is already in the paper rather than by new
argument. Nothing here is a new claim; each answer is a pointer plus the scope it turns on.

\paragraph{1. This is small-scale federated learning: five clients, one architecture, mostly one
dataset.} Agreed, and every empirical magnitude is scoped accordingly, per arm, never pooled across
arms, and enumerated in Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels} with the scale and architecture evidence in
Appendix~\ref{app:scale_external}. What does \emph{not} depend on scale is the
identification argument: any criterion whose eligibility gate is defined on the same outcome it predicts
conditions on a descendant of both mechanism and effectiveness
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}), and that holds at any client count, model size or sample size.
The boundary results forbid an inference; they do not measure a system.

\paragraph{2. The interventions are oracles: no deployed system can pin an adversary's coefficient or
read adversary identity.} Correct, and that is why Mode~S is an \textbf{instrument, not a screen}. Mode~S
measures level~(P4) only by reading adversary identity, which no deployable defense can, so these
experiments \textbf{identify the causal channel and are not a (P4) screen}. Pinning $c{=}1.0$ has one
purpose, to cut the confounded path of Figure~\ref{fig:modeS}(a) by construction, exactly as an
oracle-labelled control isolates a channel in an ablation. The one control that \emph{removes} an oracle
component (score-only, which scores the transformed stack but aggregates the untransformed selected
update) does not change the verdict (§\ref{app:targeted_full}, Appendix~\ref{app:decomposition}).

\paragraph{3. $n{=}5$ seeds is small.} It is: $n{=}5$ per arm, $n{=}8$ for \texttt{cos\_krum}, with
Student-$t$ $95\%$ intervals throughout and no claim that a small effect is zero. Where suppression does
not move we report \textbf{no evidence of a practically meaningful change under the pre-specified
$\pm0.15$ equivalence margin}, a margin frozen before the runs (Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}), never a
claim that suppression is unchanged. Two design choices do more for precision than seeds would: the
identity rungs are imported \textbf{bit-identical per seed} (largest deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$),
so the contrast is within-seed rather than across noise, and the central claim is a \textbf{sign
reversal} ($-0.273$ against $+0.125$ at $n{=}20$ on the identical cell, Table~\ref{tab:comparability}), which is not
a null result. Finally, Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} says the cell that would validate the
criterion is \emph{empty by design}: more seeds cannot repair it (Table~\ref{tab:can_cannot}).

\paragraph{4. If ASR does not move, why does a change of decision matter?} Because the change of
decision is what a preservation criterion \emph{measures}, and the gap between the two is the finding.
An invariance argument certifies the top three levels of Definition~\ref{def:levels_full} while the security
content sits at level~(P4), the adversarial contribution admitted. A criterion reading the Krum arm would
call that composition broken (the decision flips in up to $80\%$ of rounds) for a composition whose
suppression shows no measurable change: a false alarm in the direction that costs coverage
(Proposition~\ref{prop:emergent_full}, recall bound $4/5$). The converse fails too: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s
selection is bit-identical at every rung yet it never suppresses ($0.584$ at the identity, Mode~S at
$n{=}8$) and its ASR \emph{falls} $0.425$ across that ladder, so exact invariance of the statistic
neither implies suppression nor holds it fixed. As in the body, the $\kappa{=}2$ endpoint carrying that
$0.425$ sits below our own clean-accuracy floor, so \textbf{the witness we rest on is the first
gate-passing rung} ($0.173$); the full-ladder figure is quoted here only for its sign
(§\ref{app:targeted_full}).

\paragraph{5. The theory covers only positive per-client rescalings.} Yes, and we say so wherever it is
used (Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}, Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight},
§\ref{sec:restricted_theorem}). The class is not arbitrary (it is what norm clipping, contribution
weighting and reputation weighting do to a client's update) but it excludes coordinate-wise filters and
any transform that changes an update's direction, and the theorem's own margin closes under heterogeneity
($m{=}3.24 \to 1.01$ at $\alpha{=}0.1$), so \textbf{it certifies nothing where heterogeneity is high}.
The negative does not rest on the theory: it is a \emph{measured} counterexample, and a narrower theory
makes the counterexample sharper rather than weaker, since \textbf{the theorem certifies mechanism
preservation, not security}; it bounds no ASR.

\section{Sensitivity of the intervention conclusions to their two frozen thresholds}
\label{app:sensitivity}

Two thresholds are frozen in this paper and a reader is entitled to ask how much rests on them.
Both are swept here by \texttt{experiments/analyze\_margin\_sensitivity.py}, which reads
\texttt{results/comparability\_six\_cells.json} and computes no ASR; as a provenance check it
reproduces that table's published verdict column exactly. \textbf{Two things about that path are
worth stating rather than leaving a reader to notice}: the name is frozen from when the table held
six cells and we do not rename it, because two pre-registrations refer to that exact path; and its
hash \emph{did} move when the seventh cell was added, so the clause in
\texttt{pre\_registration\_randomized\_composition.md} that four artifact hashes do not move is a
constraint on \emph{that} document's analysis, which never writes here, and not a global freeze.
What the hash was there to protect was checked directly instead, and the check is reported at the
precision it holds at rather than rounded up to ``unchanged'': \textbf{no value inside the six
published cells moved}, and every field the earlier file carried is still there carrying the same
number, but the records are \emph{not} byte-identical, because two provenance fields
(\texttt{missing\_frozen\_seeds} and \texttt{rung\_coverage}) were added to each of them. The
seventh cell is added alongside the six under Amendment~4 of the comparability pre-registration.
\textbf{The hash has since moved a second time, and that move \emph{did} change a published cell},
which is why the sentence above is dated rather than standing: the pre-registered endpoint top-up of
\texttt{coord\_median}/pixel (seeds $47$--$61$, both designs,
\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak pre\_registration\_reversal\_seed\_topup.md}) takes that cell to
$n{=}20$ on both legs, so its two means and both intervals move by design, and with them the single
$m^{\ast}$ this section derives from that row ($0.178 \to 0.155$, an arm that carries no equivalence
reading either before or after). The md5 is \texttt{6e25ef3a}, from \texttt{2e8cced3}. No other cell
moved, and the binding equivalence arm named below is unchanged.

\textbf{The $\pm0.15$ practical-equivalence margin is not load-bearing.} A reading of no evidence of a practically meaningful change requires the paired $95\%$ interval to lie inside
$[-m,+m]$, so the smallest margin under which each frozen reading survives is
$m^{\ast} = \max(|\mathrm{lo}|,|\mathrm{hi}|)$. For the two arms that carry an equivalence
reading this is $m^{\ast}{=}0.032$ (Krum, CIFAR-10, at that arm's $n{=}20$) and $m^{\ast}{=}0.034$ (Krum,
EMNIST-byclass, at that arm's pooled $n{=}5$), each read off Table~\ref{tab:sixcell}'s \emph{Mode-S} interval for that
arm (the equivalence reading is a statement about the controlled estimate, so $m^{\ast}$ is not
computed from the confounded column), against the frozen $0.15$: headroom of $+0.118$
and $+0.116$. \textbf{The CIFAR-100 arm is deliberately not among them, and the reason is worth
stating because arithmetic alone would include it.} Its controlled interval $[+0.031,+0.110]$ lies
inside $\pm0.15$ and so passes an equivalence test at the frozen margin while \emph{also} excluding
zero, which is a real and familiar joint outcome; but that arm is reported as a detected rise inside a
sign reversal, and no equivalence reading is made about it, so scoring it here would make this
paragraph's binding number rest on a claim the paper does not make. The margin could
have been fixed anywhere in $(0.034, 0.5)$ without changing either reading, so the pre-registered
value is a conservative choice rather than a tuned one.
\textbf{That range is this table's and not the paper's, and one arm outside the table is why.} The
\texttt{resnet18} architecture-alone arm of §\ref{app:resnet18_dose} is not scored here, because this
ladder is built over the comparability design's cells and that arm has only the controlled leg, no
outcome-gated twin to compare it with. Its own $m^{\ast}$ is $0.183$ at $n{=}3$ (paired $95\%$ interval
$[-0.183,+0.101]$), \emph{wider} than the frozen margin, so for that arm the frozen $0.15$ \textbf{is}
load-bearing: it clears TOST at $\pm0.15$ ($p{=}0.040$, the $90\%$ interval $\alpha{=}0.05$ reads) and
clears nothing tighter than $0.137$. We state it here rather than let the range above be read as a
claim about every equivalence reading in the paper, which is what the body sentence used to say.

\textbf{The $\pm0.05$ near-zero tolerance in the AGREE definition carries no verdict at all.}
Sweeping it over $[0,1]$, both endpoints included, all seven cells of Table~\ref{tab:sixcell} keep the
verdict they are published with at every value, the tolerance-free limit $\epsilon{=}0$ among them. Every cell is decided by
its two intervals, and the tolerance is a tie-break that never fires on this table.

\textbf{The central claim turns on neither.} SIGN REVERSAL is defined as opposite signs with both
intervals excluding zero, which reads the intervals alone: \texttt{coord\_median}/pixel is
$-0.273$ $[-0.334,-0.213]$ against $+0.125$ $[+0.095,+0.155]$ at $n{=}20$. No margin and no tolerance enters
that determination. \textbf{The $\delta_2$ disturbance threshold of
§\ref{sec:composability_criterion} is not swept here, and the reason is worse for us than a sweep
would be}: no label in the $42$-pair table is computed from it. \texttt{c2\_holds} decides by
invariance \emph{class}, returning True for the exactly invariant statistics and False for the
non-invariant ones, and for the three conditional classes it defers to the stored per-pair call, so
there is no numeric $\delta_2$ anywhere in that column whose value could be varied. Its provenance
is censused in main §\ref{app:frontier} ($3$ of the $14$ admissions rest on algebra and $11$ on
that call), and we state that rather than report a stability interval the code does not support.

\section{Screening for a further downstream defense in the comparability table, and why we do not add one}
\label{app:cell7}

\textbf{Which axis this section is about, since the table can grow along two.} A cell is a
(downstream defense, attack, dataset) triple, so a new cell can add a \emph{defense} the table does not
yet contain or move an existing defense to a new \emph{dataset}. This section is about the first axis
only, and it declines. The second axis is pre-registered separately, in Amendment~4 of
\texttt{experiments/\allowbreak pre\_registration\_comparability.md}, which fixes a cell holding
\texttt{coord\_median} and the pixel backdoor unchanged and varying the dataset; that cell is the
CIFAR-100 row of main~§\ref{app:sixcell}, it adds no defense, and nothing below bears on it. Read the
argument here as about defenses, never as a claim that the table is closed at seven cells.

Table~\ref{tab:sixcell} rests on four downstream defenses, and a reader may ask why not more. The
contrast is interpretable only where the downstream defense suppresses the attack on its own,
since otherwise there is no suppression whose attribution could move, so a further cell needs a
defense meeting C1's own precondition: standalone ASR below $0.5$ at clean accuracy at least
$0.35$. One axis had that unmeasured, and this section measures it.

EMNIST-byclass under model scaling is that axis. \texttt{results/femnist/payoff\_results.json} already
carries a standalone scaling cell for seven of the twelve aggregation rules in this study, and
\texttt{krum} is the only powered one, which is cell~6: \texttt{trimmed\_mean} reaches $0.9999$,
\texttt{rfa} $0.9990$, \texttt{norm\_clip} $0.9986$, \texttt{coord\_median} $0.6567$, and
\texttt{fedavg} and \texttt{multi\_krum} both $0.3333$ at $0.041$ clean accuracy. The remaining
five had no cell at all: we measured four of them at $n{=}3$ under the identical protocol and
runner (\texttt{experiments/measure\_femnist\_c1\_inputs\_scaling.py},
\texttt{results/femnist\_c1\_inputs\_scaling/}) and exclude \texttt{fltrust} by the argument at the
end of this section, so all twelve are accounted for.

\textbf{No defense outside the table's four is admissible.} \texttt{foolsgold} and
\texttt{cos\_reputation} are the only two screened candidates that would contribute a downstream
defense the comparability axis does not already have, and both fail on the \emph{accuracy} floor
rather than the ASR threshold: a $10\times$ update rescaling destroys the model under them, to
$0.046$ and $0.100$ clean accuracy on a 62-class task. \texttt{foolsgold}'s third seed is the
clean illustration of why the precondition gates on accuracy at all, ASR $0.0000$ at $0.044$
accuracy: a model that has learned nothing suppresses every attack, and reading that as
suppression is the error the floor exists to prevent.

\textbf{\texttt{reputation} meets the precondition and we still decline the cell.} Its mean is
$0.3465$ at $0.704$ accuracy, inside the rule as written. Its three seeds are $0.9947$, $0.0152$
and $0.0296$: one total failure beside two near-total suppressions, and the mean resembles
neither. Because \texttt{reputation} is already one of the table's four defenses, such a cell
would be a second-dataset replication rather than a new defense, and we are not willing to rest a
replication on an input whose spread is this wide at $n{=}3$. We report the screen and leave the
cell unrun, and note that what would settle it is more seeds on the C1 \emph{input}, not a
ladder built on this one.

\textbf{The screen is gated on reproducing a frozen cell.} These runs measure the scaling arm
under the same runner as the frozen pixel-arm cells the main text reports for this dataset, and the
two arms share their data poisoning, differing only in the update-level rescaling. So the screen
first re-measures \texttt{krum}/scaling and refuses to run any target unless it reproduces the
frozen value: it returns $0.0239$ at $0.765$ accuracy against the frozen $0.0270$, where the
corresponding pixel cell is $0.660$. That gate distinguishes the arm under test from its pixel
twin, and it makes these four cells protocol-comparable with the frozen matrix rather than
merely indicative.

\textbf{These are C1 inputs, not predictions.} C1 is defined on standalone suppression, so the
numbers above are the criterion's inputs and no prediction of any kind is tested here; the
convention that such inputs are measured before any per-pair prediction is frozen is
\texttt{experiments/pre\_registration\_prospective.md}. No composition is evaluated, and no
pre-registration is spent. \texttt{fltrust} is excluded by argument rather than measurement:
the main text records it as bit-identical per seed under positive
per-client rescaling, which is exactly the Mode~S dose, so a ladder on it would observe an
algebraic identity.
