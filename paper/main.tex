\begin{abstract}
Federated learning composes defenses never designed together, so upstream processing can destroy the \emph{statistic} the downstream defense reads; the natural repair is to require that statistic to be \textbf{preserved}. \textbf{A statistic can be preserved while attack suppression is not, and an outcome-gated comparison cannot identify whether the downstream mechanism caused the suppression it observes.} We establish this where invariance is settled algebraically, for \emph{positive per-client rescalings}. The obstruction is structural: a criterion gated on the outcome it predicts conditions on a descendant of both the mechanism and the defense's standalone strength, so \textbf{no screen of this form identifies the mechanism's contribution, at any sample size}. Across six cells the two designs disagree on four. Ignoring this does not cost power, it costs the sign: on the identical cell the outcome-gated ladder moves $-0.272$ ($95\%$ CI $[-0.422,-0.122]$) where the instrument moves $+0.098$ ($[+0.018,+0.178]$), intervals that exclude zero and do not overlap. Preservation is a hierarchy; we break its last two links by intervening, not comparing, with an oracle that pins every adversary's coefficient. Losing decision invariance costs no suppression: disturbing Krum's statistic flips its decision in up to $80\%$ of rounds while the \emph{support} of admitted adversarial mass never changes in any measured round of any arm, suppression staying inside our pre-specified $\pm0.15$ margin. Holding decision \emph{and} adversarial-contribution invariance does not deliver it: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is bit-identical at every rung while ASR falls $0.173$. We prove sufficient conditions for the weighting$\to$aggregator class, certifying \textbf{mechanism preservation, not security}, and bounding no ASR. A \emph{Coverage} limit follows: suppression emergent in a composition cannot satisfy an inherited-suppression gate, capping recall structurally. We replicate the negative in a second invariance class, on a second dataset and architecture, and in a second \emph{transformation} class outside the rescaling family the theory covers. A prospective suite on three never-used defenses, labels committed before any run, is reported with the confound that limits it: FLTrust suppresses both attacks alone. The implication is methodological: intervene on adversarial contribution, do not infer it from downstream statistics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Federated learning~\citep{mcmahan2017communication} is vulnerable to data poisoning~\citep{chen2017targeted,bagdasaryan2020backdoor}, and an arms race has produced defenses~\citep{fung2020foolsgold,pillutla2022robust,yin2018byzantine,blanchard2017machine} with accuracy/robustness trade-offs and none universally dominant (Appendix~\ref{app:related}). \emph{Can we compose them for stronger robustness?} Composition does not even commute: FoolsGold$\to$CoordMedian suppresses a pixel backdoor to ASR $0.076$ while the reverse order reaches $0.732$: a rank aggregator emits one vector rather than per-client updates, so it cannot be an \emph{upstream} stage and the pair collapses to FoolsGold alone, bit-identically. Naive layering fails this way whenever upstream processing destroys what the downstream defense reads, and it bites hardest in decentralized pipelines with no single owner.

\textbf{Why the question is sharper without a trusted coordinator.} Such stacks compose independently developed mechanisms among heterogeneous, partially trusted, often pseudonymous participants, so preservation is the obvious discipline to demand of them. Concretely: a contributor clips its own update, a scheduler reweights contributors by reputation, and whoever merges runs a robust aggregator: three parties, three mechanisms, nobody owning the stack. The only check available at each seam is that the next stage still reads what it expects, and a preserved statistic invites the reading that the stack is therefore still robust. \textbf{That inference is what this paper refutes.} \textbf{The identification problem we isolate is defined independently of model scale}, so we study it in a controlled federated setting and claim no transfer beyond it.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} We propose no new defense. \textbf{Our contribution is a causal-identification result about how defense compositions are evaluated}, the design it forces, and one experiment that separates the channels it names. \emph{Attack-suppression preservation under a fixed committed attack} is the object throughout: suppression of that attack surviving composition, instantiated as ASR, never the statistic or the decision that follows. We claim nothing about confidentiality, availability, adaptive adversaries, or robustness beyond that suppression; Fig.~\ref{fig:modeS} is the paper in one picture.

\noindent\textbf{(1) The identification result (§\ref{sec:testability}).} A screen that keeps only pairs whose constituents already suppress the attack, then asks whether the composition preserves it, gates on a descendant of the quantity it is trying to estimate. \textbf{The consequence is non-identification, not bias}: one cell of the relevant $2\times2$ is empty for \emph{every} defense menu, so the estimand has no support and \textbf{no adjustment set, no reweighting and no sample size recovers it}. That is what separates this from ordinary collider bias, which is a bias, and correctable in principle given the right adjustment.

\noindent\textbf{(2) A second, independent limit on the same design (Prop.~\ref{prop:emergent}).} Even where such a screen is admissible it cannot \emph{find} what it is for: suppression that is emergent, present in the composition and absent from both constituents, fails the gate by definition, capping recall at $1-|\mathcal{E}|/|\mathcal{S}|$ on every menu and every attack. Our own lowest-ASR composition is the witness. This is a coverage statement about a class of screens and does not follow from the collider argument.

\noindent\textbf{(3) The boundary is constructive (§\ref{sec:protocol}).} Each clause of the evaluation protocol is forced by a term in the proposition, so the result arrives with the design that respects it rather than only a prohibition.

\noindent\textbf{(4) The demonstration is a sign inversion, not attenuation (§\ref{sec:targeted}).} On an identical cell the outcome-gated design estimates $-0.272$ where the intervention estimates $+0.098$. Collider bias is usually taught as attenuation; here the confounded design does not lose power, it \textbf{reverses the direction} of the measured effect, inside a live security-evaluation methodology.

\noindent\textbf{Also.} The preservation hierarchy (Def.~\ref{def:levels}); mechanism preservation as invariance under positive per-client rescalings (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}), with margins on the weighting$\to$aggregator class (Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}) that \textbf{certify mechanism preservation and bound no ASR}; counterexamples across four aggregators of three structural kinds; the negative replicated in a second invariance class and on a second dataset and architecture; and, reported as a refutation, the collapse of the cross-arm admission ordering we had ourselves predicted. \emph{Roadmap:} §\ref{sec:framework} names the preservation levels, §\ref{sec:testability} proves the boundary, §\ref{sec:protocol} states the protocol it forces, §\ref{sec:targeted} intervenes, and §\ref{sec:validation} replicates both broken links and asks what a screen then buys.

\noindent\textbf{What is general here, and what is a federated-learning instantiation.} The paper makes three kinds of claim and never pools them. \textbf{General:} Prop.~\ref{prop:identification} and Prop.~\ref{prop:emergent} are statements about the \emph{shape of a screen}, proved with no dataset, architecture or scale in them, so what carries beyond this paper is the design they license and \emph{not} our screen. \textbf{Mechanism-specific:} Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} and the invariance classes (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}) are proved for \emph{positive per-client rescalings} into named rank and geometric aggregators, a laboratory clean enough to settle invariance algebraically rather than a model of FL defenses in general, and they \textbf{bound no ASR}. \textbf{Empirical:} four aggregators of three structural kinds under two committed attacks, on CIFAR-10 with one arm replicated on EMNIST-byclass, at $n{=}5$ seeds ($n{=}8$ for \texttt{cos\_krum}): ``replicated on a second dataset and architecture'', never ``generalizes''. \textbf{The middle tier is both the one an FL reader will recognise and the narrowest of the three}, and that is deliberate: a broken implication has to be exhibited where invariance is decidable rather than argued. Claims do not transfer upward. Scope conditions are stated once, in Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}; evidence tiers in Table~\ref{tab:tiers}; the limits of the whole in §\ref{sec:limitations}, (L1)--(L6), so \textbf{no claim is hedged twice and none nowhere}.

\section{Mechanism Preservation: Invariance Classes}
\label{sec:framework}

\emph{What does it mean for a composition to preserve a defense?} Every per-client defense we consider acts as a positive scalar rescaling $T(u_i) = c_i u_i$, which gives the invariance question a sharp \emph{algebraic} answer (Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}), but writing preservation as an invariance of the statistic $S$ leaves a gap our own experiments exposed. Three different things can be preserved: what the downstream defense \emph{reads} (P1--P2), what it \emph{decides} and what \emph{survives} it (P3--P4), and whether the attack is still \emph{suppressed} (P5). They come apart, so we name the levels and ask of each whether it determines the one below:

\begin{center}\small\vspace{-7pt}\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Level & What is preserved & Determines the level below? \\
\midrule
(P1) & the statistic's value & yes, by definition \\
(P2) & the ordering it induces & yes, by definition \\
(P3) & the decision $d_2$ makes & \textbf{no}, by counterexample (§\ref{sec:targeted}) \\
\midrule
(P4) & the adversarial contribution admitted & \textbf{no}, by counterexample (§\ref{sec:targeted_rest}) \\
(P5) & suppression of the committed attack & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{-5pt}\end{center}

\noindent \textbf{The rule in that table is this paper's subject}: above it the implications are logical and need no experiment, below it they are causal and are settled only by intervening, there being no shared quantity to compare along. \textbf{Both breaks are measured}, at fixed $d_2$ and attack: Krum's decision flips in up to $80\%$ of rounds with the adversarial mass it admits unchanged (§\ref{sec:targeted}), and \texttt{cos\_krum}'s decision and admitted mass are bit-identical while ASR falls $0.173$ (§\ref{sec:targeted_rest}); Fig.~\ref{fig:story} draws the chain and both breaks.

The algebra fixes which level a given composition sits at: cosine similarity is exactly invariant, so (P2) holds for every $\cdot \to$ FoolsGold composition; coordinate ordering survives only under a bounded-ratio condition; consensus distance and residual magnitude are not invariant at all, so $\cdot \to$ Reputation compositions fail (P2); norm clipping does not blind FoolsGold, though it does blind Reputation.

\begin{definition}[Preservation levels]
\label{def:levels}
For an upstream transform $T$ and a downstream defense $d_2$ reading statistic $S$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,label=(P\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Value invariance:} $S(T(U)) = S(U)$.
\item \textbf{Ordering invariance:} $S(T(U)) = \varphi(S(U))$ for some $\varphi$ preserving the ordering $S$ induces on clients.
\item \textbf{Decision invariance:} the client or coordinate $d_2$ actually selects is unchanged.
\item \textbf{Adversarial contribution (admission) invariance:} the adversarial input $d_2$ admits into its aggregate is unchanged. \emph{Admission} is the pre-registered term throughout for how much adversarial input survives $d_2$.
\item \textbf{Attack-suppression preservation:} ASR stays below threshold, for the fixed committed attack the arm is run against.
\end{enumerate}
We target (P3)$\not\Rightarrow$(P4) in §\ref{sec:targeted} and (P4)$\not\Rightarrow$(P5) in §\ref{sec:targeted_rest}. The mechanism conditions the levels induce are in App.~\ref{app:framework} and~\ref{app:conditions}.
\end{definition}

\noindent \textbf{(P4) is a level, not one measurable quantity, and we do not claim it is one.} Each rule admits adversarial input through whatever its own machinery exposes: Krum's and \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selected-client indicator, \texttt{coord\_median}'s selected-coordinate share, reputation's adversarial weight share. So \textbf{our (P4) result is four within-family statements, not one cross-defense quantity}: in each family, that family's own admission instrument moves (or provably cannot move) without suppression following. The instruments are not commensurable across families and we never difference or rank across them; that is also why App.~\ref{app:channel_table}'s influence column is aggregator-specific and why Table~\ref{tab:channels} \textbf{does not order the arms}. A single cross-defense admission quantity would be a stronger claim and we do not have one.

\paragraph{What the theory delivers: supporting machinery, not the claim.} For a per-client \emph{weighting} into a \emph{rank-based} or \emph{geometric} aggregator, Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} (App.~\ref{app:proofs}) gives sufficient conditions for $d_2$'s ordering, levels (P1)--(P3), to survive: for \textbf{rank aggregators}, a count majority plus a \emph{signed} coordinate-wise ordering with $\rho < r^\star$ against the separation margin, \textbf{magnitude alone not sufficing} without the common-sign assumption; for \textbf{geometric median / RFA}, a separation condition $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} > 2(R'_B + \delta')$. \textbf{Norm-equalizing $d_1$} (NormClip with $\tau$ below every client norm) is not a bounded reweighting at all: its induced ratio is unbounded, any $\sigma_2$ keyed on update norm or consensus distance goes constant across clients, and \textbf{(P2) fails}. The theorem \textbf{certifies mechanism preservation and bounds no ASR}, constraining the \emph{aggregate} adversarial coefficient only through Cor.~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass}: it is not a sufficient condition for suppression and is not the paper's claim. Two facts about it matter downstream and are why $\rho$ alone mispredicts: the conditions are safe at \emph{both} extremes of the weight ratio, and \textbf{the margin is what heterogeneity closes}, RFA's collapsing from $3.24$ to $1.01$ at Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.1$, the limit at which the bound goes vacuous (L5).


\section{The Testability Boundary}
\label{sec:testability}

\emph{Why can we not simply compare many defense pairs?} The natural test keeps pairs whose constituents already suppress the attack and asks whether the composition preserves it. That is exactly where identification is lost, and it is this paper's central claim, so we state it rather than pointing at it.

\begin{proposition}[Identification: constituent-effect contrasts cannot identify composition effects under inherited-effect gating]
\label{prop:identification}
Let a screen certify $(d_1 \to d_2)$ against attack $a$ only if both
(i) an \textbf{eligibility gate} $E(d_1,d_2,a) := \min_{d \in \{d_1,d_2\}} M(d,a) < \theta$ holds, where $M$ and $\theta$ are the \emph{same} outcome metric and threshold the screen predicts for the composition, and
(ii) a \textbf{mechanism condition} $P(d_1,d_2)$ holds, determined by the two defenses' definitions alone.
Suppose the screen is applied only where the downstream mechanism is load-bearing, $M(d_1,a) \ge \theta$. Consider estimating $P$'s contribution to $M(d_1 \to d_2, a)$ from contrasts across a defense menu $\mathcal{D}$. Then every certified pair satisfies $M(d_2,a) < \theta$, so of the four cells of $\{P,\neg P\} \times \{M(d_2,a) < \theta,\ M(d_2,a) \ge \theta\}$ the certified set occupies at most two, and \textbf{the cell needed to separate $P$ from $d_2$'s standalone effectiveness is empty by the gate, for every $\mathcal{D}$}. No contrast varying $d_2$ identifies $P$'s contribution. Identification requires holding $d_2$, $a$ and therefore $E$ fixed while intervening on the upstream transform: \textbf{the admissible designs are within-defense.}
\end{proposition}

\noindent \textbf{Nothing in this statement is about federated learning.} $M$ is any outcome metric, $\theta$ any threshold, $P$ any condition on the two mechanisms' definitions, and $\mathcal{D}$ any menu; the proof uses no property of gradients, backdoors, robust aggregation or our defense set. \textbf{It is a result about a \emph{shape} of evaluation rather than about a domain}: any procedure that admits candidate compositions on the strength of a constituent, and then reads the composition's outcome to infer whether the other mechanism survived, has emptied the cell that would identify it. \textbf{Federated-learning defense composition is the instance we can measure}, and the rest of this paper measures it.

\noindent The proof is three lines and is in App.~\ref{app:boundary}: the load-bearing condition excludes $d_1$ from attaining the minimum in (i), so certification forces $M(d_2,a) < \theta$ and both cells with $M(d_2,a) \ge \theta$ go unpopulated whatever $\mathcal{D}$ is. Four cells follow, and that design never fills two of them:

\begin{center}\small\vspace{-7pt}\setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Mechanism condition & $d_2$ suppresses $a$ alone & it does not \\
\midrule
$P$ holds (preserved) & observed: every certified pair & \textbf{empty by the gate} \\
$\neg P$ (destroyed) & observed: predicted-FAIL pairs & \textbf{empty by the gate} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\vspace{-5pt}\end{center}

\noindent Reading down a column is the contrast that would identify $P$'s contribution, and \textbf{the only column in which $d_2$ is not already doing the work is empty}, for every menu a screen of this form could range over: excluded by design, not undersampled: more data cannot fill a cell the design never permits. The gate cannot be dropped either, because without it the screen's inherited-suppression premise is vacuous: it would certify pairs neither of whose constituents suppresses the attack. So wherever our conditions certify a pair, a low composition ASR is attributable to $d_2$'s standalone strength rather than to preservation. Equivalently, this is selection on a collider~\citep{elwert2014endogenous,pearl2009causality}: the gate is a descendant of both $P$ and $d_2$'s standalone effectiveness, so conditioning on it makes them co-vary. The hazard is standard; the gate reading as hygiene rather than as the step that removes identification is not, and we made that mistake ourselves (§\ref{sec:dose_response}).

\noindent Read forwards the boundary is \emph{constructive}, and §\ref{sec:protocol} is what it leaves. A second boundary bounds not what such a screen can be \emph{validated} on but what it can \emph{find}.

\begin{proposition}[Coverage: inherited-suppression screens cannot certify emergent synergy]
\label{prop:emergent}
Call $(d_1 \to d_2)$'s suppression of $a$ \textbf{emergent} if $M(d_1 \to d_2, a) < \theta$ while $\min_{d \in \{d_1,d_2\}} M(d,a) \ge \theta$. No emergent composition satisfies the eligibility gate of Prop.~\ref{prop:identification}. Hence, writing $\mathcal{S}$ for the compositions with $M < \theta$ and $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ for the emergent ones, any such screen's recall obeys $\mathrm{recall} \le 1 - |\mathcal{E}|/|\mathcal{S}|$, on every menu and every attack.
\end{proposition}

\noindent \textbf{Our own lowest-ASR composition is the witness}, which makes this a scope result rather than a defect found by tuning: FG$\to$RFA reaches max-committed ASR $0.093$ ($n{=}30$, CI $[0.062,\,0.123]$) where FoolsGold alone reaches $0.732$ and RFA alone $0.845$, so it is emergent, it is a false negative of our own criterion, and the bound binds at $|\mathcal{E}|/|\mathcal{S}| = 1/5$: exactly the $40\%$ recall we report. The result is specific neither to our screen nor to FL: any screen requiring a constituent to suppress the attack independently inherits it. Screening for \emph{inherited} robustness and searching for synergistic compositions are different problems, and only the first is ours (App.~\ref{app:prioritization}). \textbf{So the proposition is a characterization, not a scoreboard}: it partitions any menu into the compositions an inherited-suppression screen can \emph{certify} and the compositions such a screen renders \emph{undiscoverable}, and it puts the paper's own best composition in the second class \emph{a priori} rather than by measurement. A screen reaching $\mathcal{E}$ would have to read the composition's own suppression, the outcome it exists to avoid conditioning on: Prop.~\ref{prop:identification}'s boundary, met from the other side. \textbf{So a menu holds two kinds of good composition and a screen of this form sees only one}: \emph{inheritance}, where a constituent already suppresses and the composition keeps it, and \emph{emergence}, where neither does and the pair does. \textbf{Our blind spot is one condition}: every C1-bearing variant misses FG$\to$RFA and every C1-free variant reaches it, so $40\%$ recall is an \emph{operating point} on a measured frontier rather than a ceiling (App.~\ref{app:frontier}).

\section{A Causal Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\emph{If the comparison is inadmissible, what replaces it?} Not a better criterion but a different design, and Prop.~\ref{prop:identification} determines it: \textbf{do not select across defenses; hold the downstream defense and the attack fixed, intervene on the upstream transformation, and measure the causal channels separately.} Each clause is forced by a term in the proposition rather than chosen for convenience.

\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Hold $d_2$ and the attack fixed.} \emph{Forced by} the gate $E(d_1,d_2,a)$: vary $d_2$ or $a$ and the two sides of a contrast are conditioned on different events, so they are not comparable whatever the sample size.
\item \textbf{Intervene on the upstream transform; do not select a different $d_2$.} \emph{Forced by} the empty cell: variation in $d_2$ is exactly what the gate turns into missing support, so the intervention has to live on $d_1$.
\item \textbf{Pin the adversarial coefficient channel.} \emph{Forced by} what the proposition does \emph{not} constrain: neither the gate nor the mechanism condition prevents $T$ from reaching the outcome without passing through $d_2$'s statistic at all, by simply attenuating whichever client carries the poison (Lem.~\ref{lem:annihilation}). That open path is the confound that inverted our own sign.
\item \textbf{Measure the channels, not only the outcome.} \emph{Forced by} what $P$ asserts: it is a claim about a mechanism, so a contrast reporting only ASR cannot say which channel carried the effect, and every channel the intervention opens has to be reported, including the ones it opened by accident.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent \textbf{Operationally, for any composition claim.} (i) Name the downstream mechanism and its invariance class. (ii) State \emph{which} preservation level of Def.~\ref{def:levels} is being claimed; a claim that does not name its level is not checkable. (iii) Check the upstream transform lies in the class the argument actually covers: one that alters participation, carries cross-round state, or mixes clients across each other is covered by no invariance argument we know of, ours included. (iv) Hold $d_2$, the attack and therefore the gate's input fixed, and \textbf{do not select candidates on the outcome}. (v) Intervene on the transform and measure the channels. (vi) Report the level at which the chain broke. Steps (iv)--(v) are what Prop.~\ref{prop:identification} licenses, and following them turned our own $-0.272$ into $+0.098$ on an identical cell (§\ref{sec:targeted}).

\paragraph{The protocol's boundary, measured rather than asserted.} Every clause above holds the attack fixed. So what the protocol establishes is \textbf{attack-suppression preservation under a fixed committed attack}, and nothing wider: an adversary that reoptimizes against the composed pipeline is outside it, which is why we do not call the object ``security''. That boundary is measured. Under \emph{one} whitebox composition-aware \emph{adaptive} stress test, which solves the joint FoolsGold/RFA constraint knowing both stages, FG$\to$RFA rises to ASR $0.167 \pm 0.069$, $3.3\times$ its seed-matched committed $0.051$: \textbf{inherited robustness is not adaptive robustness.} That is one adaptive attacker against one composition and we generalize nothing from it; a protocol stated against an adaptive adversary is the sharpest thing this framework lacks (App.~\ref{app:secondary}).

\section{Statistic Preservation Does Not Identify Attack-Suppression Preservation}
\label{sec:targeted}

\emph{Does disturbing what a defense reads actually cost suppression?} \textbf{The claim: it need not, and the design that says otherwise gets the sign backwards.} Our pre-registered pooled hypothesis (H1), that ASR rises with disturbance of the statistic $d_2$ reads, is rejected, the effect \emph{negative}: that ladder moved adversarial influence and the statistic together (§\ref{sec:dose_response}). We adjudicate the implication that losing (P3) means losing (P5) by oracle intervention on two arms in two invariance classes, not by a general law.

\paragraph{The instrument, as a counterfactual.} The clean question asks what happens when the downstream statistic changes but the adversary cannot become weaker. \textbf{Mode S} enforces exactly that: it pins every adversary's coefficient at $c{=}1.0$ and spreads only the benign clients over $\rho = e^{2\kappa}$, holding the adversarial coefficient share constant at $0.266667$ to within float32 read-back noise, closing Figure~\ref{fig:modeS}(a)'s attenuation path, so a rise in ASR here cannot be attenuation. Mode~S and Mode~A (§\ref{sec:validation}) are causal instruments built from adversary identity: they measure mechanism-level effects rather than emulate deployable defenses (Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}, App.~\ref{app:targeted_full}).

\paragraph{Two readings of preservation, frozen against each other.} \emph{The statistic reading}: what matters is disturbance of $d_2$'s statistic, read off its \emph{decisions}. \emph{The admission reading}: what matters is the \emph{adversarial input $d_2$ admits}. Mode~S disturbs Krum's selection heavily while leaving the mass it admits untouched, so that arm adjudicates (Table~\ref{tab:targeted}): as $\rho$ climbs to $54.6$ the decision changes in up to $0.80$ of rounds, the adversarial mass admitted in none, and ASR shows no evidence of the predicted increase, staying inside the pre-specified margin ($p_{\uparrow}{=}0.70$, $|\Delta|{=}0.026<0.15$). \textbf{Here equivalence is established, not merely consistent}: TOST against that frozen margin gives $p{=}0.0014$ at $n{=}5$, and a pre-registered top-up to $n{=}20$, under a clause committing us to report a refutation \emph{in the abstract}, confirms it at $\Delta=-0.010$, $[-0.032,+0.012]$, half-width $14.7\%$ of the margin. App.~\ref{app:tost} scores every arm this way, \textbf{including the one where the frozen point rule passes and TOST does not}. \textbf{The statistic reading is refuted}: statistic and decision disturbance need not change suppression once the adversarial coefficient share is held fixed. \emph{The zero is a floor here}: Krum admits no adversary at any rung, so \textbf{unchanged admission is not by itself decisive} and the dissociation is read across all four aggregators (Table~\ref{tab:channels}). \textbf{We claim no per-channel decomposition of $\Delta$ASR anywhere}: a pre-registered $2\times2$ factorial does not establish that these channels decompose the \emph{suppression} effect, and at $n{=}20$ it withdraws the interaction reading we published at $n{=}5$ (\S~\ref{app:factorial}). The $\pm0.15$ margin's provenance and the rule conjoining it with \emph{every rung} $<0.5$ are in App.~\ref{app:tost}.

\section{What the Boundary Licenses, and What We Found}
\label{sec:validation}

\emph{Does the pattern hold beyond the adjudicating arm, and what does a screen then buy?} We validate on CIFAR-10 with a 4-layer CNN, $N{=}10$ clients, $f{=}0.2$ adversarial, two committed attacks (model-scaling, pixel backdoor), at $n{=}5$ seeds per cell ($n{=}8$ for \texttt{cos\_krum}, $n{=}3$ on EMNIST-byclass); \emph{ASR} means \emph{max-committed ASR}; the rest of the protocol is fixed in the supplement (\S~\ref{app:details}). The core evidence is a seed-matched intervention on one cell, not a mean comparison across cells: what §\ref{sec:testability} excludes is a cell, not a sample size.

\subsection{The confounded ladder that motivated the instrument, and the sign it got wrong}
\label{sec:dose_response}
Our earlier suites tried to validate ordering preservation by \emph{choosing a different downstream defense}, the move Prop.~\ref{prop:identification} rules out. Varying only the \emph{dose} at fixed $d_2$ and attack ($\kappa{=}0$ bit-identical to $d_2$ standalone) refutes that suite's pooled prediction that disturbance predicts each arm's rise: $\rho_s{=}+0.351$, one-sided $p{=}0.091$ against a frozen $p<0.05$, and in three of four arms ASR \emph{falls} as the dose rises ($80$ runs, one arm confirming decisively; Table~\ref{tab:dose_outcome}). \textbf{The fault is ours and is now measured}: fixing $\mathrm{mean}(c){=}1$ leaves relative weights free, so a large spread mostly \emph{dilutes} whichever client carries the poison: attenuation, not statistic disturbance (supplement \S~\ref{app:dose_caveats}, \S~\ref{app:delta_c}).

\subsection{What the instrument does to that ladder, across four aggregators and a second dataset}
\label{sec:targeted_rest}
Most of the one confirmation was attenuation: with the adversary's share pinned, reputation's rise at $\rho{=}54.6$ shrinks from $+0.762$ to $+0.178$, and what remains tracks a small \emph{admission} change, not the large decision change (App.~\ref{app:targeted_full}). The class-(a) control refutes us the other way: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is bit-identical at every rung yet ASR is $0.584$ at the identity (it never suppresses) and \emph{falls} $0.173$ at the first rung alone, where clean accuracy moves only $0.599 \to 0.567$ and our accuracy gate passes: that first accuracy-valid rung is a \textbf{sign-consistent witness} of a suppression change under bit-identical selection and admission (one-sided $p{=}0.044$, $5/8$ seeds, \emph{supportive} rather than what the identification claim rests on, which is the bit-identical antecedent), and it is \textbf{the witness we rest on} rather than the larger fall at rungs below the accuracy floor (§\ref{sec:targeted}, Table~\ref{tab:channels}). \textbf{Mode A} (payload share at fixed dispersion) separates Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}'s two regimes: confirmed for \texttt{coord\_median}, refuted for reputation. A \emph{pre-registered} replication on EMNIST-byclass/\texttt{simple\_cnn} moves the decision on $0.643$--$0.714$ of rounds with the adversary never admitted, suppression inside the pre-specified $\pm0.15$ equivalence margin: $\Delta=-0.017$ beside CIFAR-10's $-0.026$, at accuracy $0.767\to0.835$: \textbf{replicated} on a second dataset and architecture, never ``generalizes'' (App.~\ref{app:channel_table}, \ref{app:validation_full}). \textbf{And it replicates outside the rescaling family altogether.} \textbf{Mode~M} masks a random $0.8$ fraction of each \emph{benign} client's coordinates at fixed $L_2$ norm, leaving adversarial updates \emph{exactly} untouched. A mask is not of the form $c_i u_i$, so Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} and Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} \textbf{make no prediction about it}, and the branch that would have refuted us was frozen before any ASR existed: masking that made Krum admit the adversary would confirm the \emph{admission} reading instead. It does not. The decision changes in $0.467$ of rounds at admitted adversarial mass $0.000$, and suppression does not follow: $\Delta=-0.016$, $[-0.051,+0.019]$ ($n{=}10$, TOST $p{=}5{\times}10^{-6}$). \textbf{One mask is not the class of all non-rescalings} (App.~\ref{app:channel_table}).

\paragraph{And what a screen then buys: less than one number suggests.} The conditions were developed on the same menu they are scored on, so the only sub-tier that risks falsification is a \emph{prospective} one: three never-used defenses, labels and mechanisms git-committed (\texttt{262cf35}) for all $20$ pairs before any run existed. It scores $13/20$, and \textbf{we do not offer that as validation}, for a reason that is a property of our menu rather than of the screen: FLTrust suppresses both committed attacks on its own, so the $13$ pairs containing it are inheritances or near-identities for which Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}(a) permits no other outcome, and \textbf{that scope condition we added retrospectively rather than pre-registering it}. Only the FLTrust-free pairs could have surprised us and none is predicted-LOW, so they bear on specificity alone. \textbf{It is our only prospective evidence, and its score and limiting flaw are reported together} (§\ref{sec:prospective}, Table~\ref{tab:validation}, App.~\ref{app:validation_full}).

\section{Conclusion}

\textbf{The finding.} \textbf{Preserving a defense statistic is not sufficient to identify preserved attack suppression.} An intervention changes a defense's statistic and decision substantially without changing the adversarial mass admitted or the suppression that follows; conversely a bit-identical statistic and decision coexist with a change in suppression. Each direction has a measured witness.

\textbf{Why.} An outcome-gated cross-defense comparison conditions on the outcome it seeks to explain, so it cannot separate mechanism preservation from standalone effectiveness: a structural boundary (Prop.~\ref{prop:identification}) that no additional data within that design removes. Within-defense intervention fixes $d_2$ and the attack instead, and on the identical cell it reverses the sign.

\textbf{The implication is an evaluation principle, not a metric.} \textbf{To evaluate a composed defense causally, manipulate or measure the adversarial contribution channel rather than using preservation of the statistic that feeds it as a proxy for suppression.} That channel is (P4), and Mode~S reads it only by knowing adversary identity, so we offer a design for identifying it, not a quantity a deployment could screen on (App.~\ref{app:conclusion}). The open question: \textbf{can (P4) be estimated without knowing which clients are malicious?}

\section{Preliminaries and the preservation levels}
\label{sec:prelim}
\label{app:framework}

\textbf{Federated learning.} $N$ clients each hold a local dataset $\mathcal{D}_i \sim P_i$; the server learns $w^* = \arg\min_w \sum_i \frac{|\mathcal{D}_i|}{|\mathcal{D}|} F_i(w)$. In FedAvg~\citep{mcmahan2017communication}, each round the server broadcasts $w^t$, selected clients run local SGD, and the server aggregates $w^{t+1} = w^t + \frac{1}{|S_t|}\sum_{i \in S_t} \Delta_i^t$.

\textbf{Poisoning and robust aggregation.} An adversary controlling a fraction $f$ of clients can flip labels~\citep{chen2017targeted}, inject backdoor triggers~\citep{bagdasaryan2020backdoor,xie2019dba}, or scale malicious updates to dominate aggregation~\citep{bagdasaryan2020backdoor}. The server may replace FedAvg with a Byzantine-robust rule: Krum~\citep{blanchard2017machine}, Trimmed Mean, Coordinate-wise Median, Norm Clipping~\citep{sun2019can}, or RFA (geometric median)~\citep{pillutla2022robust}, each imposing an accuracy/robustness trade-off.

\textbf{Game theory.} A normal-form game is $G = (\mathcal{N}, \{\mathcal{S}_i\}, \{u_i\})$; an equilibrium is \emph{pure} when every player's strategy puts all its mass on one action and \emph{mixed} otherwise. \textbf{No claim of this paper is game-theoretic}: this is needed only to read the residual scale check of Appendix~\ref{app:scale}, and the type-space and Bayes-Nash machinery an earlier version used is not needed here.

\section{The screen, the identification boundary, and the proofs}
\label{sec:composability_criterion}
\label{app:conditions}

The paper's central practical question is \emph{which} defense pairs compose well under deterministic joint application. Specifically, the criterion screens for \emph{mechanism-preserving composition}: when one constituent defense is already individually effective (C1), can the other defense be added without breaking that suppression? This is the operationally relevant question (the criterion is not a general characterization of all composable pairs, but a sufficient condition for \emph{mechanism preservation} among pairs where at least one component has known effectiveness), never a sufficient condition for low ASR. We answer it first, with a checkable criterion, and treat the alternative, temporal mixing, as outside the scope of this paper. \textbf{But the practical question is not what the paper establishes}: what it establishes is that preservation of the statistic the screen checks is not causally tied to the security the screen predicts, so the criterion below should be read as the application §\ref{sec:condition_scoring} scopes it to (an ordering over an evaluation budget) and not as a deployment recommendation.

Observe that each robust aggregation defense $d$ operates through an \emph{admission signal} $\sigma(u)$ applied to client update $u$: it accepts, clips, or down-weights $u$ based on whether $\sigma(u)$ exceeds a threshold. Crucially, the admission signal varies by defense family:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Weighting defenses} (reputation, FoolsGold): $\sigma(u) = $ distance from consensus ($\ell_2$) or pairwise similarity.
\item \textbf{Clipping defenses} (NormClip): $\sigma(u) = \|u\|_2$.
\item \textbf{Rank-based defenses} (TrimmedMean, CoordMedian): $\sigma_k(u) = $ coordinate-wise rank of $u_k$.
\item \textbf{Geometric defenses} (RFA): $\sigma(u) = $ distance from geometric median.
\end{itemize}
When two defenses are applied sequentially (d1 transforms the updates, d2 aggregates the result), d1's transformation may \emph{corrupt} d2's signal or vice versa.

\begin{criterion}[Signal-Preservation Composability]
\label{thm:composability}
Let $d_1$ and $d_2$ be defense mechanisms. The following sufficient conditions guarantee that composition $(d_1 \to d_2)$ suppresses attack $a$. They are of \textbf{two different kinds}, and we group them accordingly, because conflating an empirical screen with a mechanistic property is the main source of confusion about what the criterion claims.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Stage 0: applicability.} Before either kind of condition is informative, the question the criterion asks has to be live.
\begin{description}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item[(C0) The downstream mechanism is load-bearing.] $\mathrm{ASR}(d_1,a) \geq 0.5$ for at least one committed attack $a$. C2 and C3 concern preservation of \emph{$d_2$'s} admission signal, so they say nothing when $d_1$ alone already suppresses everything: the composition inherits that suppression, destroying $d_2$'s signal costs nothing, and a correct prediction would be correct for a reason the criterion did not supply. The asymmetry is deliberate: a strong \emph{$d_2$} is the intended case, since asking whether upstream processing breaks an effective downstream defense is the framework's whole question, whereas a strong $d_1$ makes the question moot. C0 was \textbf{formulated post-hoc} and its provenance is in Remark~\ref{rem:c0}.
\end{description}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Mechanism-preservation conditions.} These are what Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} proves for the weighting$\to$aggregator class, and they are determined by $d_1$'s and $d_2$'s definitions alone, no experiment required. \textbf{What we recommend is an \emph{evaluation-prioritization criterion}, C1$\wedge$C2, and we name it that way rather than a ``screen'' because that is the whole of what our evidence supports.} \textbf{We disclaim predictive validity explicitly, here at first use}: the criterion orders candidate compositions for evaluation, and it does \emph{not} predict whether a composition is secure. The pooled two-channel model over $26$ cells is unconfirmed ($R^2{=}0.239$), and our own pre-registered replication \textbf{refuted} the cross-arm admission ordering the criterion would need in order to predict (§\ref{app:targeted_full}); recall is $40\%$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:emergent_full}), so the negative direction carries no information either. \textbf{It is never a security screen, and a PASS is an evaluation ordering rather than a deployment decision} (Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}). C3 is retained as a \textbf{margin diagnostic} rather than a gate, because on our data it never binds on a pair C1$\wedge$C2 already admits and shows no incremental predictive value (§\ref{sec:condition_scoring}). \emph{The pre-registered labels of §\ref{sec:composability_validation} were frozen with C3 in the gate and are not relabelled}; the demotion is a recommendation going forward, supported by a post-hoc factorial ablation, and it changes no reported number: C1$\wedge$C2 selects exactly the same two in-distribution pairs.
\begin{description}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item[(C2) Signal preservation.] $d_1$'s per-client transformation $T_1$ does not destroy $d_2$'s admission signal: $\sigma_2(T_1(u_a)) \approx \sigma_2(u_a)$ for malicious updates $u_a$.
\item[(C3) Boundary preservation.] $d_1$'s transformation does not compress the adversarial--benign separation below $d_2$'s discrimination threshold. Formally: if $d_2$ suppresses $a$ by detecting $\tau_{d_2}(u_a) > \theta$ (triggering trimming/rejection), then $\tau_{d_2}(T_1(u_a)) > \theta$ must hold, i.e., $d_1$ must not map adversarial updates from above to below $d_2$'s rejection boundary.
\end{description}
When C1 holds but C2 fails (signal destruction), composition is no better than $d_2$ alone. When C1 and C2 hold but C3 fails (boundary compression), $d_2$ retains its admission signal direction but its threshold no longer fires: the attack passes because $d_1$'s rescaling places adversarial magnitudes within $d_2$'s acceptance region.
\end{criterion}

Writing C2 as preservation of $d_2$'s admission signal elides a distinction our own experiments later force, so we name the levels before relying on the condition. They are the paper's main conceptual instrument, and every claim below is stated at one of them.

\begin{definition}[Preservation levels]
\label{def:levels_full}
For an upstream transform $T_1$ and a downstream defense $d_2$ reading admission signal $\sigma_2$:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,label=(P\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Value invariance:} $\sigma_2(T_1(U)) = \sigma_2(U)$.
\item \textbf{Ordering invariance:} $\sigma_2(T_1(U)) = \varphi(\sigma_2(U))$ for some $\varphi$ preserving the ordering $\sigma_2$ induces on clients. \emph{This is the level C2 is stated at} (made explicit in \eqref{eq:general_form}); the distinction from (P1) matters, because exact values are sufficient but never necessary.
\item \textbf{Decision invariance:} the client or coordinate $d_2$ actually selects is unchanged.
\item \textbf{Adversarial contribution (admission) invariance:} the \emph{adversarial} input $d_2$ admits into its aggregate is unchanged. \emph{Admission} is the term we use throughout for how much adversarial input survives $d_2$, and it is a \textbf{level, not one measurable quantity}: for a selector it is the indicator that an adversarial update is selected at all; for a weighted averager, the adversarial share of the weight mass; for a coordinate-wise order statistic, the adversarial share of selected coordinates, which is why Table~\ref{tab:channels_full}'s influence column is aggregator-specific by necessity. The committed pre-registrations say ``admission'', so that word is retained in every frozen rule name and no scored quantity is relabelled.
\item \textbf{Attack-suppression preservation:} ASR stays below threshold.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{(P1)$\Rightarrow$(P2)$\Rightarrow$(P3) hold, and hold by definition}: equal values induce the same ordering, and a rule reading only that ordering cannot decide differently. \textbf{These three are logical implications under the definitions above, not empirical claims about how defenses behave}, and nothing is asserted by proving them: their content is that they stop at (P3). \textbf{Below (P3) the chain breaks, and it breaks in both directions.} Both breaks are measured. \emph{Holding} decision \emph{and} admission invariance does not deliver suppression: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is bit-identical at every dose and its admitted adversarial mass is $0.000$ throughout, yet its ASR falls $0.173$ by the first rung alone through the magnitude channel, at $\rho{=}2.72$, where clean accuracy moves only $0.599 \to 0.567$ and our accuracy gate passes (paired $t{=}{-}1.976$, one-sided $p{=}0.044$, falling in $5/8$ seeds). Across the whole ladder it falls $0.425$, but that endpoint sits below our own accuracy floor, so \textbf{the gate-passing rung is the witness we rest on} (§\ref{app:targeted_full}). \emph{Losing} decision invariance does not cost suppression: Krum's selection flips in up to $80\%$ of rounds under an intervention that pins the adversarial mass, and its ASR stays inside the pre-specified equivalence margin ($|\Delta|{=}0.026 < 0.15$) (§\ref{app:targeted_full}). A criterion stated at (P1)--(P2) therefore constrains security in \emph{neither} direction. \textbf{(P4) is where the security content sits, but we do not claim it is sufficient for (P5), and it is a \emph{level} rather than one universal scalar security quantity.} It is instantiated per aggregator by the measure that aggregator's own rule exposes, Krum's and \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selected-client indicator, \texttt{coord\_median}'s share of selected coordinates, reputation's adversarial weight share (\texttt{experiments/build\_channel\_table.py}), so \textbf{(P4) names what to measure, not a single number comparable across defenses}. It is the first level that refers to \emph{adversarial influence on the aggregate} rather than to the downstream defense's internal decision, and \texttt{cos\_krum} is our own witness that (P4)$\;\not\Rightarrow\;$(P5): admission identically $0.000$ at every rung while suppression moves. \textbf{So the hierarchy answers ``which level does a security claim have to be stated at?'' and not ``which level predicts ASR?''}: no level in it is offered as a predictor.
\end{definition}
\begin{remark}[Which level C2 asserts, and which one is load-bearing]
\label{rem:levels}
At $\rho = 54.6$ the dose ladder of §\ref{app:ladder} changes Krum's \emph{decision} in $86.7\%$ of rounds while changing its \emph{admission} of adversarial input in $13.3\%$, and Krum's suppression does not deteriorate at all. A selection that flips from one benign client to another disturbs the statistic maximally and the mechanism not at all. \textbf{Terminology, once and for all: ``\emph{mechanism}'' always means the downstream defense's \emph{discriminative operation} (what separates adversarial from benign updates) and never its security behaviour, since that implication is precisely what this paper refutes.} We reserve ``mechanism preservation'' for the formal property Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} establishes, saying \emph{statistic} or \emph{decision} preservation where only the weaker property holds. C2 above is the (P2) condition (which is what makes it checkable from definitions alone) but the load-bearing reading is (P4). \textbf{This is the paper's central tension: our theorem operates at (P1)--(P3), while the experiments indicate security behaviour is decided at (P4)}; §\ref{app:targeted_full} puts the two against each other in an experiment where they predict opposite outcomes, and Figure~\ref{fig:story} is that argument in one picture.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[C0's provenance: an applicability condition formulated post-hoc]
\label{rem:c0}
C0 sits in Criterion~\ref{thm:composability} as a stage-0 applicability condition because Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} is stated over C0$\wedge$C1, but \textbf{it does not have the same evidential status as C1--C3}, and we record that here rather than let its promotion obscure it. It is \textbf{vacuous on the 7-defense menu of §\ref{sec:composability_validation}}, where the strongest single defense reaches only $0.519$, and we formulated it only after the prospective suite of §\ref{sec:prospective} introduced a defense for which it binds. It is a lesson from prospective evaluation, applied there retrospectively; it is \emph{not} part of the criterion validated on the 42 pairs, and none of the accuracy figures in §\ref{sec:composability_validation} depend on it.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{A scope condition on the criterion itself, not a limitation of our runs.} C1 and every prediction built on it are defined against a \emph{committed} adversary, one whose attack is fixed before the composition is chosen. An adversary who observes the composition can re-target whichever channel the screen certifies, so \textbf{what the screen bounds is \emph{inherited} robustness under a fixed attack, never robustness against an adaptive one}. Our single whitebox composition-aware stress test is consistent with that reading ($0.051 \to 0.167$ seed-matched, $3.3\times$ the committed-attack ASR; §\ref{sec:fg_rfa_flagship}); \textbf{its role is not to measure adaptive robustness but to show that an inherited-robustness guarantee is not adaptive robustness}, and a screening condition stated against an adaptive adversary is left open.

\subsection{The testability boundary}
\label{app:boundary}

C0 and C1 together constrain what any validation of this criterion can look like. We did not anticipate this; it turns out to explain the central weakness of our own prospective suite (§\ref{sec:prospective}, L4), and (once stated in general form) to \emph{derive} the class of within-defense designs in which C2's contribution is identifiable (§\ref{app:ladder}). Its content is narrower than ``a general identification boundary'' and worth stating precisely: \textbf{under inherited-effect gating}, cross-defense contrasts cannot identify the mechanism's contribution, because the gate conditions on a descendant of both the mechanism and the outcome. What we offer as the transferable contribution is not that negative but \textbf{the admissible design it derives}, set out below and run in §\ref{app:targeted_full}.

\begin{lemma}[Testability boundary]
\label{lem:testability}
Let $(d_1 \to d_2)$ satisfy C0 and C1, and let $a^*$ be a committed attack witnessing C0, so $\mathrm{ASR}(d_1,a^*) \geq 0.5$. Then $\mathrm{ASR}(d_2,a^*) < 0.5$: the downstream defense suppresses $a^*$ \emph{on its own}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
C1 at $a^*$ gives $\min_{d\in\{d_1,d_2\}}\mathrm{ASR}(d,a^*) < 0.5$. By C0 the minimum is not attained at $d_1$, hence it is attained at $d_2$.
\end{proof}

Nothing in the argument is specific to federated learning, to backdoors, or to our defense menu. Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} is \textbf{the enabling observation for the FL instance}; the substantive claim is the general one, and we state it as such.

\begin{proposition}[Why constituent-effect contrasts cannot identify composition effects under inherited-effect gating]
\label{prop:identification_full}
Let a screen certify $(d_1 \to d_2)$ against attack $a$ only if both
(i) an \textbf{eligibility gate} $E(d_1,d_2,a) := \min_{d \in \{d_1,d_2\}} M(d,a) < \theta$ holds, where $M$ and $\theta$ are the \emph{same} outcome metric and threshold the screen predicts for the composition, and
(ii) a \textbf{mechanism condition} $P(d_1,d_2)$ holds, determined by the two defenses' definitions alone.
Suppose the screen is applied only where the downstream mechanism is load-bearing, $M(d_1,a) \ge \theta$. Consider estimating $P$'s contribution to $M(d_1 \to d_2, a)$ from contrasts across a defense menu $\mathcal{D}$. Then every certified pair satisfies $M(d_2,a) < \theta$, so of the four cells of $\{P,\neg P\} \times \{M(d_2,a) < \theta,\ M(d_2,a) \ge \theta\}$ the certified set occupies at most two, and \textbf{the cell needed to separate $P$ from $d_2$'s standalone effectiveness is empty by the gate, for every $\mathcal{D}$}. No contrast varying $d_2$ identifies $P$'s contribution. Identification requires holding $d_2$, $a$ and therefore $E$ fixed while intervening on the upstream transform: \textbf{the admissible designs are within-defense.}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The load-bearing condition excludes $d_1$ from attaining the minimum in (i), so certification forces $M(d_2,a) < \theta$; both cells with $M(d_2,a) \ge \theta$ are therefore unpopulated whatever $\mathcal{D}$ is, and $P$ is never observed against a downstream defense that fails on its own. Any estimator of $P$'s contribution from such contrasts is unidentified.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A second boundary: what such a screen cannot find.} The gate that limits what a screen can be \emph{validated} on also limits what it can \emph{cover}, which converts the most obvious objection to our criterion, that its recall is poor, into a scope result.

\begin{proposition}[Inherited-suppression screens cannot certify emergent synergy]
\label{prop:emergent_full}
Call $(d_1 \to d_2)$'s suppression of $a$ \textbf{emergent} if $M(d_1 \to d_2, a) < \theta$ while $\min_{d \in \{d_1,d_2\}} M(d,a) \ge \theta$. No emergent composition satisfies the eligibility gate of Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}. Hence, writing $\mathcal{S}$ for the compositions with $M < \theta$ and $\mathcal{E} \subseteq \mathcal{S}$ for the emergent ones, any such screen's recall obeys $\mathrm{recall} \le 1 - |\mathcal{E}|/|\mathcal{S}|$ on every menu and every attack.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Emergence is the negation of the gate, so each member of $\mathcal{E}$ is rejected while belonging to $\mathcal{S}$: a false negative. The bound is the resulting recall.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Mechanism--effectiveness confounding: Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} in our instance.} The boundary has an uncomfortable consequence for validation, and it is worth spelling out concretely. In \emph{every} in-scope pair the criterion certifies, $d_2$ already suppresses the attack that $d_1$ fails. A certified composition's low ASR can therefore always be attributed to $d_2$'s standalone strength (C1) rather than to $d_1$ having preserved $d_2$'s mechanism (C2, C3): the two are confounded \textbf{by the conditions themselves}, not by an unlucky choice of defense menu. We name this \textbf{mechanism--effectiveness confounding} and offer it as a methodological observation about validating composition criteria of this shape, applicable beyond our own experiments.

In particular it rules out the experiment we previously named as decisive: a $d_2$ whose admission signal is invariant under positive rescaling but which is \emph{weak in isolation} cannot satisfy C1 in scope, so no such certified pair exists to test. The achievable substitute is \textbf{per-attack complementarity}: a $d_2$ that suppresses the attack $d_1$ fails while remaining weak on a \emph{different} committed attack, so its max-committed ASR stays high and it cannot suppress the composition single-handedly. Reputation is such a defense ($0.017$ on model-scaling, $0.842$ on the pixel backdoor), and §\ref{sec:metric_swap} builds the test on that observation.
\begin{proof}[Argument sketch]
(C1 sufficient) If at least one of $d_1$, $d_2$ individually suppresses $a$, composition inherits that suppression provided C2$\wedge$C3 preserve the signal chain. C1 is sufficient, not necessary: two individually-weak defenses may suppress synergistically through complementary mechanisms (e.g., partial downweighting + geometric rejection), but predicting such synergy requires the quantitative margins below. The criterion is therefore \emph{conservative}; it may reject working compositions but will not approve broken ones.

(C2 role) Suppose $d_2$ individually suppresses $a$ via signal $\sigma_2(u_a) > \theta$. If $d_1$'s transformation $T_1$ destroys this signal ($\sigma_2(T_1(u_a)) \not\approx \sigma_2(u_a)$), $d_2$ cannot detect the attack. Example: NormClip as $d_1$ equalizes all norms to $\leq\tau$, so reputation's distance signal $\sigma_2(u) = \|u - \hat\mu\|$ collapses; adversarial and benign updates become equidistant from consensus. This is measured, not hypothetical: NormClip$\to$reputation leaves model-scaling ASR at $0.807 \pm 0.048$ and the pixel backdoor at $0.842 \pm 0.047$ over 5 seeds, against reputation alone at $0.017$ (per-seed values in §\ref{app:composition}). Quantitatively: C2 holds when relative signal distortion $\Delta_{\mathrm{signal}} := |\sigma_2(T_1(u_a)) - \sigma_2(u_a)|/\sigma_2(u_a) < \delta_2$ for a defense-specific tolerance $\delta_2$.

(C3 role) Even when $d_1$ preserves the \emph{direction} of $d_2$'s signal (C2 holds), it may compress magnitudes below $d_2$'s threshold. Example: FoolsGold as $d_1$ downweights adversarial clients by $\alpha \ll 1$. TrimmedMean's coordinate-wise rank signal still distinguishes adversarial coordinates (C2 holds), but after rescaling by $\alpha$, per-coordinate values $\alpha \cdot u_{a,k}$ fall within the trimming range: the coordinate-wise extreme that would trigger trimming is no longer extreme. Quantitatively: C3 holds when the margin ratio $\Delta_{\mathrm{margin}} := (\tau_{d_2}(T_1(u_a)) - \theta)/(\tau_{d_2}(u_a) - \theta) > 0$, i.e., adversarial scores remain above $d_2$'s rejection boundary after $d_1$'s transform.

(Sufficiency) If C1 holds, C2 holds ($\Delta_{\mathrm{signal}} < \delta_2$), and C3 holds ($\Delta_{\mathrm{margin}} > 0$), then $d_2$ detects and suppresses $u_a$ even after $d_1$'s transformation. For the weighting$\to$aggregator class, Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} instantiates these margins explicitly: $\rho < r^\star$ for the rank aggregators, and the transformed-point separation condition $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} > 2(R'_B+\delta')$ for the geometric one, which Corollary~\ref{cor:checkable_margin} restates in a form that can be checked without running the composition. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\subsection{A restricted theorem: C2$\wedge$C3 for weighting$\to$rank and weighting$\to$geometric compositions}
\label{sec:restricted_theorem}
\label{app:proofs}

\noindent\textbf{What follows is a boundary theorem, and deliberately not a security theorem.} It bounds what an \emph{invariance} argument about composition can establish: preservation of the downstream defense's discriminative mechanism, levels (P1)--(P3) of Definition~\ref{def:levels_full}, and it says nothing whatever about suppression, which lives at (P4)--(P5). We state that in the same breath as the theorem because the whole point of the paper is the gap between the two: the theorem is the best case for statistic-level reasoning, proved with explicit margins, and §\ref{app:targeted_full} then shows that even exact invariance neither implies nor preserves suppression. Read it as a \emph{formal} claim in the sense of Table~\ref{tab:scope_levels}: proved, on one class, bounding no ASR.

The criterion above is an operational heuristic. For the class of compositions that matter most in practice, a per-client \emph{weighting} defense $d_1$ (reputation, FoolsGold) followed by a \emph{rank-based} or \emph{geometric} aggregator $d_2$ (TrimmedMean, CoordMedian, RFA), which contains the flagship FoolsGold$\to$RFA, C2 and C3 can be \emph{proven} rather than asserted, with an explicit tolerance on how aggressively $d_1$ may reweight. The key structural fact is that $d_2$'s admission score is scale-equivariant, so it commutes with $d_1$'s reweighting up to a bounded ratio; the composition fails only when $d_1$ compresses that ratio past a margin set by $d_2$'s separation.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded reweighting preserves the downstream discriminative mechanism]
\label{thm:bounded_reweight}
Consider a round with $n_b$ benign and $n_a$ adversarial updates under honest majority $n_b > n_a$. Let $d_1$ be a per-client reweighting $T_1(u_i) = w_i u_i$ with $w_i \geq 0$, and define the ratio over \emph{strictly positive} weights, $\rho := \max_i w_i / \min_{i:\,w_i>0} w_i$. Suppose $d_2$ suppresses attack $a$ through a client admission score $\sigma_2(\cdot\,;U)$ that is scale-equivariant, $\sigma_2(c\,u_i; c\,U) = c^{k}\sigma_2(u_i;U)$ for all $c>0$ and some degree $k\ge 0$. Assume $w_a > 0$ for adversarial clients; the $w_a = 0$ case is Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation}. The theorem establishes preservation of $d_2$'s suppression \emph{mechanism}; it does not bound ASR. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{(Rank aggregators; C2$\wedge$C3 hold up to a margin.)} If $d_2\in\{\text{TrimmedMean},\text{CoordMedian}\}$, so $\sigma_2$ is a coordinate-wise rank, then $d_2$'s per-coordinate suppression of $a$ is preserved when two conditions hold. \textbf{(i)~Count majority:} $n_a < K/2$ for CoordMedian, and $n_a \leq \lfloor\beta K\rfloor$ for TrimmedMean with $\beta$-trimming. \textbf{(ii)~Signed ordering:} on each suppression-relevant coordinate $k$, the adversarial transformed value lies strictly beyond every benign one, $w_a u_{a,k} > \max_b w_b u_{b,k}$ (upper tail) or $w_a u_{a,k} < \min_b w_b u_{b,k}$ (lower tail). \emph{Sufficient magnitude condition for} (ii): if $\mathrm{sign}(u_{a,k})$ is common to all adversaries and $|u_{a,k}| > \rho\cdot\max_b|u_{b,k}|$, then (ii) holds; writing $r^\star := \min_k \min_b |u_{a,k}|/|u_{b,k}|$ for the coordinate-wise separation margin, this is $\rho < r^\star$. Magnitude alone is \emph{not} sufficient without the sign assumption: an adversarial coordinate can be large in absolute value yet sit between the benign values. At $\rho=1$ ranks are preserved exactly, recovering Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_invariance}.
\item \textbf{(Geometric median / RFA; C2$\wedge$C3 hold up to a margin.)} The composition hands RFA the \emph{transformed points} $u'_i := w_i u_i$ and RFA runs its standard \emph{unweighted} geometric median on them; it is not a weighted geometric median, and $w_i$ enters only by moving the points. All quantities must therefore be defined on the transformed set. Let $\mu'_B$ be the geometric median of the benign $u'_b$ alone, $\mu'$ that of all of $U'$, $R'_B := \max_b\|u'_b - \mu'_B\|$ the transformed benign radius, and $\delta' := \|\mu' - \mu'_B\|$ the displacement caused by including the adversarial points. Suppression is preserved when
\[
\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} := \min_{a,b}\|u'_a - u'_b\| \;>\; 2\,(R'_B + \delta'),
\]
under which every adversarial residual $\|u'_a - \mu'\|$ strictly exceeds every benign residual $\|u'_b - \mu'\|$, so RFA's inverse-residual weight on $a$ stays below the benign floor.
\item \textbf{(Norm-equalizing $d_1$; C2 fails: the degenerate case.)} If instead $d_1$ maps every client norm to a common value (NormClip with $\tau$ below all client norms), then any $\sigma_2$ keyed on update norm or consensus distance becomes constant across clients, so $\sigma_2$ no longer separates $a$ and C2 fails. This is \emph{not} a bounded reweighting: the induced per-coordinate weight ratio is unbounded, so no $\rho$-margin applies.
\end{enumerate}
Parts (1) and (2) are sufficient conditions for $d_2$ to \emph{preserve the downstream suppression ordering}, not to exclude adversarial contributions outright. Neither is a sufficient condition for low ASR, and part (2) orders residuals per client only; it constrains the \emph{aggregate} adversarial coefficient solely through Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(1) TrimmedMean/CoordMedian act on coordinate $k$ through the rank of $w_i u_{i,k}$ among $\{w_j u_{j,k}\}_j$; $a$ is suppressed on $k$ because its transformed coordinate is extreme (trimmed, or not selected by the median). Given (ii), at most $n_a$ of the extreme order statistics are adversarial, so with (i) the selected value is benign. For the magnitude condition, $w_a>0$ gives $\rho \ge \max_b w_b/w_a$, so $|u_{a,k}| > \rho\max_b|u_{b,k}|$ implies $|w_a u_{a,k}| > w_a\rho\max_b|u_{b,k}| \ge (\max_b w_b)\max_b|u_{b,k}| \ge \max_b|w_b u_{b,k}|$; with a sign common to all adversaries this places $w_a u_{a,k}$ strictly beyond every benign value on that side. Scale-equivariance ($k{=}0$ for pure rank) makes the uniform case $\rho{=}1$ exact.
(2) All quantities are on the transformed points $u'_i = w_i u_i$, which is what RFA receives. Every benign residual obeys $\|u'_b - \mu'\| \le R'_B + \delta'$ by the triangle inequality. For adversarial $u'_a$, $\|u'_a - \mu'_B\| \ge \Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} - R'_B$, hence $\|u'_a - \mu'\| \ge \Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} - R'_B - \delta'$. Demanding that this exceed the benign bound gives $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} - R'_B - \delta' > R'_B + \delta'$, i.e.\ the stated condition. No weighted-majority assumption is needed, and none would be appropriate: RFA weights every input point equally and the $w_i$ act only by relocating the points. Our implementation runs Weiszfeld for 50 iterations at tolerance $10^{-6}$, so the argument transfers to it.
(3) Clipping to a common $\tau$ sends $\|u_i\|\mapsto\tau$ for all $i$, so $\sigma_2(u_i)=\|u_i\|$ (NormClip-as-$d_2$) or the consensus distance $\|u_i-\hat\mu\|$ collapses to a client-independent constant; separation vanishes, which is precisely C2-failure. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{The condition as stated cannot be checked before running the composition, and that is fixable.} Part (2) is stated on the transformed points, and $\delta' = \|\mu'-\mu'_B\|$ is by definition the displacement that including the adversarial points causes: evaluating it requires executing the very composition the criterion is meant to screen. In a screening criterion that is a real defect. It is also removable, because the displacement is controlled by the benign spread and the client counts alone.

\begin{corollary}[Checkable separation margin]
\label{cor:checkable_margin}
In the setting of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2), write $S'_B := \sum_b \|u'_b - \mu'_B\|$ and let $\bar R'_B := S'_B/n_b$ be the \emph{mean} transformed benign residual. Under honest majority $n_b > n_a$,
\[
\delta' \;\leq\; \frac{2 S'_B}{\,n_b - n_a\,} \;=\; C_A\,\bar R'_B, \qquad C_A := \frac{2 n_b}{\,n_b - n_a\,},
\]
and therefore the separation condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2) holds whenever
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:checkable_margin}
\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} \;>\; 2 R'_B + 2\,C_A\,\bar R'_B .
\end{equation}
No quantity in \eqref{eq:checkable_margin} is computed from the composed aggregate.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\mu'$ minimizes $F(x) := \sum_i \|x - u'_i\|$, so $F(\mu') \leq F(\mu'_B)$. Write $t := \delta'$. By the triangle inequality each benign term obeys $\|\mu' - u'_b\| \geq t - \|u'_b - \mu'_B\|$ and each adversarial term obeys $\|\mu' - u'_a\| \geq \|\mu'_B - u'_a\| - t$, so $F(\mu') \geq n_b t - S'_B + \sum_a \|\mu'_B - u'_a\| - n_a t$. Since $F(\mu'_B) = S'_B + \sum_a \|\mu'_B - u'_a\|$, the inequality $F(\mu') \leq F(\mu'_B)$ gives $(n_b - n_a)\,t \leq 2 S'_B$, which is the displacement bound. Substituting it into $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} > 2(R'_B + \delta')$ yields \eqref{eq:checkable_margin}. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Honest majority is exactly what the geometric case needs, and it enters on the displacement, not on the weight ratio.} $C_A$ diverges as $n_a \to n_b$, so Corollary~\ref{cor:checkable_margin} is the precise point at which $n_b > n_a$ becomes indispensable, while the theorem itself needs no majority assumption on the \emph{weights}. An earlier version of this paper summarized part (2) as a weight-ratio bound $\rho < n_b/n_a$; that summary was wrong, and the correction is not cosmetic, because the majority condition belongs on $\delta'$ instead. \textbf{Measured at Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.5$}: the displacement bound holds in all $17$ rounds with $w_a>0$, and is conservative there by a factor of at least $10.5$ (mean $215$), because the realized displacement is set by a force balance among the residuals rather than by the worst case a bound must cover. Condition~\eqref{eq:checkable_margin} is satisfied in $16$ of those $17$ rounds: $15/15$ at $n_a{=}1$, where $C_A = 2.67$ and the requirement at the measured mean $\bar R'_B/R'_B = 0.429$ is $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} > 4.29\,R'_B$ against a measured $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}}/R'_B$ never below $5.88$; and $1/2$ at $n_a{=}2$, where $C_A = 6.00$ tightens the requirement past what one of the two rounds supplies. \textbf{The heterogeneity limit recorded for Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass} applies here too}: at $\alpha{=}0.1$ condition~\eqref{eq:checkable_margin} holds in only $5$ of $12$ positive-weight rounds. The checkable form certifies less exactly where the condition it replaces also stops binding, and it does not rescue the guarantee there.

\noindent\textbf{A further reduction to $\rho$ alone is vacuous on our runs, and we report it as vacuous.} The natural next ask is the analogue of part (1)'s $\rho < r^\star$: a threshold on $\rho$ computable from the \emph{pre-composition} round geometry, so that no pilot is needed at all. Such a threshold exists formally. Bounding $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} \geq w_{\min}[\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}} - (\rho-1)N_A]$ and $R'_B \leq 2 w_{\min}[\rho R_B + (\rho-1)\|\mu_B\|]$, where $\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}$, $R_B$ and $\mu_B$ are the untransformed analogues and $N_A := \max_a\|u_a\|$, makes $w_{\min}$ cancel and turns \eqref{eq:checkable_margin} into a linear inequality in $\rho$ with a critical value $\rho^\star$ depending only on $(\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}, R_B, \|\mu_B\|, N_A, n_a, n_b)$. \textbf{It is empty on our data.} It requires $\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}} > 4(1{+}C_A)R_B$, and the measured $\Delta_{\mathrm{sep}}/[4(1{+}C_A)R_B]$ never reaches $1$. Its maximum is $1.015$ at $\alpha{=}0.5$, attained in an $n_a{=}1$ round where $C_A = 2.67$ makes the requirement loosest, with mean $0.754$ over those $15$ rounds and a maximum of only $0.389$ over the two $n_a{=}2$ rounds; at $\alpha{=}0.1$ the maximum is $0.853$. Consequently $\rho^\star \leq 1$ in $16$ of $17$ rounds at $\alpha{=}0.5$ and in all $12$ at $\alpha{=}0.1$, against a measured $\rho$ of at least $1.034$ and $1.015$ in the two settings respectively: \emph{no} value of $\rho$ satisfies the reduced condition, which is a stronger negative than a merely conservative threshold. The looseness is in the composition of the two bounds rather than in either one separately, and closing it would need a curvature assumption on the benign configuration that we are not prepared to assume. \textbf{We therefore claim no pilot-free screen for the geometric class}, and the calibration protocol below prices it accordingly.

\noindent\textbf{What part (2) does not assume.} Recorded explicitly, because the step invites readings it does not support: there is no honest-majority assumption on the \emph{weights}, no requirement that adversarial updates lie outside the benign convex hull, and the count majority $n_b > n_a$ is used in part (2) only through Corollary~\ref{cor:checkable_margin}, nowhere in the theorem's own proof.

Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2) orders residuals \emph{per client}: each adversarial point receives less inverse-residual weight than each benign point. That alone does not bound the adversary's \emph{total} share of the aggregate: several adversaries could each be down-weighted yet collectively hold substantial mass. The separation margin controls this, but only quantitatively:

\begin{corollary}[Bounded aggregate adversarial influence]
\label{cor:aggregate_mass}
Let $m := \Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}}/[2(R'_B+\delta')]$ be the realized separation margin, so Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2) reads $m>1$. Every benign residual is at most $D_b := R'_B+\delta'$ and every adversarial residual at least $D_a := \Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}}-R'_B-\delta' = (2m-1)D_b$. Bounding $\sum_j 1/d_j$ below by the benign terms alone and each adversarial term above by $1/D_a$,
\[
\Lambda_a \;=\; \sum_{a}\lambda_a \;\leq\; \frac{n_a/D_a}{n_b/D_b + n_a/D_a} \;=\; \frac{n_a}{n_b(2m-1)+n_a},
\]
so $\mu'$ is a convex combination whose adversarial share is at most $\Lambda_a$: writing $\mu' = (1-\Lambda_a)\bar u_B + \Lambda_a \bar u_A$ for its benign and adversarial parts, the displacement from the benign-only combination $\bar u_B$ is at most $\Lambda_a\|\bar u_A - \bar u_B\|$.
\end{corollary}

\noindent The bound is only as good as the margin, and \textbf{the separation condition alone is not enough}: as $m\to1$ it degrades to $n_a/(n_b+n_a)$, permitting the adversary $40\%$ of the mass at $n_a{=}2$, $n_b{=}3$. A margin comfortably above $1$ does the work. We measure $m \geq 2.82$ in every round with $w_a>0$ (mean $4.71$ over those rounds), capping the adversarial share at $5.1\%$ for $n_a{=}1$ and $12.6\%$ for $n_a{=}2$. The realized figure is far tighter than the bound: measuring $\Lambda_a$ directly, it never exceeds $1.8\%$ in any round, and the bound is satisfied in all 17 measured rounds with $w_a>0$. With Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} this completes the picture, and the mechanism survives for \emph{opposite} reasons: at $w_a{=}0$ the adversaries hold most of the Weiszfeld mass ($\Lambda_a=0.71$ measured) but carry no payload; at $w_a>0$ they carry payload but hold at most $12.6\%$.

\noindent\textbf{This is the one place in the paper where the theory predicts a number and the measurement lands inside it}, so we state the pair plainly: \textbf{bound $12.6\%$, realized $\le 1.8\%$}, in every measured round with $w_a>0$. \textbf{And it is also the one place where heterogeneity makes the guarantee measurably empty.} The bound is a function of the realized margin $m$ alone, and at Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.1$ RFA's \textbf{all-round mean} margin \textbf{collapses from $m{=}3.24$ to $m{=}1.01$}, exactly the limit at which Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass} degrades to $n_a/(n_b{+}n_a)$ and certifies nothing, with two of three certified compositions crossing the ASR threshold there (§\ref{sec:oos_pass}(E)). So the theorem's explanatory value is genuine and measured in \emph{both} directions: it predicts a number that holds where heterogeneity is moderate, and its own quantity records where it stops holding. \textbf{That is not a robustness check; it is one of the paper's three central empirical findings} (§\ref{app:targeted_full}, §\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{$\Lambda_a$ is level (P4) in measurable form, but (P4) is a \emph{level}, not a single metric.} Before the instantiation, one clarification the rest of the paper depends on: \textbf{(P4) is a conceptual level of the hierarchy and is instantiated defense-specifically}, because different aggregator families have different notions of what it means for adversarial input to enter the aggregate at all. A \emph{selector} admits or does not, so its (P4) is an indicator in $\{0,1\}$; a \emph{weighted averager} has an adversarial coefficient share in $[0,1]$; a \emph{coordinate-wise order statistic} has a fraction of selected coordinates that are adversarial. There is no single scalar all three compute, and this is why the influence column of Table~\ref{tab:channels_full} is \textbf{necessarily aggregator-specific} rather than a common measurement across rows: a fact about the level, not a shortcoming of the table. With that said: Definition~\ref{def:levels_full}'s levels are not equally meaningful. (P1)--(P3) are properties of a statistic and of a decision rule reading it; \textbf{(P4) is the first level whose content is the adversary's influence on the aggregate}, and for a geometric aggregator $\Lambda_a$ is exactly that level made measurable: Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass} bounds it, Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} drives it to a benign-only convex combination, and §\ref{app:targeted_full}'s instruments intervene on it directly. So the negative results do not leave the theory empty-handed: \textbf{the level the evidence points to is one the theory can bound, just not from a $(d_1,d_2)$ definition pair alone}, which is what a screen would need. That gap is the open problem we state in §\ref{sec:conclusion}. \textbf{Three quantities are distinguished throughout and never used interchangeably}: \emph{admitted adversarial mass}, whether an adversarial update enters $d_2$'s aggregate at all, binary for a selector and a coordinate fraction for an order statistic; the \emph{adversarial coefficient share}, the fraction of the upstream coefficient vector's total mass held by adversaries, which is what the instruments of §\ref{app:targeted_full} pin; and \emph{aggregate adversarial influence} $\Lambda_a$, the weight the adversary's direction actually carries in the aggregate. Unqualified, ``admission'' means the first. (Symbols are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:notation}.)

\begin{lemma}[Zero-weight annihilation]
\label{lem:annihilation}
Zero-weight clients are \emph{not} removed from the composition: $d_2$ receives them as zero vectors and still sees all $K$ points. Our implementation runs the $\varepsilon$-smoothed Weiszfeld iteration ($\varepsilon=10^{-8}$), whose fixed point is $\mu' = \sum_i \lambda_i u'_i$ with $\lambda_i \propto 1/\max(\|u'_i-\mu'\|,\varepsilon)$: a convex combination for \emph{every} configuration, including the singular case $\mu'\in U'$ that a zero-weight client at the origin can create, so no non-degeneracy assumption is required. If $w_a = 0$ for every adversarial client, then $u'_a = 0$ and, writing $\Lambda_a := \sum_{a}\lambda_a$, the adversarial terms vanish identically:
$\mu' = \sum_{b\text{ benign}}\lambda_b u'_b = (1-\Lambda_a)\,\bar u$ with $\bar u$ a convex combination of benign transformed updates. The aggregate is a \emph{contraction by $1-\Lambda_a\in(0,1)$ of a benign-only weighted average}: the attack direction never enters $\mu'$. The origin points' only effect is that contraction: a reduction in effective step size, i.e.\ a utility cost, not a security failure. The same holds for CoordMedian, where a zeroed coordinate carries no payload but shrinks the aggregate when selected.
\end{lemma}

\noindent\textbf{Why the positive-weight restriction matters.} Defining $\rho$ over all weights would make it \emph{infinite in every round}: FoolsGold's normalization subtracts the minimum weight, so 2 of 5 clients receive exactly zero (measured, $N{=}10$, $K{=}5$). A bound of the form $\rho < 4$ would then never be satisfiable, and Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2) would be vacuous on the very composition it is stated over. Restricting $\rho$ to strictly positive weights and handling $w_a{=}0$ separately via Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} is what makes the analysis apply to the algorithm we actually run.

\noindent\textbf{Operational calibration protocol.} To evaluate the criterion for ($d_1{\to}d_2$): \textbf{C1} is available from standard single-defense baselines (zero cost). \textbf{C2/C3 for the theorem-backed class} (FG or reputation as $d_1$; RFA, CM, TM as $d_2$): for the \emph{rank} aggregators, Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(1)'s bound $\rho < r^\star$ needs only $\rho$ and the coordinate-wise separation, both read off the C1 baseline runs, so this case really is free. \textbf{For the \emph{geometric} case an earlier version of this paper claimed the same, and that was false}: Corollary~\ref{cor:checkable_margin} removes the one quantity that would have required running the composition, but the benign spread that replaces it is measured on the transformed points, so RFA carries the same short-pilot cost as the other families and we no longer exempt it. \textbf{C2/C3 for other families}: one short pilot (5--10 rounds, ${\sim}10\%$ of full cost) measuring $\sigma_2(T_1(u_a))$ vs.\ $\sigma_2(u_a)$ suffices. The full 50-round experiment runs only for criterion-PASS candidates. $\delta_2 = 0.5$ separates all PASS from C2-FAIL cases on the development set; insensitive to choice: any $\delta_2 \in [0.3, 0.7]$ yields identical classifications on all 42 pairs.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-invariance of coordinate-wise aggregators]
\label{prop:rank_invariance}
Let $d_2$ be a coordinate-wise rank aggregator (TrimmedMean or CoordMedian) with admission statistic $\sigma_k(u) = \mathrm{rank}_k(u)$ among the received updates $\{u_1, \ldots, u_m\}$. Let $T_1(u_i) = \alpha \cdot u_i$ be a \emph{uniform} positive rescaling ($\alpha > 0$, same for all clients). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt]
\item $\sigma_k(T_1(u_i)) = \sigma_k(u_i)$ for all $i, k$, i.e., C2 and C3 hold trivially.
\item Under \emph{heterogeneous} rescaling $T_1(u_i) = \alpha_i \cdot u_i$ with $\alpha_i \neq \alpha_j$, rank preservation fails when rescaling reorders coordinates across clients: $\exists\, k$ s.t.\ $\mathrm{rank}_k(\alpha_i u_{i,k}) \neq \mathrm{rank}_k(u_{i,k})$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(1) Rank is invariant to positive scalar multiplication: $u_{i,k} > u_{j,k} \iff \alpha u_{i,k} > \alpha u_{j,k}$ for $\alpha > 0$. (2) Counterexample: let $u_{1,k} = 3$, $u_{2,k} = 2$, with $\alpha_1 = 0.1$, $\alpha_2 = 1$. Then $\alpha_1 u_{1,k} = 0.3 < \alpha_2 u_{2,k} = 2$, reversing the rank.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:cm_robust}
CoordMedian (pure rank selection) is robust to C3 boundary compression under any uniform rescaling but vulnerable under heterogeneous client-specific rescaling (e.g., FoolsGold or reputation weights). This predicts that compositions X$\to$CoordMedian will show \emph{intermediate} ASR when $d_1$ applies heterogeneous weights: consistent with observed NC$\to$cm (0.433) and rfa$\to$cm (0.485), both near the 0.5 threshold.
\end{corollary}

Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_invariance} concerns rank under \emph{uniform} rescaling. The sharper question is which admission signals survive \emph{heterogeneous} per-client rescaling, since that is what every per-client defense in our menu applies: NormClip ($c_i = \min(1,\tau/\|u_i\|)$), reputation ($c_i \propto e^{-d_i/s}$) and RFA-reweighting ($c_i \propto 1/\max(\|u_i-\mu'\|,\varepsilon)$) are all of the form $T(u_i) = c_i u_i$ with $c_i > 0$ strictly, each positive by construction. FoolsGold is of the same form with $c_i \ge 0$ but attains $c_i = 0$ in \emph{every} round (its shift-and-clamp gives the least-trusted client weight exactly zero, 2 of 5 clients here), so it falls outside the strict hypothesis below and is governed by Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} instead: the same distinction we draw for FLTrust's ReLU zeros in \S\ref{sec:prospective}. The strictly-positive case has a sharp answer, and it partitions the menu:

\begin{proposition}[Invariance classes under positive per-client rescaling]
\label{prop:invariance}
Let $T(u_i) = c_i u_i$ with $c_i > 0$, \textbf{strictly}: the form taken by NormClip ($c_i = \min(1, \tau/\|u_i\|)$), Reputation ($c_i \propto e^{-d_i/s}$) and RFA-reweighting ($c_i \propto 1/\max(\|u_i - \mu'\|, \varepsilon)$), each positive by construction. \textbf{FoolsGold is deliberately not in that list}: its shift-and-clamp gives the least-trusted client weight exactly $0$ in \emph{every} round (2 of 5 clients here), so a zero coefficient is not a \emph{positive} rescaling and FoolsGold-as-$d_1$ falls outside the hypothesis, governed by Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} rather than by (a)--(c). Then:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep,topsep=2pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \textbf{Cosine similarity is exactly invariant:} $\cos(c_iu_i, c_ju_j) = \cos(u_i,u_j)$. FoolsGold's weight vector is a function of the pairwise-cosine matrix alone, so $w(T(U)) = w(U)$ identically, and \textbf{C2 holds for every $\cdot\to$FoolsGold composition}.
\item \textbf{Coordinate ordering is not invariant} in general; it survives under the bounded-ratio condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(1).
\item \textbf{Consensus distance and residual magnitude are not invariant.} Heterogeneous $c_i$ can invert their ordering, so $\cdot\to$reputation compositions fail C2, and $\cdot\to$RFA needs the separation condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(2).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) $c_iu_i/\|c_iu_i\| = u_i/\|u_i\|$ for $c_i>0$, so every pairwise cosine, each client's normalized maximum similarity, and hence every FoolsGold weight is unchanged. (c) Counterexample for consensus distance: with $u_1{=}(1,0)$, $u_2{=}(0,1)$, $u_3{=}(3,3)$ the coordinate-wise median is $(1,1)$ and $u_3$ is farthest; under $c=(1,1,0.1)$ the median becomes $(0.3,0.3)$ and $u_3$ becomes the \emph{closest} point, reversing the ordering reputation depends on.
\end{proof}

\noindent This corrects a natural but wrong intuition. Norm clipping does \emph{not} blind FoolsGold: it cannot, by (a), though it does blind reputation, whose signal is consensus distance rather than similarity. We verified (a) in the shipped implementation: FoolsGold's weight vector changes by at most $3\times10^{-8}$ (float32 rounding) under NormClip, across 1000 synthetic trials and live FL rounds in which 1--2 of 5 clients were genuinely clipped (raw norms up to $24.5$ against $\tau{=}5$). Note this also means Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(3)'s norm-equalizing degenerate case requires $\tau$ below \emph{all} client norms; at our $\tau{=}5$ only 1--2 of 5 clients clip per round, so norms are not equalized and that case does not describe our runs.


\subsection{The framework in general form}
\label{sec:general_form}

Positive rescaling is the instantiation that matters for current defenses, but the structure is more general, and stating it separates what is essential to the framework from what is an artifact of today's defense menu. Write $d_2$'s admission mechanism as a statistic $S(U)$ of the round's updates together with a decision rule that consumes only the \emph{ordering} $S$ induces over clients, which are extreme, which are far from consensus, which are down-weighted. Let $T$ be $d_1$'s per-client transform. Then $(d_1\to d_2)$ is \textbf{mechanism-preserving} exactly when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:general_form}
S(T(U)) \;=\; \varphi\bigl(S(U)\bigr) \quad\text{for some } \varphi \text{ preserving the ordering } d_2 \text{ acts on,}
\end{equation}
which is C2; C3 additionally asks that $\varphi$ not compress the quantitative gap below $d_2$'s operating threshold. Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} is the case $T(u_i)=c_iu_i$: for $S=$ cosine similarity, $\varphi=\mathrm{id}$, so \eqref{eq:general_form} holds unconditionally; for $S=$ coordinate value, $\varphi$ is a per-client positive scaling, order-preserving only under the bounded-ratio condition $\rho < r^\star$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(1); for $S=$ consensus distance or residual magnitude, no $\varphi$ exists, because the reference point (median, geometric median) moves with $T$, so $S(T(U))$ is not a function of $S(U)$ at all. Three consequences worth stating. First, this is why the conditions are checkable without running an experiment: one reads $S$ off $d_2$'s definition and $T$ off $d_1$'s, and the question becomes algebraic. Second, it identifies what a genuinely new instantiation would require: an $S$ and a non-rescaling $T$, e.g.\ a $d_1$ that projects updates onto a subspace or applies a coordinate-wise mask, for which \eqref{eq:general_form} would need to be re-derived rather than inherited. Third, it marks the framework's real boundary: a $d_1$ whose transform is not per-client (one that mixes updates across clients, or carries cross-round state) admits no $T$ of this form, and nothing here applies to it.

\subsection{Breaking the confound: a matched metric-swap ablation}
\label{sec:metric_swap}

Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} says mechanism--effectiveness confounding cannot be escaped by picking a different defense off a menu: in scope, C0$\wedge$C1 force the invariant-signal $d_2$ to be one that suppresses $a^*$ alone. So we break the confound by construction instead, holding the suppression \emph{principle} fixed and varying only the invariance class of the statistic $d_2$ reads.

\textbf{The design's premise was refuted by its own baselines.} Matching failed in both arms, for two independent reasons, and because the C1 inputs are measured before the freeze we learned this before the suite ran: the only reason it can be reported as a finding rather than a post-hoc excuse. \texttt{cos\_reputation} does not merely weaken relative to its Euclidean original, it collapses, from Reputation's $0.017$ on model-scaling to $\mathbf{0.982}$. \texttt{cos\_krum} reaches $0.078$ on model-scaling, which passes C1's numeric threshold, but at $\mathbf{0.146}$ clean accuracy, the hollow-suppression artifact already documented for FoolsGold ($0.200$ at $0.10$) and fg$\to$krum ($0.078$ at $0.099$), so C1 fails under the accuracy gate we apply everywhere else. The mechanism is visible in the baselines rather than asserted: undefended FedAvg under model-scaling is itself at $0.100$ accuracy, so Krum's $0.577$ is \emph{restoring} utility the attack destroyed and \texttt{cos\_krum}'s $0.146$ fails to. \textbf{Magnitude information is what lets this defense family handle a magnitude attack}: a direction-only mirror of a Euclidean scorer cannot see a scaling attack at all, so removing the magnitude dependence removes the suppression with it: exactly the coupling the pre-registered contingency anticipated, stated there as the possibility that ``invariance and standalone effectiveness may be inherently coupled'' for this family. On the magnitude-neutral pixel backdoor the mirror does survive ($0.300$ at $0.598$), but there its control Krum fails C1 ($0.583$), so those arms are unmatched in the opposite direction and the pixel cells cannot carry the test either.

The consequence is that \textbf{the confound re-materialized inside an experiment built specifically to break it}, which we report as the round's methodological finding rather than as a failed run. Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} establishes that mechanism--effectiveness confounding is structural in scope; this is what trying to escape it looks like when the mechanism being varied is the mechanism the defense relies on. Two responses were available and we took neither. We did not switch to a pixel-only protocol, which would have made \texttt{cos\_krum} pass C1 and manufactured two predicted-LOW cells: choosing the attack after seeing the C1 inputs is attack-shopping regardless of the resulting labels. We did not adjust the accuracy gate to admit $0.146$, which would have produced a predicted-LOW pair by weakening the very criterion that catches hollow suppression elsewhere. The pre-registration's second non-negotiable (``no switching of committed attack, threshold, seed count, or accuracy gate'') forecloses both, and either would have bought a positive test at the cost of the property that makes the test worth running.

\textbf{Outcome: 8/8 correct, and zero positive tests.} Because C1 binds for all eight pairs (on model-scaling through the two baseline failures above, on the pixel backdoor by design) every frozen label was HIGH, so the suite bears on specificity only, as a consequence of inputs measured before the freeze rather than a choice made afterwards. We ran all 8 pairs $\times$ 2 attacks $\times$ 3 seeds anyway, because a suite whose labels are all one class is still falsifiable in the direction that matters: a single LOW cell would have been a false positive against a criterion claiming $100\%$ precision. All eight came out HIGH ($8/8$ correct, TP $=0$, FP $=0$, TN $=8$, FN $=0$, max-committed ASR spanning $0.508$--$0.935$) with labels frozen in \texttt{pre\_registration\_metric\_swap.md} at commit \texttt{356897b}, timestamped before \texttt{results/metric\_swap/} existed and reproduced verbatim in the runner's \texttt{PAIRS} list so scoring cannot drift. We state the limitation in the same breath as the result: the suite contains \textbf{no falsifiable prospective positive test}, and $8/8$ is not validation of the framework's predictive claim: eight correct negatives on pairs our own conditions could only have called HIGH is weak evidence, and it supports a claim about specificity, not prediction.

One prediction in the suite was genuinely two-sided, and it is the round's only prospective positive result. \texttt{cos\_krum}'s C1 verdict on model-scaling turns entirely on whether the accuracy gate is applied ($0.078$ passes the numeric threshold; $0.146$ accuracy fails the gate), so rather than choose a reading afterwards we froze \emph{both}, with the resolution rule fixed in advance: accuracy-gated $\Rightarrow$ HIGH, numeric-only $\Rightarrow$ LOW, and a LOW outcome at accuracy $\ge 0.35$ would have recorded our two primary labels as misses. Both pairs came out \textbf{HIGH} (NormClip$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.508$, RFA$\to$\texttt{cos\_krum} $0.566$), refuting the secondary reading and vindicating the accuracy gate against a pre-registered alternative specification of itself on data neither of us had seen. The weaker half is flagged in the supplement (\S~\ref{app:metric_swap_detail}): the NormClip arm sits barely above threshold and it is the RFA arm that carries the conclusion.

\textbf{A post-hoc contrast that is not confounded.} The cross-pair scoring above is confounded in exactly the way Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} predicts, but the frozen suite happens to contain a comparison that is not, and it is the strongest C2 evidence in either paper. Compare a composition against \emph{the same} $d_2$'s own standalone ASR on the same attack, under two different upstream transforms. C1 is then identical by construction (same downstream defense, same attack, same measured standalone effectiveness on both sides), so the residual $\Delta = \mathrm{ASR}(d_1{\to}d_2, a) - \mathrm{ASR}(d_2, a)$ cannot be attributed to $d_2$ being a stronger or weaker defense. What does vary is how much the upstream transform actually disturbs $d_2$'s statistic, and that quantity was measured on the mechanism itself (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}, \texttt{results/cos\_invariance\_check.json}) rather than inferred from any ASR. The frozen label scoring is fixed at $n{=}3$ and must not move, so we topped the six powered cells up to $n{=}5$ in a separate directory, with the tested direction fixed in the top-up script before the new seeds ran (\texttt{results/metric\_swap\_topup/}). For Krum under model-scaling (standalone $0.061$ at $0.577$ accuracy): NormClip changes Krum's selection in $0/9$ rounds and the composition reaches $0.062$ ($\Delta = +0.001$, per-seed $0.120 / 0.018 / 0.047 / 0.058 / 0.068$); RFA changes it in $6/9$ rounds and the composition reaches $0.779$ ($\Delta = +0.718$, per-seed $0.974 / 0.116 / 0.982 / 0.904 / 0.916$): a gap of $+0.716$ between two compositions sharing a downstream defense, an attack, and a baseline, and at $n{=}5$ it is statistically resolved (Welch $t = 4.28$, two-sided $p = 0.012$; paired $t = 4.60$, $p = 0.010$; Mann--Whitney one-sided $p = 0.008$), which it was not at $n{=}3$.

The pattern holds across the suite once cells without power are excluded: $\Delta$ is observable only where standalone $d_2$ genuinely suppresses, since a baseline already above $0.5$ shows $\Delta \approx 0$ by ceiling effect whatever the transform did. Restricting to standalone ASR $< 0.5$ at accuracy $\ge 0.35$ leaves six cells, and at $n{=}5$ measured disturbance still splits them perfectly: the three undisturbed cells ($0/9$) give $\Delta \in [+0.001, +0.190]$, the three disturbed ones ($>0/9$) give $\Delta \in [+0.718, +0.878]$: a separation of $+0.527$ with no overlap. Two of the three undisturbed cells are \texttt{cos\_krum} on the pixel backdoor, where the mirror suppresses standalone ($0.300$ at $0.598$ accuracy) and carries that suppression through under \emph{both} upstreams. Here the two extra seeds \emph{weaken} the confirming half rather than strengthen it, and we report it as measured: $\Delta$ moves from $+0.000$ at $n{=}3$ to $+0.190$ under NormClip and from $-0.027$ to $+0.147$ under RFA, leaving both compositions still below threshold ($0.490$ and $0.447$ at $0.61$ accuracy) but no longer intact. Exact invariance of the \emph{selection} therefore does not by itself carry suppression through unchanged: the magnitude channel of Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} is a second route by which $\Delta$ can grow, and the ladder's arm 3 (§\ref{app:ladder}) is built to measure exactly that prospectively rather than describe it post-hoc. The ten excluded cells are listed rather than dropped silently in the supplement (\S~\ref{app:metric_swap_detail}).

The suite contains a second such contrast, on Reputation under model-scaling (standalone $0.017$ at $0.777$ accuracy), and the two are informative in opposite ways. NormClip disturbs Reputation's weight ordering in $3/9$ rounds and the composition reaches $0.806$; RFA disturbs it in $9/9$ and the composition reaches $0.895$. The gap is only $+0.089$ (both arms are far above threshold, because Reputation's ordering is disturbed under \emph{both} upstreams) but within-arm variance is small, all five seeds move in the predicted direction (per-seed deltas $+0.149 / +0.089 / +0.030 / +0.061 / +0.115$), and it is resolved at both $n{=}3$ and $n{=}5$ (Welch $t = 3.06$, two-sided $p = 0.018$; paired $t = 4.31$, $p = 0.013$). So the two contrasts trade off exactly as the disturbance measurements imply: Krum has the large effect because one of its upstreams leaves it untouched, and Reputation was resolvable even at $n{=}3$ because neither upstream does.

\textbf{This is post-hoc, and we do not present it otherwise.} It was not pre-registered, it re-uses a suite frozen for a different purpose, the two extra seeds per cell were added \emph{after} seeing the $n{=}3$ result, and the six-cell split is a description of that suite rather than a test on new data. The top-up cut in both directions, which is why we report all of it: it resolved the Krum gap (Welch $p = 0.16$ at $n{=}3$, where per-seed paired deltas $+0.854 / +0.097 / +0.935$ left one seed not separating, to $p = 0.012$ at $n{=}5$) and at the same time degraded the invariant control's $\Delta$ from $\approx 0$ to $+0.19$. What the analysis does have, which neither the FLTrust contrasts of §\ref{sec:prospective} nor the cross-pair scoring has, is C1 held exactly fixed and a disturbance measurement taken on the statistic rather than read back from the outcome. We offer it as the cleanest available piece of C2 evidence and as the shape a properly prospective version of this test would take: fix a downstream defense that suppresses the attack, then vary the upstream along measured disturbance rather than along defense identity. Table~\ref{tab:can_cannot} sets out, claim by claim, what this design can and cannot conclude.

\textbf{What follows is a consequence of the identification boundary, not a claim the paper argues for.} Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} says a criterion gated on the outcome it predicts cannot be validated by cross-defense contrast; what such a criterion can still do is \emph{order an evaluation budget}, and that is the only use we claim for it. \textbf{Does every condition earn its place? C3 does not: reported as a finding.} Scoring all 42 pairs on C1, C2 and C3 \emph{independently} rather than recording only the first failure: against a $12\%$ base rate of low-ASR pairs, C1 alone selects 4 at $50\%$ precision, C2 alone 14 at $36\%$, and C1$\wedge$C2 selects 2 at $100\%$. \textbf{The surviving C2 failures are exactly the non-invariant signal class}: after the Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} reclassification every one of them is a $\cdot\to$reputation pair (nc$\to$rep $0.819$, rfa$\to$rep $0.882$, fg$\to$rep $0.798$), consensus distance being the signal a positive rescaling does not preserve, while all three non-degenerate $\cdot\to$FoolsGold pairs are C1 failures instead. \textbf{C3 has no demonstrated incremental predictive value in this study}: alone it selects 35 pairs at $9\%$ precision, below the base rate, and it never binds on a pair C1$\wedge$C2 already admits. So \textbf{we demote C3 from a gate to a margin diagnostic and recommend C1$\wedge$C2 as the criterion}, retaining C3 on theoretical grounds only: Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} shows margin compression is a real failure mode, and the $\alpha{=}0.1$ regime below is precisely a margin collapse. \textbf{The demotion changes no number}, C1$\wedge$C2 selects the same two pairs at the same precision (recomputed by \texttt{analyze\_screening\_cost.py}), which is why we report it as a finding about the criterion rather than a revision of results. \emph{The pre-registered PASS/FAIL labels and the $16/18$ and $22/24$ tallies were frozen with C3 in the gate and are not relabelled}; this ablation is post-hoc and labelled as such, and the recommendation is for use going forward.

\subsection{Out-of-Sample PASS Validation}
\label{sec:oos_pass}

The development and held-out validation above tests predictions from the \emph{same} attack families (model\_scaling, backdoor\_pixel) and scale ($N{=}10$, cifar\_cnn) used to design the criterion. To confirm that PASS predictions generalize we pre-registered three out-of-sample tests (commit \texttt{ca627e5}, before any of them ran); all held, the accounting is in the summary below, and per-cell values for every test are in the supplement (§\ref{app:oos_detail}). We state plainly what this is and is not: it is evidence that the conditions keep identifying compositions which survive a change of attack family, scale and architecture; it is \emph{not} an out-of-sample classification result, because every prediction in it is positive and the matching FAIL controls proved uninformative. Negative-direction evidence rests entirely on the in-distribution tiers.

\textbf{(E) Heterogeneity: where the criterion breaks.} Earlier heterogeneity sweeps in this work covered \emph{single defenses} only; no composition had been run at any $\alpha \neq 0.5$. Two caveats cut in opposite directions: fg$\to$rfa at $\alpha{=}0.1$ is strongly bimodal across seeds ($0.065$--$0.923$), so at $n{=}3$ the direction is clear but the magnitude is not; and clean accuracy collapses to $0.20$--$0.48$, so this regime stresses utility as much as security. Crucially Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} supplies a \textbf{diagnostic quantity that retrospectively explains this boundary} rather than predicting it: re-measuring on the transformed points at $\alpha{=}0.1$, RFA's separation margin $m := \Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}}/[2(R'_B+\delta')]$ collapses from an all-round mean of $3.24$ (met in 68\% of rounds) to $1.01$ (met in 20\%). Over the payload-carrying rounds alone (the ones Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism} plots) the same collapse reads $4.71 \pm 1.51$ ($n{=}17$) to $2.06 \pm 2.35$ ($n{=}12$), with the per-round minimum falling $2.82 \to 0.00$; the all-round means are lower because they average in the Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation} rounds, where FoolsGold zeroes every adversary and $m$ is recorded as $0$. Either way the collapse is measured, and the all-round figure is the one that reaches the vacuous limit. This sits exactly at the $m \to 1$ boundary where Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass}'s bound degrades to $40\%$ adversarial mass: extreme heterogeneity inflates the benign radius $R'_B$ until the benign cluster is no longer separated from the adversarial points, which is precisely the precondition the theorem requires. A practitioner running the 5--10 round pilot would measure $m \approx 1$ and decline to deploy, so the criterion is mechanistically diagnostic at this boundary, though we emphasise this is a post-hoc measurement of a known failure, not a pre-registered prediction of it.

The one axis not validated with composition experiments is \emph{dataset} (CIFAR-100, with 100-class output): the conditions are dataset-agnostic in mechanism, but the downstream aggregator's decision boundary may shift with the number of classes (L1).

\textbf{The abscissa is measured, not nominal.} What matters to C2 is not $\rho$ but what the rescaling does to the statistic $d_2$ actually reads, so before freezing any prediction we pushed live FL updates through the shipped transform and recorded each arm's decision at every $\kappa$ on the \emph{same} raw updates, computing no ASR. That establishes three things before any outcome exists (Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}): \emph{there is a dose}: disturbance is exactly $0$ at the identity rung and rises monotonically with $\kappa$ in every non-invariant arm; \emph{the class-(a) invariance claim holds in the shipped code}: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is unchanged in $\mathbf{0}$ of $120$ cells at $\rho$ up to $54.6$; and \emph{class (b) is graded rather than binary}, at roughly half the class-(c) disturbance ($0.487$ vs.\ $0.867$/$0.933$ at $\kappa{=}2.0$), which is what arm 4's prediction turns on.

\textbf{Frozen predictions.} The \emph{primary} test is pooled: across all 16 $(\text{arm},\kappa)$ cells, the rise in ASR over that arm's own $\kappa{=}0$ rung is predicted to be positively rank-correlated with the measured disturbance rate (Spearman, one-sided, $\alpha = 0.05$). Its predictor is a \emph{measured property of the statistic}, not the identity of the defense and not its standalone effectiveness, both held fixed within every arm. The \emph{secondary} predictions are the per-arm shapes of Table~\ref{tab:dose_design}, tested by a one-sided Jonckheere--Terpstra trend test across the four ordered rungs at $n{=}5$, with arm 3's ``flat'' scored as an \emph{equivalence} claim at a $0.15$ margin fixed and justified in advance (under a quarter of the $+0.629$ within-defense gap for Krum (the $n{=}3$ value on record at the freeze; the later top-up puts it at $+0.716$, so the margin is if anything conservative) and $0.300+0.15 = 0.450 < 0.5$, so ``within the margin'' and ``still suppressing'' coincide for this arm) and arm 4's class-(b) signature being the \emph{shallower slope}.

\textbf{One prediction we deliberately do not make.} Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}(1)'s ordering-survival condition $\rho < S$ would give arm 4 a crossing rung fixed by a measured $S$, but \textbf{no frozen prediction here uses $S$}: we measured it and it is \textbf{not a stable per-attack constant}, ranging $0.00$--$12.59$ on model-scaling (median $0.57$) and $0.61$--$1.63$ on the pixel backdoor (median $0.92$) across the 12 rounds that sampled an adversary, so the $S = 10$ used illustratively in §\ref{sec:restricted_theorem} is a stipulation rather than a measurement. This is a limitation of our own theorem that the dose-response measurement surfaced, reported as one (L5) rather than quietly left unchecked.

\textbf{Arm 2 confirms: the ladder yields one prospective confirmation, while the pooled prediction is rejected.} Reputation under model-scaling rises monotonically from $0.017$ at the identity rung to $0.487$ at $\rho = 7.39$ and $0.779$ at $\rho = 54.6$ (Jonckheere--Terpstra $z = +4.64$, one-sided $p = 1.7\times10^{-6}$; permutation $p < 5\times10^{-5}$), while clean accuracy moves only $0.777 \to 0.701$, so what is lost is suppression, not the model. Same $d_2$, same attack, same standalone baseline at every rung: nothing about effectiveness varies, and $55\times$ dispersion in a \emph{positive per-client rescaling} (a transform the criterion treats as benign for invariant statistics) carries a defense from $0.017$ across the $0.5$ threshold. Two qualifications keep this in proportion. It validates the \emph{destruction} half of C2's mechanism (a non-invariant statistic, actually disturbed, loses suppression); the \emph{preservation} half (invariance $\Rightarrow$ suppression survives) is arm 3, which came out indeterminate. And even within this arm the measured disturbance is not what orders the curve: it saturates at $0.867$ by $\kappa{=}0.5$, where ASR is still $0.022$, and the jump to $0.487$ happens at the next rung at the same measured disturbance, so the \emph{dose} moves ASR here while the flip rate does not.

\textbf{Ecological validity: the real transforms do not lie on the synthetic curve.} Pre-registered caveat (iv) required this check, and it fails in an informative way. RFA$\to$Krum sits at ASR $0.779$ with measured disturbance $0.667$, where the synthetic ladder's nearest rung ($0.600$) gives $0.011$: a residual of $+0.767$. NormClip$\to$Reputation sits at $0.806$ with disturbance $0.333$, against $0.017$ at the identity rung: $+0.789$. Two anchors do land: RFA$\to$Reputation, $0.895$ at disturbance $1.000$ against $0.779$ at $0.933$ ($+0.116$), and both \texttt{cos\_krum} anchors, which coincide with its curve ($+0.000$ and $-0.044$) because that statistic is invariant under all three transforms. This is not a range mismatch: read off the same live rounds, NormClip realizes $\rho$ of mean $3.61$ (max $4.90$) and RFA mean $11.93$ (max $17.38$), both inside the ladder's $\{1.00, 2.72, 7.39, 54.60\}$ span. The conclusion is about our abscissa: \textbf{the rate at which a transform flips a defense's decision is not a sufficient statistic for what that transform does to the defense}: two transforms at matched flip rate differ by up to $0.77$ in ASR. \textbf{And the axis they \emph{are} unmatched on is now measured, not conjectured.} Adversary-correlated dispersion $\Delta_c := \mathbb{E}[c_i \mid i \in A] - \mathbb{E}[c_i \mid i \notin A]$ (§\ref{sec:composability_criterion}) separates exactly these two anchors from the ladder: RFA reaches $\Delta_c = -1.21$ and NormClip $-0.72$, against $-0.34$ at the ladder's most dispersed rung: $2$--$3.6\times$ more extremely targeted at $\rho$ that is $3.7$ and $13\times$ \emph{smaller}, so \textbf{matched on the statistic, badly unmatched on the targeting, and $\rho$ does not order $\Delta_c$}. \textbf{This rescues no predictor}: $\Delta_c$'s magnitude does not order suppression either; RFA is the most extremely targeted of the four real transforms and rfa$\to$krum still fails at $0.779$, while FoolsGold's $\Delta_c$ is \emph{positive} ($+0.663$). These figures are \textbf{post-hoc and exploratory}, computed with no ASR and rescoring nothing, and are tabulated in the supplement (\S~\ref{app:delta_c}). Arm 4 has no real-transform anchor at all, since \texttt{coord\_median} was not on the metric-swap menu, so its ecological validity is untested.

\textbf{What we now claim less of.} C2's causal content is established prospectively for one statistic (distance to the client median) and refuted for another (summed pairwise distance) at $\rho$ up to $54.6$; the pooled claim that measured disturbance predicts loss of suppression \emph{across} defenses is refuted, and we no longer make it. Within-defense disturbance remains the only design the testability boundary admits, and §\ref{app:boundary}'s general statement stands untouched by this outcome; it is a statement about admissible designs, and this is the admissible design, run and reported as it landed. What changes is the empirical status of C2: one confirmed arm, not a validated screening mechanism.

\subsection{The targeted intervention: isolating C2 from the attenuation channel}
\label{app:targeted_full}
\label{app:channel_table}

\textbf{Reading \texttt{cos\_krum}'s entry in Table~\ref{tab:channels}.} Its $-0.425$ is the full-ladder endpoint, which lies below our accuracy floor; \textbf{the witness we rest on is instead its first, gate-passing rung} ($0.173$, §\ref{sec:targeted_rest}). The rank discordance between $\Delta\Lambda_a$ and $\Delta$ASR across the two moving arms is §\ref{sec:targeted_rest}'s refutation of our own predicted ordering. \textbf{What Mode~S does to the ladder's confirmation.} Reputation was the ladder's one confirmed arm (§\ref{sec:dose_outcome}); Mode~S shows most of that confirmation was attenuation. With the adversary's share held fixed, reputation's rise at $\rho{=}54.6$ shrinks from the ladder's $+0.762$ to $+0.178$ (one-sided Welch $t{=}-5.22$, $p{=}0.0017$ that it is smaller), and what remains tracks a small \emph{admission} change ($0.000 \to 0.020$) rather than the large decision change ($0.000 \to 1.000$), so H-admission is confirmed here too (Table~\ref{tab:targeted}). The confound is thus \emph{explained}, not explained away: a genuine, much smaller, admission-driven effect survives it. The class-(a) control cuts the other way and refutes us: \texttt{cos\_krum}'s selection is bit-identical at every rung (decision and admission both $0.000$), yet ASR is $0.584$ at the identity (it never suppresses) and \emph{falls} $0.425$ across the ladder ($p_{\downarrow}{=}0.0008$; the $\kappa{=}2$ rung is below the accuracy gate). Exact invariance of the statistic neither implies suppression nor holds it fixed; the magnitude channel moves it regardless. We report this as a refutation of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}'s practical content, not a confirmation of its algebra.

\textbf{Mode A: the two regimes, and where they hold.} The payload dose gives Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} a single falsifiable shape: ASR single-peaked at $r{=}1$, since an over-attenuated adversary carries no payload (Regime~B) and an over-amplified one is discarded by a rank aggregator (Regime~A). It is \textbf{confirmed for \texttt{coord\_median}}, the rank aggregator the regimes are stated for: ASR rises $0.124 \to 0.315 \to 0.443$ into $r{=}1$ and falls $0.443 \to 0.296 \to 0.098$ past it, both sides significant ($p_{\downarrow}{=}3.0{\times}10^{-4}$ amplified, $6.4{\times}10^{-4}$ attenuated) at accuracy $\ge 0.74$ throughout (Figure~\ref{fig:modeA}). It is \textbf{refuted for reputation}, which is monotone: ASR is maximal at the most attenuated rung ($0.894$ at $r{=}0.135$) and near zero once $r \ge 1$, because an attenuated adversary reads as \emph{consistent} to a reputation score and stops being down-weighted. Reputation is not a rank aggregator, so the two-regime shape was never licensed for it; the split is the theorem behaving exactly where it applies and not where it does not.

\paragraph{A pre-registered replication on a second downstream defense, and what it refutes.} One arm cannot establish which channel matters, and the two Mode-S arms above move together on both candidate predictors: reputation is higher on \emph{both} decision and admission change and also had the larger rise, so nothing in that pair separates them. \texttt{coord\_median} under the pixel backdoor is \emph{rank-discordant} and therefore does: of the interpretable Mode-S arms it has the \textbf{smallest decision change} ($0.482$) and the \textbf{largest admission change} ($0.033$), so the decision reading predicts its rise is the \emph{smallest} of the three and the admission reading predicts the \emph{largest}. \paragraph{The confounded design gets the causal sign wrong.} This is the paper's central empirical result. On this identical cell (matched aggregate displacement, decision change and clean accuracy, Table~\ref{tab:comparability}), \textbf{the confounded design does not lose power, it gets the sign wrong}: the outcome-gated ladder moves $-0.272$ ($p_{\downarrow}{=}0.0015$; $0.443 \to 0.170$) where the instrument moves $+0.098$. Closing the attenuation channel does not sharpen a weak positive effect, it \textbf{inverts it}, so a criterion calibrated against the confounded contrast would have been calibrated against the confound, and a cross-defense contrast would have read the fall as evidence about the statistic. That is why the identification boundary of §\ref{app:boundary} is not a technicality about power.

\paragraph{What the two arms jointly establish, stated formally.} The arms above and the class-(a) control (\texttt{cos\_krum}) are between them a \emph{constructive} answer to the question C2 was written to settle. Both halves are witnessed by runs already reported, so the following formalizes measurements rather than adding a claim.

\begin{proposition}[Statistic-level non-identifiability]
\label{prop:nonidentifiability}
Within the positive per-client rescaling class there exist transform families $T_1,T_2$ and downstream defenses $d_2$ such that
(a) $T_1$ and $T_2$ induce arbitrarily different statistics and \emph{decisions} at $d_2$ while inducing the \emph{same} admitted adversarial mass and the same suppression; and
(b) $T_1$ and $T_2$ induce the \emph{same} admitted adversarial mass (indeed the same statistic and the same decision) while inducing \emph{different} suppression.
Consequently no predicate of the downstream statistic alone determines the admitted adversarial mass, and no predicate of the admitted mass alone determines suppression.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Both halves are exhibited by runs reported in this section. (a) Mode~S into Krum under model-scaling, $\kappa{=}0$ against $\kappa{=}2$: $\rho$ moves from $1$ to $54.6$ and Krum's decision changes in up to $0.80$ of rounds, while the support of the admitted adversarial mass changes in none of the $60$ measured rounds (a floor on this arm: no adversary is admitted at any rung), the adversarial coefficient share is constant at $0.266667$, and suppression shows \textbf{no evidence of a practically meaningful change under the pre-specified $\pm0.15$ equivalence margin} ($|\Delta|{=}0.026$). (b) \texttt{cos\_krum} under the pixel backdoor: by Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}(a) its statistic is invariant, and its decision and admission are measured bit-identical ($0.000$) at every rung, yet ASR falls $0.173$ by the first rung alone, at $\rho{=}2.72$, where the accuracy gate still passes ($0.599 \to 0.567$; paired $t{=}{-}1.976$, one-sided $p{=}0.044$, falling in $5/8$ seeds).
\end{proof}

\noindent \textbf{The corollary is the paper's title claim}: no metric defined \emph{solely} on preservation of the downstream statistic can identify security equivalence, because (a) exhibits statistic disturbance without security change and (b) exhibits security change without statistic disturbance. \textbf{Scope, stated immediately}: this is a non-existence claim \emph{within the positive-rescaling class on these defenses and attacks}, established by exhibited counterexample: \textbf{not} a claim that no metric of any kind can order security, and not a claim about transforms outside the class.

\begin{proposition}[$\rho$ and $\Delta_c$ are independently controllable]
\label{prop:rho_deltac}
Within the same class, the statistic-level dispersion $\rho = \max_i c_i/\min_i c_i$ and the adversary-correlated dispersion $\Delta_c = \mathbb{E}[c_i \mid i \in A] - \mathbb{E}[c_i \mid i \notin A]$ are not functionally related: there is a family with $\Delta_c \equiv 0$ exactly while $\rho$ ranges over $[1, 54.6]$, and a family with the statistic's dispersion held fixed while $\Delta_c$ ranges over $[-1.138, +2.469]$. Hence neither quantity determines the other.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By construction, with the realized values tabulated in the supplement (\S~\ref{app:delta_c}) and computed with no ASR involved. Mode~S pins every adversarial coefficient at $c{=}1$ and spreads only the benign clients, giving $\Delta_c \equiv 0$ at every $\rho$ up to $54.6$; Mode~A holds the benign weights uniform and sweeps only the adversary-to-benign ratio $r$, moving $\Delta_c$ over $[-1.138,+2.469]$ at fixed statistic dispersion.
\end{proof}

\noindent This is why the two instruments were the right ones, and it makes the supplement's §\ref{sec:rho_vs_deltac} warning precise: \textbf{a transform reported by $\rho$ alone leaves $\Delta_c$ entirely free}, so $\rho$ cannot be a sufficient statistic for what the transform does to a defense. It does \emph{not} follow that $\Delta_c$ is a predictor: §\ref{sec:dose_outcome} reports that its magnitude fails to order suppression either.

\textbf{Harness sanity, which is an identity check and not corroboration.} The $\kappa{=}0$ rung is identity$\circ$Krum, i.e.\ Krum alone, and it lands \emph{bit-identically} on the EMNIST-byclass Krum figure from our earlier payoff-matrix sweep ($0.0269784552$ at accuracy $0.7670795973$). Both pipelines are deterministic given a seed and both run three trials from base seed $42$, so the agreement shows they execute the same protocol rather than a similar one: the strongest form the pre-registered check can take, and for the same reason \textbf{not} independent corroboration of that figure: the same computation run twice cannot corroborate itself.

\textbf{What the instrument does \emph{not} hold fixed, named in full, and what it pins exactly.} Mode~S pins the \emph{adversarial} coefficients; it does not pin the benign ones and was never claimed to. So the dose also moves (a) Krum's pairwise distances, hence the statistic itself; (b) \emph{which} benign client Krum selects, in $0.533/0.800/0.733$ of rounds at $\rho{=}2.72/7.39/54.6$; (c) the \textbf{magnitude and geometry of the update actually aggregated}, since the selected client enters scaled by its own coefficient; and (d) the model trajectory, which diverges across rungs in consequence. Channel (c) is not a conjecture but a \textbf{number we already measure and print}: the $\Delta$~agg.\ column of Table~\ref{tab:channels_full} is $\|\mathrm{agg}(T(U)) - \mathrm{agg}(U)\|/\|\mathrm{agg}(U)\|$, the relative displacement of the aggregate the defense \emph{emits}, and it reads $\mathbf{0.892}$ on this cell. The supplement \S~\ref{app:decomposition} splits that $0.892$, as vectors, into $0.986$ of \emph{re-selection} (a different benign client is chosen, and in $0/11$ of the rounds where that happens it is an adversary) and $0.973$ of \emph{rescaling} (the chosen client arrives scaled by its own coefficient); for \texttt{cos\_krum} the displacement is \emph{entirely} rescaling, since $0/15$ selections change at any rung. The claim this arm licenses is therefore sharper than a claim about the statistic alone: \textbf{a $0.892$ displacement of the emitted aggregate, together with decision changes in up to $80\%$ of rounds, leaves suppression inside the pre-specified equivalence margin, provided the adversarial coefficient share does not move.} \textbf{What is pinned is pinned exactly}: $c_{\mathrm{adv}}{=}1.0$ at every rung with maximum deviation $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$ and the share constant to $3.5\mathrm{e}{-}07$ on CIFAR-10, $3.9\mathrm{e}{-}07$ on EMNIST-byclass: float32 norm read-back noise, not slack in the instrument (\texttt{measure\_admission.py}). Naming (a)--(d) is what keeps the claim to the one channel the instrument closes by construction.

\paragraph{Closing channel (c) as well: the pre-registered score-only control.} Channel (c) (the magnitude and geometry of what is actually aggregated) is the one confound Mode~S leaves open, so we closed it too rather than argue about it. \textbf{Score-only Krum} scores on the transformed stack exactly as before and then aggregates the \emph{untransformed} selected update: the dose can change \emph{which} client contributes and nothing whatever about \emph{what} that client contributes. Statistic and decision are disturbed identically to the uncontrolled arm (the selection is computed on the same transformed stack, so $\Delta$~dec.\ is the same $0.733$) while $\Delta$~agg.\ is no longer $0.892$ but the re-selection component alone, $0.986$ relative to $\|u_{s_0}\|$ (the supplement \S~\ref{app:decomposition}), the rescaling component being closed by construction. This is the reviewer-suggested control in its own terms: hold the selected update's magnitude and direction fixed while perturbing the selection statistic independently.

\textbf{Frozen before the arm existed.} The decision rule is \texttt{experiments/} \texttt{pre\_registration\_score\_only.md}, git-committed at \texttt{35788d9} before any score-only ASR existed, and the runner refuses to start unless its own \texttt{PREREG\_COMMIT} matches that hash. \emph{Primary:} $\Delta = {}$mean ASR$(\kappa{=}2) - {}$mean ASR$(\kappa{=}0)$ against the suite's existing $\pm 0.15$ equivalence margin, on \texttt{krum} / \texttt{committed\_scaling} / CIFAR-10 / \texttt{cifar\_cnn}, the same four rungs and seeds 42--46. $\Delta > +0.15$ would have said that \textbf{the published flat result was partly an artifact of the magnitude channel} and that the paper's central claim narrows to ``with benign magnitudes free to move'': that branch is why the arm was worth three hours of compute. The $\kappa{=}0$ rung is imported rather than re-run, because at the identity the transform returns the stack unwrapped and score-only Krum \emph{is} Krum alone; that is an exact claim, not an approximation, and it was verified bit-identically at one seed before the arm started.

\textbf{The negative survives with the magnitude channel closed.} $\Delta = \mathbf{-0.023}$ (mean ASR $0.062 \to 0.039$) against $-0.026$ for the uncontrolled arm on the identical cell: \textbf{closing the magnitude channel moves the effect by $0.003$}. The refuting threshold $0.212$ is nowhere approached, and mean clean accuracy clears the $0.35$ floor at every rung ($0.565/0.629/0.624/0.630$), so no rung is a collapsed model. \textbf{The central claim is therefore instrumented against two independent confounds} (attenuation, closed by Mode~S, and magnitude, closed here) and the sharpened statement is that a $0.986$ displacement of the emitted aggregate together with decision changes in $0.733$ of rounds leaves suppression inside the pre-specified equivalence margin.

\textbf{Three disclosures, from the freeze rather than from hindsight.} (i) The secondary Jonckheere--Terpstra test was specified to detect a monotone \emph{rise} (the outcome that would have refuted flatness with attenuation already closed) and the observed trend is a \emph{fall} ($J{=}58.5$, $z{=}-1.110$, $p_{\uparrow}{=}0.867$, permutation $p_{\uparrow}{=}0.874$). We report it and do \textbf{not} score it as support for flatness; the pre-registration states in advance that at $n{=}5$ a null from this test is not evidence of flatness. (ii) The two branches had unequal headroom. With the identity rung at $0.062$, refuting us required mean ASR$(\kappa{=}2) > 0.212$ against a ceiling of $1$ and was fully reachable, whereas the indeterminate branch ($\Delta < -0.15$) was \emph{arithmetically unreachable}: ASR cannot fall below $0$, so the largest possible fall on this cell is $0.062$. (iii) \textbf{Selectors only.} A weighted averager and a coordinate-wise order statistic emit no single selected client, so ``the selected update'' is undefined for them and the control does not cover them, which means the \texttt{coord\_median} arm carrying the $+0.098$ rise is \textbf{not} magnitude-controlled, and neither is reputation's $+0.178$. Score-only Krum is also an instrument and not a defense: it aggregates an update its own scoring stage never saw.

\textbf{The corollary the pre-registration staked on \texttt{cos\_krum}, and exactly what it settles.} Section~5 of the same document asserted, before the run, that score-only \texttt{cos\_krum} must be \emph{bit-identical} across rungs: its statistic is exactly invariant under positive rescaling, so the selection cannot move, and with the emitted update untransformed the whole 50-round trajectory must repeat. It does (accuracy $0.528400$ and ASR $0.003111$ at all four rungs, $|\Delta| = 0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$ throughout) and the $\kappa{=}0$ value reproduces the published cell at that seed exactly, so the two pipelines agree at $0.000000$ rather than approximately. \textbf{What the check does not do is confirm the arm's published fall.} It runs one seed, as frozen, and that seed is unrepresentative of the arm: under the uncontrolled dose its own ladder \emph{rises} $+0.224$ where the eight-seed mean \emph{falls} $-0.425$. Closing the magnitude channel removes all $0.224$ of that seed's movement, which corroborates the \emph{channel} (for \texttt{cos\_krum} the emitted displacement is entirely rescaling, so no other route is open) but attributing the arm's mean fall to magnitude rests on that decomposition rather than on this check, and settling it empirically would need the arm's other seven seeds. Expanding a pre-registered seed count after seeing the data is precisely what pre-registration exists to prevent, so we report the invariance as confirmed and the arm-level attribution as \textbf{corroborated mechanically, not established at the arm mean}.

\subsubsection{The 2$\times$2 channel factorial, and the decomposition it refuses to license}
\label{app:factorial}
Mode~S opens two channels into a selector: the \emph{statistic} channel (the dose moves Krum's pairwise distances, so \emph{which} client is selected changes) and the \emph{magnitude} channel (the selected client enters scaled by its own coefficient). Score-only closes the second. The fourth cell closes the first instead: \textbf{emit-only Krum} scores the \emph{untransformed} stack, pinning the decision to the identity rung's, and aggregates the \emph{transformed} selected update. The four cells are then a factorial, and if the two channels acted independently the three measured effects would satisfy $\Delta_{\mathrm{full}} = \Delta_{\mathrm{SO}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{EO}}$. \textbf{They do not.} Rules were frozen in \texttt{experiments/} \texttt{pre\_registration\_emit\_only.md} at \texttt{b995f1b} before any emit-only ASR existed, with the runner refusing to start unless its own \texttt{PREREG\_COMMIT} matches that hash; the $\kappa{=}0$ rung is imported rather than re-run, because at the identity the transform returns the stack unwrapped and emit-only \emph{is} Krum alone, an exact claim verified bit-identically before the arm started. All three harness checks the freeze required pass at $0.00\mathrm{e}{+}00$ across five published arms and the score-only control, so the refactor that added this cell perturbed no published computation.

\noindent \textbf{The primary rung is forced, and the additive model gets the sign wrong.} At $\kappa{=}2$ mean clean accuracy is $0.333$, below the $0.35$ floor, so the primary contrast is reported at $\kappa{=}1$ and \textbf{that substitution is forced by the floor rather than chosen after seeing ASR}: Section~5 of the pre-registration named $\kappa{=}2$ as the rung at risk, and named it for this cell specifically, because emit-only aggregates the \emph{rescaled} update and so degrades training in a way the other three cells do not. Seed-matched at $\kappa{=}1$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}} = +0.046$ sits inside the frozen inert margin of $0.05$, so \textbf{the magnitude channel is inert on its own}, which is what we predicted. But $\Delta_{\mathrm{SO}} + \Delta_{\mathrm{EO}} = +0.016$ against a measured $\Delta_{\mathrm{full}} = -0.039$: an additivity residual of $-0.054$, outside the frozen $0.05$ margin, and \textbf{the additive model does not merely mis-size the joint effect, it reverses its sign}. So \textbf{no per-channel decomposition of the uncontrolled arm is licensed}, and we withdraw any reading of the re-selection/rescaling split as a decomposition of suppression. That split remains sound as the thing it says it is: the supplement's algebraic decomposition (\S~\ref{app:decomposition}) decomposes a \emph{displacement}, and says in its own terms that it makes no claim about suppression.

\noindent \textbf{Three reasons this verdict is fragile, all of them ours to report.} (i) It is knife-edge on both margins at once: $|\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}}|$ falls \emph{inside} the inert margin by $0.004$ and the residual falls \emph{outside} the additivity margin by $0.004$, so a perturbation of that size in either quantity would flip that half of the verdict. (ii) Both pre-registered secondaries are null: the per-seed sign count for emit-only is $3$ up against $2$ down, and the Jonckheere--Terpstra trend across the four rungs gives $z = -0.437$, $p_{\uparrow} = 0.669$, so there is no monotone trend to report. (iii) One seed carries the whole result. Seed~42's emit-only run collapses to clean accuracy $0.2613$, below our own floor \emph{at that seed} though the rung's mean of $0.487$ clears it, and its $\Delta$ is $+0.272$ against $-0.007$, $+0.007$, $+0.013$ and $-0.058$ for the other four; excluding it would give $\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}} = -0.011$ and a residual of $-0.003$, which would read as separable. \textbf{We do not exclude it}: Section~5 fixes $n{=}5$ on seeds $42$--$46$ and applies the floor to a rung's \emph{mean} rather than per seed, so no per-seed exclusion is licensed, and the pre-registered top-up settled it without one. \textbf{At $n{=}20$ the knife edge is gone and the verdict it produced is withdrawn}: $\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}} = +0.010$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.028,+0.047]$, so the magnitude channel is \emph{established} inert, and the residual is $-0.019$, $[-0.061,+0.023]$, inside the additivity margin, so the interaction reading does not survive. Seed~42 remains the only draw of $20$ past either outlier threshold and is still excluded from nothing; realized sd falls $0.130 \to 0.080$. Separability is now \emph{consistent with} and \textbf{still not established}, its half-width $0.042$ against a $0.05$ margin, exactly as the top-up's own pre-registration projected in advance would be unavoidable at this variance, and dropping the one seed that produces the unwelcome answer is precisely what pre-registration exists to prevent. Section~6.1 of the same document declared, before the run, that at $n{=}5$ only a large $|\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}}|$ would be decisive, and $|\Delta_{\mathrm{EO}}|$ is not large. \textbf{We therefore report this arm as not decisive, and act on it only in the direction that withdraws a claim}: low power is a reason to stop asserting a decomposition, never a reason to assert one. Every number above is emitted by \texttt{experiments/} \texttt{analyze\_emit\_only\_factorial.py}, which reads only frozen artifacts and asserts that all four primary quantities reproduce from the per-seed cells to $<10^{-9}$ before printing anything. \textbf{Selectors only}: the factorial is defined for \texttt{krum} and \texttt{cos\_krum} and the harness raises for any other $d_2$, so it is a within-family result on one selector, one attack, one dataset and one architecture.

\textbf{What this settles, and what it does not.} The intervention delivers the controlled isolation of C2 the two cross-defense suites could not: within a single defense, with attenuation excluded by construction \emph{and}, in the score-only arm, the magnitude of the aggregated update held exactly fixed as well, \textbf{substantial disturbance of the statistic $d_2$ reads need not produce a corresponding change in suppression once the adversarial coefficient share is held fixed} (on the adjudicating Krum arm under Mode~S and, by pre-registered replication, both on a downstream defense in a different invariance class and on a second dataset and architecture) while \emph{admission} of adversarial input is the channel the evidence points to. The statistic-level reading the framework was first written in is refuted; the admission-level reading is confirmed as a \emph{direction} in these interventions rather than established in general. It does \emph{not} rescue the framework's positive direction wholesale: the pooled two-channel model over all $26$ cells is \textbf{not} confirmed ($R^2{=}0.239$), and the replication refutes its cross-arm ordering outright, so admission is a better single predictor than the statistic without being a quantitative one. It addresses neither recall nor discovery (FG$\to$RFA remains a false negative), and nothing in §\ref{sec:dose_outcome} is rescored: its pooled refutation, its two refuted arms and its indeterminate arm stand exactly as reported.
\paragraph{Mode~M: coordinate masking, a second transformation class.} Every other arm in this paper transforms client updates by a positive per-client scalar, the family Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance} classifies and Thm.~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} bounds. \textbf{Mode~M} is outside it. Benign client $i$ has a random $m$ fraction of the coordinates of each parameter tensor zeroed, drawn from an independent Bernoulli$(1{-}m)$ seeded by \texttt{default\_rng([seed, round, slot])} so it is reproducible and uncorrelated with adversary status, and the surviving coordinates are then \textbf{renormalized to the update's original $L_2$ norm}. Renormalization is load-bearing and was required in the pre-registration \emph{before any ASR existed}: an unnormalized mask shrinks benign norms and re-opens the magnitude channel that \S\ref{app:score_only}'s control exists to close. The unnormalized variant was considered and rejected for that reason, and the rejection is recorded rather than omitted. \textbf{Adversarial updates are returned unmodified, exactly}: the maximum deviation of every adversarial coefficient from $1$ is $0.00$ with object identity preserved, a strictly stronger pinning than Mode~S's $3.5{\times}10^{-7}$ float32 read-back. Rungs are $m \in \{0, 0.2, 0.5, 0.8\}$ and $m{=}0$ returns the update list unwrapped, so the identity rung is bit-identical to Krum standalone by construction; we check rather than assert this, the identity rung agreeing to $10^{-9}$ across all three directories that carry those seeds.

\textbf{The channels were measured before any ASR existed}, as the EMNIST-byclass arm's were. Krum's decision changes in $0.000/0.133/0.400/0.467$ of rounds across the ladder while the adversarial mass it admits is $0.000$ at every rung, at an aggregate displacement of $1.095$ against Mode~S's $0.892$ at a \emph{larger} decision change. So the arm was established prospectively as a generalization test, and the pre-registration (\texttt{684b31e}) then split four ways, including two that would have refuted us: a monotone rise in $m$ would confirm the statistic reading, and masking that made Krum admit the adversary would void the equivalence claim at that rung and confirm the \emph{admission} reading. \textbf{Neither fired.} At $n{=}10$ (seeds $42$--$51$) the paired contrast $m{=}0 \to m{=}0.8$ is $\Delta=-0.016$, $95\%$ CI $[-0.051,+0.019]$, contained in the frozen $\pm0.15$ margin at a half-width $23.1\%$ of it (TOST $p{=}5.3{\times}10^{-6}$), every rung mean clearing the $0.5$ ceiling and the $0.35$ accuracy floor, and Jonckheere--Terpstra finding no increasing trend ($p_{\uparrow}{=}0.895$). The mask analogue of the score-only control gives $-0.018$, $[-0.047,+0.010]$, agreeing with the full ladder to $0.002$: since adversarial updates are unmasked in \emph{both} conditions the two ladders differ only when a benign client is selected, so their agreement localizes the absent effect to \emph{which client Krum picks} rather than to the content it emits.

\textbf{What this arm does and does not license.} Only the flat-with-admission-unchanged outcome generalizes the negative, and that is the outcome observed; had admission moved, the arm would have been reported as a confirmation of the admission reading instead. \textbf{One mask is one transformation, not the class of non-rescalings}: rotations, projections, quantization and sign compression are untested, so the supportable claim is that the negative replicates on a second, structurally different transformation class, never that it holds for arbitrary upstream processing. Mode~M has nothing to impose in $24/120$ measured rung-rounds, identical to Mode~S's figure, and like Mode~S it is an \emph{instrument} and not a defense, since it reads adversary identity. The \texttt{cos\_krum} mask ladder was considered and dropped for compute before any ASR existed, which is why the masking column carries one outcome and three channel-only entries.

\subsection{Six cells, and a pre-registered mechanism that did not survive them}
\label{app:sixcell}

Table~\ref{tab:comparability} contrasts the two designs on the one arm that motivated the instrument. That contrast is one cell, and a single cell cannot say whether the sign reversal is a property of the evaluation design or of \texttt{coord\_median} under a pixel backdoor. We therefore ran the contrast on six cells: four that already existed in frozen artifacts and are \textbf{re-scored, never re-run}, and two that did not exist and were run for this purpose ($60$ runs, plus $8$ under Amendment~2 below).

\textbf{Both designs are the same computation.} The two ladders differ in exactly two tokens, the family name and whether \texttt{generic\_compose} receives an \texttt{adv\_mask}; data partition, participant draw, attack, local training and seeds are identical. \texttt{run\_comparability\_cells.py} refuses to start unless it first reproduces a published \texttt{results/dose\_response/} cell bit-identically ($|\text{diff}| = 0.000\mathrm{e}{+}00$), so the new cells are the same experiment as the four they are compared against, and \texttt{analyze\_comparability.py} refuses to print any pooled number unless all four published contrasts reproduce.

\textbf{What was frozen, and when.} Before any new run, we pre-registered a mechanism: the two designs disagree exactly where the downstream defense admits adversarial mass, gated on the admission-level change $\Delta\Lambda_a$ at the top rung. The four existing cells separate perfectly under that rule, which is precisely why \textbf{they are training data and cannot also be evidence for it}. The rule mechanically generated a prediction for each new cell, committed in advance: DISAGREE for \texttt{coord\_median}/scaling ($\Delta\Lambda_a{=}0.0086$, disclosed at the time as the weakest margin of any cell), AGREE for \texttt{krum}/EMNIST-byclass ($\Delta\Lambda_a{=}0.0000$). The document names in advance what would refute it and commits to reporting refutation rather than fitting a threshold.

\textbf{One process failure, disclosed because it nearly reversed this section.} The EMNIST cell was first scored with its two designs on non-identical seed sets ($n{=}5$ against a frozen $n{=}3$), and the disagreement was carried entirely by two seeds with no counterpart in the other design. We amended the pre-registration to complete the controlled ladder to the frozen seed set rather than resolve the cell on the three shared seeds, because the shared-seed subset gives AGREE and choosing it would have chosen the answer; the completed comparison is the fully paired $n{=}5$ against $n{=}5$ in the table, and it still disagrees. In the same amendment we asserted that pairing the two designs across a shared seed set was the convention the freeze had used. \textbf{It was not}, and what caught the error was not our judgement but the analyzer's own gate: under cross-design pairing the four \emph{published} cells stop reproducing (\texttt{krum}/scaling's controlled ladder moves $-0.010$ at $n{=}20$ to $-0.026$ at $n{=}5$), and the script refused to print. A third amendment withdraws the claim. The full sequence, including the amendment that would have undone the refutation, is left in the pre-registration.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}
\label{app:related}

\noindent\textbf{The contrast in one sentence.} Prior work asks whether a defense withstands an attack. We ask whether an \emph{evaluation criterion} can causally attribute the security of a composition to preservation of the downstream mechanism.

\textbf{Byzantine-robust aggregation, studied in isolation.} Krum~\citep{blanchard2017machine}, Trimmed Mean~\citep{yin2018byzantine}, RFA~\citep{pillutla2022robust}, FoolsGold~\citep{fung2020foolsgold}, FLTrust~\citep{cao2021fltrust} and FLAME~\citep{nguyen2022flame} are each proposed and evaluated as a \emph{single} stage. We propose no new defense; we reason about \emph{ordered pairs} of existing ones, which is a different object: the upstream stage's output is the downstream stage's input, so the question is no longer whether a mechanism works but whether it \emph{survives} a transform applied before it. Ensemble adversarial training~\citep{tramer2018ensemble} \emph{assumes} that upstream processing preserves downstream signals; we make that assumption explicit, checkable, and (as it turns out) \textbf{the wrong abstraction for security}.

\textbf{Adaptive attacks are why an inherited-robustness guarantee is not a security guarantee.} Optimized model poisoning defeats Krum, trimmed mean and FoolsGold once the attack is chosen \emph{against} the defense~\citep{shejwalkar2021manipulating,bhagoji2019analyzing}, and the same holds for distributed and durable backdoors~\citep{xie2019dba,wang2020attack,zhang2022neurotoxin}. Our C1 quantifies over a \emph{committed} attack by construction, so this literature bounds what any inherited-suppression criterion (ours included) can promise. Our single whitebox composition-aware stress test (§\ref{sec:fg_rfa_flagship}) is reported in order to \emph{measure} that gap ($0.051 \to 0.167$ seed-matched, $3.3\times$), not to defend the criterion against it; a screening condition stated against an adaptive adversary is what the framework most lacks.

\textbf{Evaluation methodology, which is where this paper sits.} The adjacent precedent is adversarial robustness, where reported numbers proved unreliable until evaluations were required to be adaptive~\citep{croce2020robustbench}. Our point is methodological in the same way but about \textbf{identification} rather than attack strength. Wherever a screen gates candidate pairs on standalone effectiveness and then reads the composition's outcome as evidence about the \emph{mechanism}, Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} shows that inference cannot work at \emph{any} sample size: the counterfactual is excluded by the gate, not undersampled. \textbf{We do not claim this is how the FL literature generally proceeds}, and we can name one external instance rather than a practice: \citet{fenaux2025hammer} pair a defense effective against large-deviation updates with one effective against small-deviation updates, and validate the pairing by the combined defense's attack success rate, which selects components by standalone effectiveness and then reads the composition's outcome. Composition itself is named as an open problem in the field's survey of them~\citep{kairouz2021advances}. The claim we do make is structural rather than sociological, and its clearest instance is \emph{our own} screen: Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} shows C0$\wedge$C1 \emph{force} the downstream defense to suppress the attack alone, so the gate is constitutive of an inherited-suppression screen and not an incidental filter on it. Statements of a composition-preservation intuition in that literature are informal; what is new here is the \emph{level hierarchy} at which the intuition fails (Definition~\ref{def:levels_full}) and the class of designs that can test it (§\ref{app:boundary}, §\ref{app:targeted_full}). \textbf{Where the novelty sits, as three moves.} \emph{Standard}, and textbook outside this literature: conditioning on a common descendant of treatment and outcome destroys identification~\citep{elwert2014endogenous,pearl2009causality}. \emph{New}: an inherited-suppression protocol does not merely risk that structure, it \textbf{necessarily creates} it, by the constitutive argument just given, while reading as a reasonableness filter (only study defenses that work) rather than as the step that removes identification. That is the reading we held ourselves (§\ref{sec:dose_response}). \emph{Further new}: the repairing intervention then exhibits \textbf{empirical non-implications between adjacent preservation levels}, (P3)$\not\Rightarrow$(P4) and (P4)$\not\Rightarrow$(P5), which the collider argument does not predict and which is what makes the hierarchy an empirical object rather than a taxonomy.

\textbf{Attacks and defenses.} Backdoor attacks~\citep{chen2017targeted,bagdasaryan2020backdoor,wang2020attack,xie2019dba} and robust defenses~\citep{pillutla2022robust,sun2019can,blanchard2017machine} form the landscape. Beyond our norm-based menu, certified robustness~\citep{xie2021crfl} and filtering defenses~\citep{nguyen2022flame,fung2020foolsgold} build stronger single mechanisms; we compose fixed defenses deterministically rather than strengthening a single mechanism, so these are complements to our object rather than baselines for it. Randomized transformations~\citep{xie2018mitigating,liu2018towards} in centralized settings are the conceptual neighbor of a \emph{randomized-menu} question, which this paper does not ask: every claim here concerns deterministic joint application (§\ref{sec:composability_criterion}). \textbf{An earlier version of this paper compared against a randomized menu and reported that deterministic composition dominates it; that comparison is not part of this submission and no claim here rests on it.}

\textbf{Backdoor persistence and durability.} The persistence mechanism we formalize has empirical precursors. \citet{bagdasaryan2020backdoor} design model-scaling explicitly to embed triggers that survive gradient updates; \citet{zhang2022neurotoxin} (Neurotoxin) engineer triggers to occupy ``less-used'' gradient directions, achieving multi-round persistence even under strong norm clipping. These works establish the phenomenon, and this paper \emph{uses} it rather than modelling it: persistence is why a committed attack stays committed across rounds, which is what makes a single committed attack against a fixed defense pair the right setting for an identification question. \textbf{We make no formal claim about persistence.} An earlier version of this paper advanced a $(\gamma, \alpha, p_{\mathrm{eff}})$ model of when persistence eliminates the value of defense switching, together with a heuristic boundary as $\gamma \to 1$; that material is not part of this submission, and we withdraw the claim rather than leave it asserted without the evidence that supported it. What remains is $\gamma$ as a measured descriptive quantity (Appendix~\ref{app:notation}) and the DBA persistence replication of §\ref{app:dba_f06}, which returns an \emph{intermediate} verdict and establishes no boundary.

\textbf{Defense composition and ensembling.} Composing defenses is well-studied in adversarial ML for centralized models: adversarial training combined with input purification~\citep{croce2020robustbench}, detection-then-rejection pipelines, and ensemble adversarial training~\citep{tramer2018ensemble}. These works implicitly require that upstream processing does not destroy downstream signals; our signal-preservation criterion formalizes this requirement explicitly with measurable margins. In FL the closest \emph{structural} precedent is bucketing~\citep{karimireddy2022byzantine}, which restores existing robust aggregators under heterogeneous data by inserting an upstream reshuffling step ahead of them: this is the object we study, an upstream transform feeding a fixed downstream aggregator. Its target is convergence under heterogeneity rather than suppression of a committed attack, so it does not meet Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}; what it shows is that the contrast our protocol demands is the natural one for this object, since establishing that an upstream stage helps at all requires comparing the same aggregator with and without it. In Byzantine-tolerant distributed systems, protocol composition (e.g., layered BFT) has a long history; the key distinction is that Byzantine agreement composes for fault tolerance among replicas, while our setting composes for poison resilience across client updates where the adversarial signal is a continuous gradient, not a binary fault. \textbf{A predictive criterion for FL composition does exist in recent work, and we withdraw the priority claim we previously made here.} \citet{fenaux2025hammer} categorize defenses by the deviation of adversarial updates from the mean of the updates and predict that pairing a large-deviation defense with a small-deviation one closes the gap either leaves alone. Our object is prior to theirs and different in kind: not which pairings succeed, but whether a comparison across defenses can identify \emph{why} one did. Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full} therefore applies to their criterion and to ours alike, and it constrains what validating evidence for such a criterion can establish rather than whether the criterion is correct.

\section{Limitations, in full}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{(L1) Limited scope and architecture-conditional boundaries.} Three attack families (model\_scaling, pixel, DBA), two architectures (CifarCNN, ResNet18, plus \texttt{simple\_cnn} on the replicated arm), and one dataset (CIFAR-10) for every composition suite but one. The collapse boundary is architecture-conditional (ResNet18 suppression at NC10/rep90: $0.865$ vs.\ CifarCNN $0.097$, a $10\times$ shift; per-seed values in §\ref{app:resnet18_replication_details}), so results at other $N$, $K$ or datasets may differ. Cross-distribution transfer is validated across architecture, scale ($N \in \{10,100\}$) and adversarial fraction ($f \in [0.05,0.6]$; §\ref{sec:oos_pass}(D)), and \emph{dataset} transfer is now tested for the flagship Mode-S arm alone, EMNIST-byclass with \texttt{simple\_cnn}, where the negative replicates at $\Delta = -0.017$ (§\ref{app:targeted_full}), while remaining untested for the 42-pair screen and for every other composition suite: the signal-preservation conditions are dataset-agnostic in mechanism (cosine similarity, geometric distance), yet the downstream aggregator's decision boundary may shift with output dimensionality. \emph{The $2\times2$ channel factorial is narrower still}: it is defined only for the two selectors whose decision can be transplanted (\texttt{krum}, \texttt{cos\_krum}) and the harness raises for any other $d_2$, so it is one attack on one aggregator family, and at $n{=}5$ we report it as \textbf{not decisive} (§\ref{app:factorial}) and act on it only where it withdraws a claim. \emph{Two further gaps in the intervention itself}: we ran neither DBA nor Neurotoxin through it, and the score-only control that closes the magnitude channel is a single arm, one aggregator and one attack. \emph{And scale is a prediction, not a measurement}: the \emph{pre-registered} PASS predictions hold at $N{=}100$/ResNet18 (FG$\to$RFA $0.069$) but far from converged accuracy, so they bound nothing about converged training, and \textbf{the negative was never measured at that scale}. \emph{What transfers:} the qualitative PASS/FAIL prediction and the mechanism. \emph{What does not:} the exact suppression ASR. A practitioner at $N{=}10^4$ should use the criterion to select candidates and then run pilot evaluations at their target scale: the criterion identifies \emph{what to try}, architecture-specific calibration determines \emph{how well it works}.

\textbf{(L2) The composability criterion is an evaluation-prioritization rule, not a security screen and not a discovery tool.} \textbf{It has no established predictive validity}: it orders candidates for evaluation, and the pooled model that would let it predict is unconfirmed ($R^2{=}0.239$) with its cross-arm ordering refuted (§\ref{app:targeted_full}). The criterion gives \emph{sufficient} conditions (C1$\wedge$C2, with C3 as a margin diagnostic): it reliably identifies mechanism-preserving compositions in the common case where one defense is known-effective, but it cannot certify synergistic compositions where no single defense suppresses the attack alone. The conservative bias is by design: false negatives are operationally cheap to recover from (try the composition anyway) while false positives would deploy an insecure stack. Accuracy is 16/18 $=$ 89\% on the development set, 22/24 $=$ 92\% on the pre-registered held-out set (90.5\% combined 2-way) and 9/10 $=$ 90\% on pre-registered 3-way triples (supplement \S~\ref{sec:three_way}). \textbf{None of those tallies should be read as discrimination}: only 5 of the 42 two-way pairs are low-ASR, so a constant HIGH call scores $37/42 = 88.1\%$ and the combined $90.5\%$ beats it by one pair. Precision ($2/2$) and recall ($40\%$) are the quantities that carry information, and they are why we present the criterion as a prioritizer. All clean C1$\wedge$C2$\wedge$C3 PASS predictions are correct; the four misses are either the predicted intermediate behavior of rank-based aggregators (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank_invariance}: dev-set NC$\to$cm, rfa$\to$cm; held-out fedavg$\to$cm) or one speculative LOW call the criterion does not license (held-out fg$\to$NC, a C1 failure). Formalization is partial: Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight} proves C2$\wedge$C3 for the weighting$\to$rank/geometric class with explicit margins, the remaining families are empirically validated, and C3's threshold condition depends on $d_2$'s discrimination boundary, which in practice requires calibration runs.

\textbf{(L3) Per-round aggregator switching only.} We test per-round defense selection. Randomized \emph{internals} (DP noise, subsampling smoothing à la CRFL, random participation masks) modify the embedding mechanism directly and may retain value even under persistence. Three adaptive attacks are tested against composition: projection-based (best ASR $0.597$ at $\epsilon{=}1$), Neurotoxin-style~\citep{zhang2022neurotoxin} top-$k$ gradient projection, and a two-phase consensus-shifting attack against rep$+$tm that biases updates toward the target at rate $r$ for 25 rounds before injecting full backdoor updates (App.~\ref{app:secondary}). At shift\_rate $\in \{0.01,0.05\}$, $n{=}10$ each: mean ASR $0.684 \pm 0.138$ and $0.658 \pm 0.144$, comparable to committed\_pixel ($0.679$) and \emph{below} the temporal mix ($0.915$). At $f{=}0.2$ the honest $80\%$ anchor the coordinate-wise median, limiting Phase-1 drift to ${\sim}r/(1{-}f)$ per round, so Phase 2 degenerates to standard committed\_pixel: a mechanism that weakens monotonically with $f$, and at $f \leq 0.05$ over $T \geq 200$ rounds cumulative drift may eventually overcome it. The robustness claim is therefore specific to the tested regime ($f{=}0.2$, $T{=}50$).

\textbf{(L4) No falsifiable prospective positive evidence, and C0 was added retrospectively.} This is the sharpest limitation we can state about our own design, and we state it rather than let a reader find it. Of the prospective suite's 8 predicted-LOW pairs (§\ref{sec:prospective}), 6 are $\cdot\to$FLTrust compositions that are near-\emph{identities}: FLTrust gates on a scale-invariant cosine and renormalizes every update to the server-gradient norm, discarding exactly the magnitude information an upstream positive rescaling modifies (verified bit-identical per seed for pass-through and non-clipping $d_1$). Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}(a) permits no other outcome, so these confirmations cannot falsify it; the remaining 2 have FLTrust as $d_1$, where suppression is inherited and C0 puts them out of scope. Two further caveats: C0 was formulated \emph{after} seeing this suite, so its 7 exclusions are retrospective rather than pre-registered; and the three matched contrasts that most directly probe Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance} (a $19$--$20\times$ ASR gap between $\cdot\to$FLTrust and $\cdot\to$Krum at fixed $d_1$) confound C1 with C2, because in our menu the invariant-signal $d_2$ is also the only $d_2$ effective alone. We had named the decisive experiment as a downstream defense with a rescaling-invariant admission signal that is \emph{weak in isolation}; Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} shows no such pair can be certified in scope, so that experiment does not exist: the confounding is structural, not a gap in our menu. §\ref{sec:metric_swap} runs the achievable substitute, holding the suppression \emph{principle} fixed while varying only the invariance class, and it did not deliver a positive test either: standalone effectiveness could not be held fixed, because for this defense family the magnitude information the mirrors discard is the same information the originals use to suppress a magnitude attack ($8/8$ frozen labels, TP${}=0$). So after two pre-registered attempts the criterion's positive direction still rests on in-distribution and out-of-sample PASS evidence, and the strongest C2-specific evidence in the paper is the \emph{post-hoc} within-defense contrast of §\ref{sec:metric_swap}, which holds C1 exactly fixed but was not pre-registered and is statistically resolved on only one of its two cells at $n{=}3$. §\ref{app:ladder} is the pre-registered design that answers this, and it delivers exactly one falsifiable prospective positive result (arm 2, reputation under model-scaling, $0.017 \to 0.779$ across the dose at $p = 1.7\times10^{-6}$ with clean accuracy nearly unchanged) while refuting two of its own four predictions and its pooled prediction. So the honest state of the positive direction after the ladder is: one confirmed arm, on the \emph{destruction} half of C2's mechanism, with the preservation half's designated control indeterminate (§\ref{sec:dose_outcome}). The targeted intervention of §\ref{app:targeted_full} advances this without closing it: closing the attenuation channel by construction, it isolates C2 within a single defense, substantial disturbance of the statistic does not produce a corresponding change in suppression \emph{on the adjudicating Krum arm under Mode~S}, with \emph{admission} of adversarial input the channel the evidence points to there (H-statistic refuted, H-admission confirmed on that arm, pooled model not confirmed), and it \emph{refutes} the preservation-half reading the ladder left open, since the exactly-invariant \texttt{cos\_krum} neither suppresses nor holds ASR fixed. A third pre-registered attempt (§\ref{app:targeted_full}) was designed to be the falsifiable prospective \emph{positive} test: a replication arm chosen because the statistic reading and the admission reading order its rise \emph{oppositely}, with the ordering frozen in git before the arm was run. It replicated the negative on a defense in a different invariance class and \textbf{refuted the positive ordering}: the arm with the largest measured admission change rose \emph{less} ($+0.098$) than an arm whose admission change was smaller. So what remains absent is still a validated \emph{positive} predictor: after that replication the pooled two-channel model is not confirmed over $26$ cells ($R^2 = 0.239$) and its cross-arm ordering is refuted outright, so the criterion's positive direction is unestablished as a screen, and admission is a better \emph{direction} than the statistic without being a quantitative one.

\textbf{(L5) Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}'s conditions need a pilot, and (1)'s is not operationally checkable as stated.} Neither $\rho < r^\star$ nor $\Delta'_{\mathrm{sep}} > 2(R'_B + \delta')$ is readable off a $(d_1,d_2)$ definition pair: both need a pilot measurement. \textbf{So two kinds of cheapness have to be kept apart, and the paper has previously run them together.} Prop.~\ref{prop:invariance}'s classes are \emph{algebraically} checkable: (a) and (c) are decided from the definitions of $d_1$ and $d_2$ alone, with no data, no runtime cost and no per-deployment work. Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}'s conditions are closed-form inequalities and are \emph{still} none of those things, because their terms have to be instantiated by measurement on the deployment they are being checked for. \textbf{Algebraically checkable does not imply deployment-time cheap}, and only the first of the two is something this paper delivers. Regime~A is worse than that. Its ordering-survival condition is $\rho < S$ with $S$ the attack's coordinate extremeness against the benign scale. Building the dose--response ladder (§\ref{app:ladder}) required us to \emph{measure} $S$ rather than stipulate it, and it is not a stable per-attack constant: on model-scaling it ranges $0.00$--$12.59$ across the 12 measured rounds that sampled an adversary (median $0.57$), on the pixel backdoor $0.61$--$1.63$ (median $0.92$). The $S{=}10$ of §\ref{sec:restricted_theorem} is an illustrative stipulation, not a measurement. Two consequences we state rather than work around. First, a practitioner cannot check $\rho < S$ from a single round: the quantity swings by orders of magnitude round to round, and on both committed attacks its median is below $1$, so Regime~A's condition fails even at the identity rung under this estimator. Second, we therefore built \emph{no} prediction in §\ref{app:ladder} on $S$, resting the ladder on directly measured disturbance of $d_2$'s statistic instead. The margin form of the theorem remains valid as a statement about $\rho$ and $S$; what is not established is that $S$ admits a stable estimator at the round granularity a deployment would have to check it at, and a usable Regime~A needs an $S$ estimator with characterized variance, which we do not provide. \textbf{And where the geometric condition \emph{is} measurable it stops being informative in the regime that matters most}: the margin it rests on is exactly what heterogeneity closes, RFA's all-round mean falling from $3.24$ to $1.01$ at Dirichlet $\alpha{=}0.1$, the limit at which Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass} degrades to $n_a/(n_b{+}n_a)$ and certifies nothing (§\ref{sec:restricted_theorem}, §\ref{sec:oos_pass}(E)). So under extreme heterogeneity the conditions are not merely awkward to check but empty, which is the sense in which §\ref{sec:framework} calls the bound vacuous there.

\textbf{(L6) Measured disturbance is not a transferable predictor, and our dose instrument varies the wrong quantity for three of its four arms.} The pre-registered ladder (§\ref{sec:dose_outcome}) refutes the pooled claim it was built to test ($\rho_s = +0.351$, $p = 0.091$) and refutes two of four per-arm shapes, with ASR \emph{falling} as the dose rises in three arms. Two distinct faults, both ours. First, the abscissa: at matched measured disturbance the real upstream transforms sit up to $+0.79$ ASR above the synthetic curve, and Krum's within-margin arm does not reproduce the very contrast (§\ref{sec:metric_swap}) we attributed to disturbance of its statistic, so the rate at which a transform flips a defense's decision is not a sufficient statistic for what the transform does: \emph{which} clients are reweighted matters, and $\rho$ does not measure that. We now measure the quantity that does, $\Delta_c$ (the supplement \S~\ref{app:delta_c}): the two anchors that miss are the two most extremely targeted ($-1.21$, $-0.72$ against the ladder's $-0.34$) at $\rho$ that is $3.7$ and $13\times$ smaller. That names the axis of the mismatch without supplying a replacement predictor: $\Delta_c$'s magnitude does not order suppression either. Second, the instrument: mean-1 normalization fixes the aggregate's scale but not any client's relative weight, so with the dose assigned independently of adversary status a large $\rho$ mostly dilutes whichever client carries the poison (measured: the ladder's $\Delta_c$ drifts monotonically negative with the dose, $0.000 \to -0.140 \to -0.240 \to -0.336$), and three arms end up measuring Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}'s attenuation regime rather than C2 disturbance. The adversary-targeted dose that separates the two, recorded as not run before the freeze (caveat (iii), the supplement \S~\ref{app:dose_caveats}), is run in §\ref{app:targeted_full}, and in retrospect Mode~S is the \textbf{$\Delta_c$-pinned} instrument ($\Delta_c$ exactly $0$ at every rung, $\rho$ up to $54.6$) and Mode~A the \textbf{$\Delta_c$-swept} one ($-1.14$ to $+2.47$): with every adversary's weight pinned, Krum's statistic is disturbed in up to $80\%$ of rounds while the admission of adversarial input does not move and ASR stays inside the pre-specified equivalence margin, so \emph{which} clients are reweighted, not how often a decision flips, is indeed the operative variable, and the ladder confound is \emph{explained} (reputation's rise shrinks from $+0.762$ to $+0.178$ once the adversarial share is held fixed). That isolates C2's causal content within a single defense; it does not rescue the pooled positive direction ($R^2 = 0.239$ over $26$ cells, cross-arm ordering refuted by the replication).

\section{Evidence status: what is proved, what is supported, what is not}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\label{app:conclusion}

\textbf{Proved:} the invariance classes of Proposition~\ref{prop:invariance}; sufficient conditions for mechanism preservation on the weighting$\to$aggregator class (Theorem~\ref{thm:bounded_reweight}, Lemma~\ref{lem:annihilation}, Corollary~\ref{cor:aggregate_mass}); the identification boundary for outcome-gated screening (Proposition~\ref{prop:identification_full}, with Lemma~\ref{lem:testability} its FL instance); the recall bound on inherited-suppression screens (Proposition~\ref{prop:emergent_full}); and, formalizing the interventions, statistic-level non-identifiability (Proposition~\ref{prop:nonidentifiability}) and the independent controllability of $\rho$ and $\Delta_c$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:rho_deltac}). \textbf{The two boundary results are general} (they are statements about the shape of a screen, not about FL), so what carries beyond this paper is the design they license, not the screen. \textbf{Empirically supported:} that substantial disturbance of the downstream statistic need not produce a corresponding change in suppression when the adversarial coefficient share is held fixed, with adversarial admission the channel the evidence favours, \emph{on the adjudicating Krum arm under Mode~S} and, by pre-registered replication, both on a downstream defense in a \emph{different} invariance class and on a \emph{second dataset and architecture} (§\ref{app:targeted_full}); and that the screen behaves as a conservative prioritizer across 42 pairs. \textbf{Not established:} the positive predictive direction of the criterion: the pooled two-channel model is unconfirmed over $26$ cells ($R^2{=}0.239$) and the replication refutes its cross-arm admission ordering, so admission is a direction and not a quantitative predictor; any ability to discover emergent synergy; and generalization beyond star-topology FL, or beyond CIFAR-10 for anything but the one arm replicated on EMNIST-byclass.

\subsection{Equivalence testing at the existing seed counts: where the frozen margin passes and where it only appears to}
\label{app:tost}

\textbf{Why this is here and what it is not.} The pre-registered rule scores a \emph{point} estimate
against a margin: $|\mathrm{mean}\,\mathrm{ASR}(\kappa{=}2) - \mathrm{mean}\,\mathrm{ASR}(\kappa{=}0)| <
0.15$ and every rung $< 0.5$. A point estimate inside a margin is consistent with a negligible effect
\emph{and} with an effect the sample cannot locate, and those are different claims. Table~\ref{tab:tost}
scores the same frozen margin as a two-one-sided-$t$ (TOST) equivalence test on the \emph{paired}
per-seed differences, declaring equivalence only when the $90\%$ interval lies inside $(-0.15,+0.15)$.
It is \textbf{post-hoc}, reads only frozen artifacts, \textbf{adds no runs and revises no published
verdict}; the emitter is \texttt{experiments/analyze\_tost\_existing.py}. Pairing is not a modelling
choice: every rung of an arm runs at the same seeds, so each difference holds one data partition fixed
and the seed-to-seed spread that dominates this suite drops out of the contrast.

\textbf{What it shows, including against us.} \textbf{The flagship passes}, and passes at $n{=}5$:
$p{=}0.0014$, the pairing holding $\mathrm{sd}$ to $0.042$, so \emph{the equivalence claim on the
adjudicating arm does not depend on a larger seed count}. So do the score-only control ($p{=}0.0071$)
and the EMNIST-byclass replication ($p{=}0.0004$, $n{=}3$, $\mathrm{sd}{=}0.007$). The two arms whose
suppression genuinely moves correctly \textbf{fail} it, which is the positive control working:
reputation ($+0.178$) and \texttt{cos\_krum} ($-0.425$). \textbf{And one arm passes the frozen rule
while failing the test}: \texttt{coord\_median}'s $+0.098$ sits inside $\pm0.15$, but its $90\%$
interval reaches $+0.160$ and TOST gives $p{=}0.073$. We therefore report that arm's equivalence as
\textbf{not established} rather than as inherited from the point estimate. \textbf{This is the one place
the paper's frozen scoring rule was more permissive than the evidence warrants, and we say so rather
than leave the width implicit.} \texttt{cos\_krum} additionally sits above the $0.5$ ceiling the frozen
rule conjoins with the margin ($0.584$ at the identity), which the body already states as its never
suppressing.
